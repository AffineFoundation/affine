-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. How performant is the king?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SWE-bench Pro / Claude Code (full 731 tasks, reign 8): king 64.6 %, teacher
81.4 % (paired: teacher-only 127, king-only 20).
Reign 9, 281 / 282 tasks finished (reign9_cc_swepro.txt; 28 HarnessError
runs = 10 %, counted as fails in the first line, excluded in the second):
  set A (150 random tasks, unbiased rate)  reign-9 64.7 %  teacher 77.3 %  reign-8 56.7 %
    paired vs teacher: king-only 13, teacher-only 32, sign p = 0.0066
  set A, scored runs only (n=142)          reign-9 68.3 %  teacher 78.9 %  reign-8 57.7 %
  set B (reign-8's 131 teacher-only tasks) reign-9 41.2 %  teacher 100 %   reign-8 0 %
swe_rebench_lite_300 / mini-swe (25 tasks): reign-9 14/25, teacher 15/25.
Reported Qwen3.8-27B SWE-bench Pro = 53.5 (their harness); Opus 4.6 = 61.7.

Read: the king is 10–13 points under the teacher on an unbiased panel and
gaining across reigns (reign 8 → 9 = +8 points on set A; it recovers 41 %
of the tasks reign 8 lost to the teacher). The distillation meter is
moving the right direction on a benchmark it never touches.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. What the king does right
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Same skeleton as the teacher: ~100 steps per run, 14 edits, 8–10 test
    runs, 0 % zero-test runs (reign 9). It reads, edits, tests, submits.
  - Step 0–32k tokens of context: identical-call repeat rate 1.8 % (teacher
    0.5 %), median reasoning 181 chars — close to the teacher's early game.
  - Reign 9 stopped going silent: reasoning-only replies (a <think> block
    with no visible text or tool call, the compaction killer) fell from 50 %
    of runs (reign 8, >=3 per run) to 10 %.
  - On the duel meter itself the king is in band on 56–69 % of its own
    bench steps at shallow depth (teacher 76–78 %).

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. What the king does wrong  (every number is king vs teacher on the same tasks)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3a. It thinks short, and it is getting shorter across reigns.
    median reasoning chars per step (251 paired tasks): reign-8 298,
    reign-9 242, teacher 438
    p90 per step: king 3.2k, teacher 9.1k;  p99: 13.8k vs 36.6k
    When the teacher's reference thought on the same prefix is long
    (>=1500 chars) the king writes 0.27x (reign 9) / 0.51x (reign 8) of it;
    the teacher re-sampled on its own prefixes writes 0.84x.
    Live duels, 53 verdicts / 129k answered turns: same ratio 0.50x on the
    >=1500-char ref turns, 1.02x on the <300-char ones.

3b. It loops. Identical tool call issued >=3x in a run: reign-9 73 %,
    reign-8 62 %, teacher 31 %; >=5x: 39 / 24 / 3 %.
    Solve rate when an identical call repeats >=3x: 0.56 vs 0.75 without.
    The loop is a depth effect: repeat rate by prompt depth
      king    0–32k tok 1.8 %  32–64k 3.2 %  64–100k 6.3 %  >100k 7.9 %
      teacher            0.5 %         0.8 %          1.0 %        1.2 %
    re-Reads of a file already read: king 4 → 12 % of Read steps, teacher 2 → 4 %.

3c. Context bloat follows from 3a+3b: median max prompt 113k tokens (king)
    vs 64k (teacher); 65 % of king steps sit beyond 64k tokens vs 27 % for
    the teacher. Reign 8 then died on compaction ("Prompt is too long");
    reign 9 mostly survives it but pays in steps.

3d. Where the meter sees this. depth_onpolicy_probe (bench steps re-scored
    with fresh teacher refs, k=3, tau 0.03, band 2.0/0.002):
      king − teacher mean min(R,G):  0–40k chars −0.006/−0.008 (r8/r9)
                                     120–250k     −0.024/−0.012
                                     250k+        −0.021/−0.016
    The gap is 2–3x wider at depth, and it is the G leg (grounding) that
    opens: ΔG −0.026…−0.029 at 120k+ vs −0.007…−0.010 shallow. Repeated
    steps score below fresh ones for every model. So min(R,G) already
    discriminates the failure — it is just not sampled where it happens.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Why: two caps, one incentive
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4a. D never shows the depth where the king breaks.
    datagen/slicer.py MAX_PREFIX_CHARS = 120_000 (~32k tokens) — a
    "serving-window cap" from the 65,536-token era. max_model_len went to
    131,072 on 2026-09-07; the cap did not follow.
    Effect on D: 82 % of teacher_claude_code steps, ~30 % of pi / bashtool
    steps dropped at slicing. On the bench, 92 % of king steps and 84 % of
    teacher steps happen beyond that cap. Depth bins 120–250k / 250k+ chars,
    where the gap is widest, have zero support in D. A miner can be SFT'd to
    the teacher on everything D contains and still loop at 80k tokens,
    because nothing in the objective ever looked there.

4b. The thought cap forfeits exactly the turns that need thought.
    [duel] max_thought_tokens = 1024 (+768 action) = 1792 tokens per sample.
    Since wvk 12 a sample with no parseable action scores forfeit −0.1
    (p1 of valid turn scores is −0.087). A miner that thinks as long as the
    teacher on a hard turn does not lose a little — it takes the floor.
    Evidence, live (53 min(R,G) verdicts):
      forfeited turns  median teacher-ref thought 1,766 chars
      answered turns                              358 chars
      53 % of forfeits land on turns whose ref is >=1500 chars (base 12 %)
      forfeit rate king 2.7 %, challenger 4.5 % — each 2 % ≈ one δ (0.002)
      across 73 verdicts: Spearman(margin, challenger median thought chars)
      = −0.46 (p 5e-5); Spearman(margin, challenger forfeit rate) = −0.55
      (p 4e-7). Longer-thinking challengers lose. (Confounded — weak models
      may both ramble and lose — but the forfeit rows above show the
      mechanism directly.) The live king's median thought is ~260 chars
      against a 358-char teacher-ref median.
    Evidence, teacher side: 19 % of the teacher's own Claude Code steps
    exceed 1024 thought tokens; 13 % exceed 1792. In the duel the teacher's
    3 refs fail to parse (refs<2, turn dropped) on 6.4 % of live slice turns,
    rising with depth in the probe: 4 % → 11 % → 15 % → 29 %.
    So: the meter cannot score the teacher's deep-thought turns, and it
    punishes a miner that tries them. The gradient a miner feels is
    "think less than 1024 tokens or eat −0.1" — the exact behaviour in 3a,
    monotone across reigns 8 → 9 (298 → 242 chars/step) while the bench
    rate still rose because the rest of the trajectory improved.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Decision
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Single best D update — raise the prefix cap to the serving window.
    MAX_PREFIX_CHARS 120_000 → 300_000 in affine/datagen/slicer.py (and the
    view.py guard that imports it), then one fold. Data event, no wvk
    (corpus refresh rule, AGENTS.md §5). 300k chars ≈ 78k tokens (p50) /
    90k (p10) at the 3.3–3.9 chars/token measured with the Qwen3.8
    tokenizer on 200 baked D prefixes — inside the 131,072 max_model_len
    with 1792 gen headroom; the probe scored 400k-char prefixes on the
    live swarm with 5 ContextLengthErrors in 1,461. Add a
    tokenizer-measured guard (drop > 110k tokens) at fold so the cap is
    stated in the unit that actually binds. Cost: echo prefix caching
    already amortises the long prefix across the ~10 echoes per turn; the
    fold reprocesses existing traces (nothing to regenerate — the deep steps
    are already in traces/).
    Why this over more harnesses / frontier data / language balance: those
    add breadth at depths D already covers; the measured gap is a depth
    gap. Traces already hold the data; the slicer throws it away.

Single best scoring update — raise max_thought_tokens 1024 → 4096.
    [duel] knob, contract change → wvk event under an explicit dated
    directive (not made here). Cost: mean generated thought tokens 339 → 662
    per teacher sample (1.95x on the generation half; echoes unchanged);
    teacher truncation 19.4 % → 5.5 %; 2048 would be 1.46x / 11.9 %.
    Ordering: this is the change that removes the perverse gradient in 4b;
    the D change is what lets the meter see the depth where 3b happens.
    Do both; if only one can ship, the thought cap ships first — it is the
    one that currently rewards the failure.

Not recommended, and why
  - A repeat-action penalty term: the probe shows repeated steps already
    score lower on min(R,G) (king −0.015 vs −0.012 fresh; teacher −0.001 vs
    +0.003); a new term is not needed once those depths are in D.
  - Visible-span G: decided NO 2026-09-08 (teacher's own tool-loop replies
    are prose-free 20–33 %).
  - Frontier-action modulation: null on the reign chain (v6 frontier probe).
  - Per-dialect caps alone (max_tokens_by_kind): the long-thought turns
    are bash/tool_call coding turns, not just boxed; a per-kind cap does not
    reach them.

Checks to run after shipping
  - Fold: share of D turns with prefix > 120k chars (target 25–35 %).
  - Verdicts: forfeit_rate on both sides (expect < 1 %), teacher refs<2
    share (expect < 3 %), median_len_z rising toward the teacher's ref.
  - Next reign's bench: repeat >=3x share and re-Read share by depth bin.
