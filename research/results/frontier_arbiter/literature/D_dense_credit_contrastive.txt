FRONTIER ARBITER — LITERATURE NOTES D
Dense per-turn credit from delayed outcomes / contrastive and log-ratio scorers / pairs from failures /
off-policy evaluation from logged data / verifier generalisation across the coverage gap
Read 2026-09-21 from the primary pages (arXiv abs + HTML). Numbers are the papers' own. Sources
covered by notes A/B (Math-Shepherd, OmegaPRM, AgentPRM, GiGPO, ReST-EM, STaR, SDFT/SDPO/OPSD, TML

What we are designing around (AGENTS.md §1–2; store doc dataset-rule-solutions.md §0–1, §3g, §3i,
§6.5, §6b.5, §7):
  F1  Any rule built only from teacher likelihoods of the miner's tokens has the teacher as maximiser
      (kings sit at parity). One term must be anchored to something the teacher does not define: a
      verified outcome, or information the teacher was not given — computed into D at fold time.
  F2  "Which action" is noise on ~90 % of turns (41 outcome-relevant turns per 1,000); an anchored
      term has content only at decision states, so D must be shaped dense in them or the term dilutes.
  F3  Dead on measurement: frontier opinion in any form (similarity, hinted refs on the thought leg →
      out of band; hindsight refs as action-leg context → plan-agreement, `ls -la` beats the king,
      teacher-vs-king separation 1.84 → 0.00; frontier preference judge 42 % accuracy; deliberated
      frontier consensus correct 0/18 at decision states — the consensus IS the teacher's failure).
  F4  Alive: (S1) decision-state dataset with continuation-verified S+/S− actions, ~10–12
      continuations per kept state, only where a grader can be rebuilt (≈ 40 % of turns); (S2)
      frontier proposals verified by the teacher's continuation (27 % of ceiling states); (S9) R /
      typicality / A demoted to gates. VAV (verified action value) is directionally right but starved
      at N = 3–4 per state — needs N ≈ 8–16 or pooled classes.
  F5  Gaming surface a new term must survive: filler / flat lift (centering kills it on R, band kills it
      on G), parroting a published target, splicing a Qwen thought onto a foreign action, generic
      exploration (`ls -la`, repeat-last), memorising published (x → label) pairs, incumbency
      (anything anchored to the king rewards king-likeness).
  F6  Replayability: every term must recompute bit-identically from published D + published weights
      + a verdict's stamps. A learned scorer is replayable only if its weights are pinned in the
      manifest per fold.

Notation: x prefix; z thought; y action; C = frozen teacher Qwen3.8-27B; lpC(a|b) per-byte log-lik;
C+ / C− = the same teacher fine-tuned on solved / failed trajectories of D; μ = a prover/continuation
policy; V^μ(x) = P(task solved | continue from x with μ); Q^μ(x,y) = same after taking y at x;
A^μ(x,y) = Q^μ − V^μ (advantage under the prover). Trajectory outcome o ∈ {solved, failed} exists for
all 135k trajectories; per-state continuation labels exist only where harvested (S1/S2).

The unifying identity used throughout (Bayes on the trajectory outcome, exact when C+ is the
success-conditioned teacher and every turn is sampled by C):
    log C+(y|x) − log C(y|x) = log P(o=1|x,y) − log P(o=1|x) = log Q^C(x,y) − log V^C(x)      (★)
    log C+(y|x) − log C−(y|x) = logit P(o=1|x,y) − logit P(o=1|x)                             (★★)
So the implicit PRM (§1.1), the hindsight ratio of HCA (§2.2), the value term of VAS (§5.5), Setlur's
advantage-under-a-prover (§1.3), and the store doc's VAV table (S6) are estimates of the SAME quantity:
the teacher's own continuation advantage of the miner's action. They differ in producer (learned
density ratio vs Monte-Carlo table vs learned value net), in where they have support, and in how they
fail under optimisation. Maximiser of any of them = argmax_y Q^C(x,y): the policy that always takes
the action from which the TEACHER finishes best — strictly above the teacher wherever the teacher's
sampled action is not its own best continuation (i.e. at every split state; ≈ 0 elsewhere by F2).

1.1 Yuan et al., "Free Process Rewards without Process Labels" (arXiv 2412.01981, Dec 2024)
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01981
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Train an ORM on response-level labels with the reward PARAMETRISED as a log-ratio,
    r_θ(y) = β·log π_θ(y|x)/π_ref(y|x) (either CE on correct/incorrect or DPO on pairs). The partial
    sums q_θ^t = Σ_{i≤t} β·log π_θ(y_i|y_<i)/π_ref(y_i|y_<i) are then exact step Q-values of the
    implied reward, and r^t = q^t − q^{t−1} is a per-step (per-token) reward — no step labels, no MC
    rollouts. Proposition: the ORM's optimal solution under this parametrisation equals the exponential
    of a Q-function, so step credit is a by-product of outcome training.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Target = response correctness (math final answer). Producer = ONE
    forward pass of π_θ and π_ref per response; π_θ trained on ~29k instructions × 8 responses.
(3) Optimum vs fixed teacher. The score is an advantage under π_θ's induced Q; its argmax is the
    action with highest predicted success, not the reference's habit — above π_ref where π_ref's
    habit is not its best action (★). With CE on outcome labels the term is exactly the log
    success-likelihood ratio; with DPO it is the same up to the pairing.
(4) Numbers. Trained on 33k math instructions × 8 outcome-labelled responses; on MATH best-of-N the
    implicit PRM (CE loss) beats Math-Shepherd (MC step labels) and RLHFlow PRMs while using less
    than 1/38 of Math-Shepherd's training data; further gains with weighted majority voting.
    Scaling RESPONSES per instruction helps more than scaling instructions (instructions must be
    task-relevant; response diversity matters little). Adding real step labels on top brought NO
    further gain. Dropping π_ref (scoring with log π_θ alone) did not hurt accuracy.
    Over-optimisation: not studied here (offline BoN only) — see PRIME.
(5) MAPPING. Fold-time: SFT the frozen teacher on the solved trajectories of D (all 135k have o;
    ~0 marginal data cost) → C+; pin weights in the manifest. Duel-time per turn:
        IPRM(y_A) = lpC+(y_A|x) − lpC(y_A|x)            (action only; per-byte or per-turn sum)
        IPRM_z(z_A,y_A) = lpC+(z_A,y_A|x) − lpC(z_A,y_A|x)   (thought+action)
    Dense (every turn), no continuations, replayable given pinned C+. Avoids: filler/flat lift (a
    constant lift cancels in the ratio only if C+ and C agree on filler — they will, filler is not
    predictive of success); generic exploration scores ≈ 0 (`ls -la` is equally likely under C+
    and C). Re-opens: (a) MEMORISATION — C+ is public; a miner can fit C+ directly (it is a fixed,
    published target: the rule's maximiser is "sample where C+/C is largest", which is a model a
    miner can distil). This is S7's objection (public learned scorer on the over-optimisation
    curve). (b) Ratio pathology: log-ratios are maximised on tokens RARE under C — a miner shifts
    mass to low-probability tokens that C+ happens to up-weight (spurious features of solved
    trajectories: task difficulty, harness, repo). Needs a plausibility gate (§3.3 contrastive
    decoding's α-mask = "lpC(y) within band of C's own samples" — the live A/typicality leg as a
    GATE). (c) Confounding: solved trajectories are easier tasks; C+ learns "easy-task style".
    Centering per state (★ subtracts log V^C(x) automatically) handles a per-state constant but not
    per-token style. Cost at duel: +1 echo per rollout per side (C+ forward over the miner's
    tokens) ≈ +15 % echo work; fold: one SFT of a 27B on ~70k solved trajectories per fold.

1.2 Cui et al., "PRIME: Process Reinforcement through Implicit Rewards" (arXiv 2502.01456, Feb 2025)
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01456
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Online RL where the implicit PRM of 1.1 (initialised FROM THE POLICY/SFT model, so no
    separate RM pretraining) is updated every step on the policy's fresh rollouts + outcome verifier
    labels, and its per-token log-ratio rewards are combined with the outcome reward in RLOO/GRPO-
    style advantages. Reward = r_outcome + β·Σ_t log π_φ/π_ref over tokens.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Outcome verifier (math answer, code tests) → implicit PRM update →
    dense token rewards. Producer = the PRM's own forward pass; no MC.
(3) Optimum above base. Yes — the policy is RL-trained against verifiers; the PRM is a variance-
    reduction/credit device. Its optimum is the verifier's, not a fixed model's.
(4) Numbers. Eurus-2-7B-PRIME: +15.1 % average over the SFT start on 7 reasoning benchmarks
    (AIME24 26.7 %, +20 points over SFT), 2.5× sample efficiency vs outcome-only RL, +6.9 % final
    performance over outcome-only; surpasses Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct with 10 % of its data.
    OVER-OPTIMISATION: with the PRM FROZEN (no online update) training is unstable and rewards are
    hacked — PRM accuracy on the shifting policy distribution collapses; the online update is
    reported as ESSENTIAL ("the PRM must be updated online to mitigate distribution shift and
    reward hacking"). Initialising the PRM from the SFT policy beats a separately trained PRM.
(5) MAPPING. The direct lesson for a KOTH rule: a FROZEN public implicit PRM is a hackable target;
    PRIME's remedy (online update on the optimiser's own samples) translates to (i) re-fitting C+
    every fold on D INCLUDING the king-seat and challenger rollouts (S8 DAgger states — the king's
    own failures enter D, so C+/C− learn the current exploit), and (ii) a moving target that is
    still replayable because each fold pins its C±. Failure mode that stays open: between folds the
    scorer is fixed and the fold cadence (weeks) is slower than a miner's RL loop (hours). Cost:
    fold-time SFT; no duel-time change beyond 1.1.

1.3 Setlur et al., "Rewarding Progress: Scaling Automated Process Verifiers for LLM Reasoning"
    (arXiv 2410.08146, Oct 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08146
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Define the process reward for step y at prefix x as the ADVANTAGE under a PROVER
    policy μ: A^μ(x,y) = Q^μ(x,y) − V^μ(x), estimated by MC rollouts of μ from x and from x∘y. Train a
    PAV (process advantage verifier) to regress it; use PAV as a dense reward in beam search and in
    online RL alongside the outcome reward.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Target = whether the prover μ finishes correctly; producer = MC rollouts
    of μ (a fixed, usually WEAKER or complementary model), then a regressed verifier.
(3) Optimum above the base — and above the prover. Theory (their §4): rewarding the base policy π's
    own advantages is uninformative (a strong π has ≈ 0 advantage on most steps and 0 where it cannot
    finish); a prover μ that is (a) distinct from π and (b) able to distinguish steps — "not too
    strong, not too weak" — gives informative dense signal. The maximiser of Q^μ ranks π's actions by
    what μ can do with them, which is above μ wherever μ's own sampled step is not μ's best step.
(4) Numbers. Test-time beam search with PAV: > 8 % more accurate and 1.5–5× more compute-efficient
    than an ORM at equal budget; online RL with PAV dense rewards: 5–6× sample efficiency and > 6 %
    accuracy over ORM-only RL (Gemma 2B/9B/27B; MATH). Ablation: prover = base policy itself gives
    the smallest gain; a weaker prover often beats a stronger one. Over-optimisation: dense PAV
    rewards did not degrade final accuracy in their runs (they are combined with outcome reward).
(5) MAPPING. This is the theoretical licence for the store doc's VAV (S6/§6.3) with μ = C: score the
    miner's action by the TEACHER's continuation advantage, A^C(x, class(y_A)) — exactly F4's
    surviving mechanism — and it says the anchor should be a prover DISTINCT from the scored
    policy (the teacher is distinct from every miner) and possibly WEAKER for informativeness: at
    ceiling states (V^C = 0) C as prover has zero advantage everywhere; a weaker/cheaper prover
    (Qwen3.6-35B genesis, or the king seat) gives non-zero advantages on the same states and is
    cheaper to run. Two-prover tables (C and genesis) would cover more states. Cost: MC continuations
    (the ~12 per state the doc quotes; §7 recommends N = 8 on ≥ 8 containers). Failure modes: the
    doc's own — table sparsity, action-equivalence matcher, coverage 40 %. Re-opens nothing new;
    memorisation of published (x → best class) is the accepted lesson at that state.

1.4 Zhang et al. (Qwen), "The Lessons of Developing Process Reward Models in Mathematical Reasoning"
    (arXiv 2501.07301, Jan 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07301
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Audit of PRM data synthesis: MC-estimated step labels (Math-Shepherd/OmegaPRM style)
    vs LLM-as-judge vs human; propose CONSENSUS FILTERING — keep a step label only when MC estimate
    and LLM judge agree — and evaluate on ProcessBench (locate the first erroneous step) instead of
    best-of-N.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Step correctness; producers = MC completions (8 per step) + a judge.
(3) Optimum. Not a training method; a labelling audit. Relevant claim: MC labels conflate "this step
    is correct" with "the completion model can finish from here" — a step can be correct and still
    MC-labelled bad if the completer is weak, and wrong steps get MC-labelled good if the completer
    recovers.
(4) Numbers. MC-only PRMs under-perform LLM-judge- and human-labelled PRMs on ProcessBench
    (error-step location F1) and generalise worse; consensus filtering (keep only steps where MC
    and judge agree) discards a large share of MC data yet improves both BoN and ProcessBench and is
    more data-efficient; the released Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B/72B lead open PRMs on ProcessBench. BoN
    accuracy is shown to be a misleading PRM metric (ORM-like PRMs that only judge the final answer
    do well on BoN while locating no step errors).
(5) MAPPING. Their criticism of MC labels is, for us, the DEFINITION of the wanted quantity: we do
    not want "step correctness" in the abstract, we want "the teacher finishes from here" — a
    teacher-relative value (★). So MC noise (their objection) is our signal; their real warning is
    label variance at small N (matches §6.5: N = 3–4 cannot rank). The consensus idea transfers as
    an agreement FILTER on which decision states enter the `decision` group (keep a state only where
    the MC split is stable across the sequential harvest AND a cheap detector — h_causal §3i — flags
    it), not as a scoring term. Also: publish ProcessBench-style "first-bad-turn" evaluation of any
    learned scorer on held-out split states before it ranks miners.

1.5 [discovered] Luo/Liu et al. (Tongyi), "Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Implicit Step
    Rewards" — iStar (arXiv 2509.19199, Sep 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19199
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Implicit PRM for multi-turn AGENTS: reward model π_φ initialised from the policy,
    trained by a trajectory-level DPO objective on pairs of the policy's own rollouts (solved vs
    failed on the same task); implicit step reward r_φ(a_t) = β·log π_φ(a_t|h_t)/π_θold(a_t|h_t) per
    turn; step advantages (group-normalised within a trajectory) added to episode advantages in GRPO.
    Alternating updates of policy and π_φ.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Trajectory success only; step credit falls out of the log-ratio. No MC.
(3) Optimum above base. RL against the environment; step term is credit assignment.
(4) Numbers. WebShop and VisualSokoban: beats GRPO/RLOO/PRIME-style baselines and frontier LLMs
    listed in the paper; SOTOPIA goal completion up to +15 %; faster convergence than outcome-only
    GRPO; ablation: removing the online RM update or step-level advantages each hurts.
(5) MAPPING. Same as 1.1 at TURN granularity with the pair unit we already have (same task, solved vs
    failed trajectory — 135k trajectories, multiple per task): train C+ by trajectory-DPO instead of
    SFT so that C+/C is explicitly the outcome contrast (★★ with π_ref = C). The DPO form is less
    confounded by task difficulty than SFT-on-solved (pairs are within task). Same public-target
    and rare-token gaming as 1.1; cost identical.

1.6 [discovered] Wang et al., "SPA-RL: Reinforcing LLM Agents via Stepwise Progress Attribution"
    (arXiv 2505.20732, May 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20732
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. A progress estimator (LLM encoder + MLP head) outputs a scalar per step, trained so
    that the SUM of step contributions over a trajectory regresses the final reward (return
    decomposition, RUDDER-like, no value bootstrapping); step reward = contribution + a grounding
    term (was the action valid/executable); PPO on the redistributed reward.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Final task reward only; producer = the trained estimator (one pass).
(3) Optimum above base. RL against the environment with denser credit.
(4) Numbers. WebShop / ALFWorld / VirtualHome: +2.5 % success and +1.9 % grounding accuracy on
    average over GRPO/PPO/RLOO baselines; ablations show both progress and grounding terms matter.
(5) MAPPING. A return-decomposition head trained on D's 135k outcomes gives a per-turn contribution
    c(x,z,y) for EVERY turn (dense, coverage 100 %) with no continuations. As a RULE it is S7 (public
    learned scorer, weights pinned per fold) with the RUDDER guarantee that the redistribution is
    return-equivalent only in expectation over the training distribution — off-distribution (a
    miner's novel action) the head is unconstrained. Use: as a decision-state DETECTOR (|c| large ⇒
    the turn mattered) to steer where continuations are spent — cheaper than h_causal ($0), and
    trainable now. Not as the ranking term.

2.1 Arjona-Medina et al., "RUDDER: Return Decomposition for Delayed Rewards" (arXiv 1806.07857,
    Jun 2018; NeurIPS 2019) https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07857
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Learn g(τ_{≤t}) (an LSTM) to predict the episode return from the prefix; redistribute
    reward r̃_t = g(τ_{≤t}) − g(τ_{≤t−1}) (contribution analysis). Return-equivalent MDPs share
    optimal policies; the redistribution makes expected future reward ≈ 0 at every step so credit
    is immediate.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Episode return; producer = the return predictor on logged episodes.
(3) Optimum. Preserves the environment's optimal policy (theorem); its accuracy is that of the
    return predictor on the data distribution.
(4) Numbers. Delayed-reward grid tasks: exponentially faster than TD(λ)/MC in delay length;
    Atari with delayed rewards (Bowling, Venture …): PPO+RUDDER beats PPO. No LLM numbers.
(5) MAPPING. r̃_t = g(x_{t+1}) − g(x_t) with g fitted on D's 636k turns → per-turn credit for the
    LOGGED turn. Gives a "which turns mattered in this trajectory" field for D (decision-state
    detector; also a first-wrong-turn locator on failed trajectories: the turn with the largest
    negative r̃). Cannot score a MINER's alternative action (g never saw it) — same limit as 1.6.
    Cost: one small model fit per fold; $0 at duel.

2.2 Harutyunyan et al., "Hindsight Credit Assignment" (arXiv 1912.02503, Dec 2019; NeurIPS 2019)
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02503
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Learn the HINDSIGHT distribution h(a | x, z): probability that action a was taken at
    state x given a future statistic z (a future state, or the return). Then
    Q^π(x,a) − V^π(x) = E_z[ (h(a|x,z)/π(a|x) − 1) · z ]  (return-conditional form) — credit for a is
    how much more likely a is in hindsight of the outcome than it was a priori.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Future outcome z; producer = a classifier of "which action, given the
    outcome" trained on the behaviour policy's own data.
(3) Optimum. Exact for the behaviour policy on its own support; the ratio h/π is the importance
    weight that converts outcomes into per-action credit.
(4) Numbers. Toy/delayed-effect chains and small tasks: faster/unbiased credit vs MC/TD baselines. No
    scale numbers.
(5) MAPPING. THIS IS (★): h(y | x, o=1) is what SFT-on-solved learns (C+), π is C, and the ratio
    h/π = C+/C is the hindsight credit of action y for success. HCA states the exact condition
    under which the implicit-PRM term is a true advantage: y must be in the BEHAVIOUR support (the
    teacher's own actions at x). A miner action C never takes has h estimated by extrapolation — no
    guarantee, which is precisely the rare-token gaming of 1.1(5b). Concrete: define the ranking
    term only over the class of actions with C-support (lpC(y_A|x) above a floor = A as a GATE),
    and let the ratio rank within that class. Dataset field: per turn, store lpC and lpC+ of the
    LOGGED action so that the hindsight credit of every stored turn is published (dense, 636k) —
    memorisable but only for actions already in D.

2.3 Mesnard et al., "Counterfactual Credit Assignment in Model-Free RL" (arXiv 2011.09464, Nov 2020;
    ICML 2021) https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09464
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Future-conditional value functions V(x, φ) where φ is a hindsight statistic of the
    future that is trained to be INDEPENDENT of the action taken (an adversarial/independence
    constraint); the counterfactual baseline separates luck (what the future would have been under
    any action) from skill (the action's own effect). Advantage = return − V(x, φ).
(2) Signal/target/producer. Return + a learned hindsight feature that must not encode the action.
(3) Optimum. Unbiased policy gradient with provably lower variance when independence holds; biased if
    φ leaks the action (then all credit is absorbed by the baseline).
(4) Numbers. Bandit-with-distractor tasks and "Key-to-Door": large variance reduction vs vanilla
    baselines; no LLM numbers.
(5) MAPPING. The LUCK term for us is TASK DIFFICULTY: D holds many trajectories per task, so
    V_task = teacher solve rate on that task is a hindsight-derived baseline that is independent of
    the action at any one turn. Every outcome-anchored term should be reported as
    (outcome or Q) − V_task, not raw — otherwise easy tasks mint credit for every action on them.
    Second lesson (independence): a hindsight note that quotes the action (h_answer, §3i) or a
    verifier that sees the miner's action while forming φ is biased by construction — the W1/W3
    plan-agreement failure is the CCA bias in words: the hindsight feature was not independent of
    the action, so the baseline absorbed the skill. Cost: $0 (a per-task field in D).

2.4 Yuan et al. (ByteDance), "Agent-R: Training Language Model Agents to Reflect via Iterative
    Self-Training" (arXiv 2501.11425, Jan 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11425
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. MCTS over agent tasks yields good and bad trajectories sharing a parent node; the
    ACTOR itself judges each step of the bad trajectory and names the first error it can recognise
    ("within its current capability"); splice: bad prefix up to that step + a reflection sentence +
    the good sibling branch → revision trajectory; SFT on revisions; iterate (threshold for "good"
    rises 0.5 → 0.7 → 1.0 over 3 iterations).
(2) Signal/target/producer. Environment reward on leaves (WebShop/SciWorld/TextCraft) for good/bad;
    the actor's self-critique for WHERE to splice (cheap; no MC per step).
(3) Optimum above base. The actor is trained on revisions of its own failures verified by the env;
    ceiling = the env, not a fixed model. Revision trajectories beat expert trajectories.
(4) Numbers. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, 300/200/200 MCTS seeds: +5.59 % average over the best baseline
    across the three environments; beats GPT-4-Turbo/Claude-3 on average; fewer loops; earlier
    correction with iterations. Training on revision trajectories > training on optimal trajectories.
(5) MAPPING. Locating the first wrong step WITHOUT full MC: Agent-R uses (a) the sibling structure
    (same parent node) for the ground truth of "a better branch existed" and (b) a model's opinion
    only to pick the splice point. For us (a) = split states; (b) says a cheap detector may choose
    WHERE to spend the ~12 continuations but must never define WHAT is right (F3). Dataset use:
    revision trajectories (king failure prefix → reflection → teacher-verified good branch) are
    the coached `king_coached` shape already alive; as scoring, nothing new.

3.1 Liu et al., "Tuning Language Models by Proxy" (arXiv 2401.08565, Jan 2024)
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08565
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Decode a large base M with logits z_M + α(z_E+ − z_E−) where E+ is a small TUNED model
    and E− its untuned base. The logit difference of the small pair transfers the tuning to the
    large model without touching its weights.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Whatever E+ was tuned on (chat, code, math); producer = two extra small
    forward passes per token.
(3) Optimum above base? Above the LARGE base M, yes (it gains E's tuning); at most ≈ the directly
    tuned large model. It is a steering operator; the "expert" defines the target.
(4) Numbers. Llama-2-70B base + 7B chat/base pair closes 88 % of the gap to Llama-2-70B-Chat on
    aggregate (AlpacaFarm, GSM, Toxigen 0 % toxicity, TruthfulQA — exceeds the chat model on truth);
    analysis: the shift lands mostly on reasoning/style tokens, not facts.
(5) MAPPING. As a SCORER: the per-token quantity z_E+ − z_E− summed over the miner's tokens is the
    implicit-PRM term (★★) with E± = C±. Proxy-tuning adds one idea: the pair need not be the
    27B teacher — a SMALL pair (e.g. 4B) fine-tuned on solved/failed D gives the same ratio at
    ~1/7 the echo cost, and the paper shows the ratio transfers across scale. Gaming when E± are
    public: the miner sets its own decoding to M + α(E+ − E−) — i.e. runs proxy-tuning against
    our published pair — and reaches the maximiser directly with no capability change. That is
    the definition of a distilable public target: the rule would be crowned by "teacher decoded
    with our own contrast", which is above the teacher (fine) but reachable by anyone for the cost
    of a fine-tune (the incentive collapses to a one-time race). Mitigation = PRIME's: re-fit every
    fold on data that includes the current king's rollouts.

3.2 Liu et al., "DExperts: Decoding-Time Controlled Text Generation with Experts and Anti-Experts"
    (arXiv 2105.03023, May 2021) https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03023
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. P̃(x_t) ∝ P(x_t) · (P+(x_t)/P−(x_t))^α — base LM times the ratio of an expert LM
    (fine-tuned on desired-attribute text) and an anti-expert (undesired); α controls strength.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Attribute labels (toxicity, sentiment) on training text; two small LMs.
(3) Optimum above base. Above the base on the attribute; quality (fluency) degrades with α.
(4) Numbers. Detoxification (Perspective API, max toxicity over 25 generations; GPT-2 base 0.527):
    lowest toxicity of all compared methods (PPLM, GeDi, DAPT) at comparable fluency; sentiment
    steering beats PPLM/GeDi in automatic and human evaluation; works with small (anti-)experts
    (GPT-2 small) steering GPT-3.
(5) MAPPING. Same ratio as 3.1; the anti-expert is the new ingredient: a FAILURE-tuned C− makes the
    term (★★) a logit of success rather than a log-probability ratio, which removes the per-state
    constant AND is symmetric under filler (a flat lift that both C+ and C− assign equally cancels).
    Re-opens: with public C−, a miner learns to avoid what C− likes — including legitimate but
    failure-correlated behaviour (long repro heredocs on failed trajectories, deep states). C−
    learns "the style of losing", which is largely "the style of hard tasks". Must be within-task
    paired (1.5) or task-baselined (2.3).

3.3 Li et al., "Contrastive Decoding: Open-ended Text Generation as Optimization" (arXiv 2210.15097,
    Oct 2022) https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15097
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Maximise log p_expert(y) − log p_amateur(y) subject to the PLAUSIBILITY constraint
    p_expert(y_t) ≥ α · max_v p_expert(v) (α-mask). The constraint is what stops the ratio from
    picking implausible tokens the amateur dislikes even more.
(2) Signal/target/producer. No labels; expert = large LM, amateur = small LM of the same family.
(3) Optimum. Above the expert on their quality metrics because the amateur cancels generic/
    repetitive mass; the α-mask is load-bearing (without it: degenerate).
(4) Numbers. OPT-13B vs 125M amateur, GPT-2 XL vs small: higher coherence and human preference than
    nucleus/typical decoding; no training. Follow-ups (O'Brien & Lewis 2023) show +6 points GSM8K.
(5) MAPPING. The α-mask is the exact shape the store doc arrived at from the other side (S9: A and
    typicality as pass/fail gates; §5.4 rule). Contrastive decoding tells us WHY the gate is
    necessary for any ratio term: unconstrained log-ratios reward improbable tokens. Concrete rule
    shape: turn = ratio(y_A) if lpC(y_A|x) ≥ log α + max-over-refs lpC(y_C^i|x) (miner action at
    least α-plausible relative to the teacher's own samples) else forfeit. Also the reverse
    "amateur" trick without labels: expert = C, amateur = the KING — ratio C/king rewards what the
    teacher likes and the king does not; maximiser = C decoded away from the king; anchored to the
    incumbent (F5 incumbency, inverted) — cute, but it cannot exceed C.

3.4 Mitchell et al., "An Emulator for Fine-Tuning Large Language Models using Small Language Models"
    (EFT, arXiv 2310.12962, Oct 2023) https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12962
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Fine-tuning = KL-regularised RL whose implicit reward is r(x,y) = log π_ft(y|x) −
    log π_ref(y|x); apply the small-scale reward to a large base: π(y) ∝ π_base_L(y)·(π_ft_S/π_ref_S)^β.
    "Up-scaling" (small fine-tune, large base) and "down-scaling" decouple pretraining knowledge
    from fine-tuning behaviour; β interpolates at test time.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Same as 3.1; framed as a REWARD: log-ratio = implicit reward of the
    fine-tune (the DPO identity).
(3) Optimum. Optimal policy of the implicit reward under KL to the base — above the base by
    construction of RL, capped by what the fine-tune encoded.
(4) Numbers. Llama-2/Llama/Falcon families: up-scaling recovers most of the large fine-tuned
    model's helpfulness; scaling pretraining mainly raises factuality, scaling fine-tuning mainly
    helpfulness; speculative-decoding variant ~2.5× faster sampling.
(5) MAPPING. EFT closes the loop: our (★) is the implicit reward of "fine-tuning C on solved D",
    and the KL-regularised optimum for that reward is exactly VAS's π ∝ C·exp(β·V^C) (§5.5). So a
    rule turn = lpC(y_A|x) + β·[lpC+(y_A|x) − lpC(y_A|x)] with β ∈ (0,1] is an interpolation between
    "be the teacher" (β = 0, today) and "be the success-conditioned teacher" (β = 1); β < 1 is a
    built-in plausibility regulariser (the KL term). Replayable, dense, one extra echo. Gaming as
    in 3.1 (public pair → proxy-tune to the target).

3.5 Sanchez et al., "Stay on topic with Classifier-Free Guidance" (arXiv 2306.17806, Jun 2023)
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17806
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. log p̃(y_t) = log p(y_t|∅) + γ·[log p(y_t|c) − log p(y_t|∅)]: amplify the effect of a
    conditioning c (prompt, instruction) by contrasting conditional and unconditional logits of the
    SAME model; γ > 1 over-weights prompt adherence.
(2) Signal/target/producer. No labels; the "expert" is the model conditioned on more context.
(3) Optimum. Above the model's own conditional decoding on adherence; degrades past γ ≈ 1.5–3.
(4) Numbers. GPT-2/Pythia/LLaMA: gains on Q&A, reasoning, code (HumanEval), MT; "equivalent to
    a model with twice the parameters" on some benchmarks (e.g. LLaMA-7B + CFG ≈ 13B); stacks with
    other inference methods.
(5) MAPPING. c = PRIVILEGED context p (hindsight note, verified end state) and the same teacher:
    lpC(y|x,p) − lpC(y|x) is the CFG term — and it is EXACTLY what W1/W3 measured as the informed
    action leg (§3g/§3i): it scores hint-following, `ls -la` wins, control → 0. CFG's own failure
    (over-adherence to c at high γ) is the plan-agreement failure at γ = 1. Nothing to rescue for
    the action leg; the note is a data tool. One variant not yet measured: c = the OUTCOME TOKEN
    alone ("this trajectory was solved") prepended as a one-bit privileged context — the minimal p
    that cannot leak a plan; lpC(y|x, o=1) − lpC(y|x) is a zero-training approximation of (★) if C
    can condition on the bit (it cannot without fine-tuning → this is C+ again).

4.1 Song et al., "Trial and Error: Exploration-Based Trajectory Optimization for LLM Agents" (ETO,
    arXiv 2403.02502, Mar 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02502
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. SFT on expert trajectories → explore (roll the agent on training tasks) → pair each
    FAILED own trajectory with the EXPERT success on the same task → trajectory-level DPO → iterate.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Task reward for failure; expert trajectory as the winner. Pair unit =
    whole trajectory of the same task.
(3) Optimum above base. Above SFT-on-expert (learns from own failures); bounded by the expert
    winners' quality (they are the positive side of every pair).
(4) Numbers. WebShop / ScienceWorld / ALFWorld, Llama-2-7B/13B: +8 – 9.5 % average reward over
    SFT; +20 % on ScienceWorld-Unseen (OOD); further iterations add ~1–2 %; using self-generated
    successes as winners also works.
(5) MAPPING. Pair unit we can build at $0: D has multiple rollouts per task (teacher, king seat,
    coached) → (solved, failed) trajectory pairs per task without any same-state requirement. That
    is the training set for C± by trajectory-DPO (1.5) — within-task pairing removes the difficulty
    confound of SFT-on-solved. As a SCORER, trajectory pairs are too coarse (credit spreads over
    all turns; F2 says 90 % of them are noise). Cost: fold-time DPO only.

4.2 Lai et al., "Step-DPO: Step-wise Preference Optimization for Long-chain Reasoning" (arXiv
    2406.18629, Jun 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18629
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. For a wrong solution, locate the FIRST erroneous step (GPT-4 judges against the
    ground-truth answer/solution), keep the correct prefix, sample corrections from the policy
    until one reaches the right answer; DPO on (prefix, correct step, wrong step).
(2) Signal/target/producer. Final-answer correctness + a judge for the first error; policy-sampled
    positives ("in-distribution" winners beat GPT-4-written ones).
(3) Optimum above base. Above SFT; the positive step must be reachable by the policy and verified by
    the final answer — so the ceiling is the policy's best-of-many, verified.
(4) Numbers. 10k pairs: +~3 % MATH for > 70B models; Qwen2-72B-Instruct-Step-DPO 70.8 % MATH /
    94.0 % GSM8K, above GPT-4-1106/Claude-3-Opus at the time; self-generated data > human/GPT-4
    corrections.
(5) MAPPING. "First wrong step" via a judge is F3's dead route in our domain (the frontier judge is
    at chance at decision states); what transfers is the PAIR SHAPE: same prefix, verified-good vs
    failed step — our split states, with the winner verified by continuation rather than by a
    judge. Step-DPO's observation that in-policy positives beat foreign positives matches §3f/§3g:
    a foreign (frontier) action as the positive re-targets to the foreign style; the positive at a
    split state should be a TEACHER action that solved (S1), with frontier proposals only where the
    teacher has none (S2).

4.3 Xie et al., "Monte Carlo Tree Search Boosts Reasoning via Iterative Preference Learning"
    (MCTS-DPO, arXiv 2405.00451, May 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00451
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. MCTS over reasoning steps with value = outcome validation (final answer) blended
    with the model's own step self-evaluation; per-node Q-values yield step-level preference pairs
    (high-Q vs low-Q sibling under the same parent); DPO; iterate on-policy.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Outcome check + self-evaluation; MC via tree rollouts.
(3) Optimum above base. Above SFT; bounded by what the tree finds and the outcome checker.
(4) Numbers. Mistral-7B SFT baseline: GSM8K → 81.8 % (+5.9), MATH → 34.7 % (+5.8), ARC-C → 76.4 %
    (+15.8); on-policy iteration beats offline pair collection; step-level > instance-level pairs.
(5) MAPPING. The sibling-under-same-parent Q-contrast IS the VAV table with N from tree visits;
    their finding "step-level pairs > instance-level" is F2 in their words. Their blend of outcome
    with self-evaluation would, for us, import a model opinion into the value — F3 says no; keep
    the value pure MC (teacher continuation).

4.4 Pang et al., "Iterative Reasoning Preference Optimization" (IRPO, arXiv 2404.19733, Apr 2024)
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19733
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Sample many CoTs per problem, label by final answer, build (correct, incorrect) pairs,
    DPO + an NLL term on the winner; iterate with the new model as sampler.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Final-answer correctness; policy's own samples.
(3) Optimum above base. Self-improvement bounded by pass@k of the current sampler (like STaR/ReST-EM
    in notes A, plus negatives).
(4) Numbers. Llama-2-70B-Chat: GSM8K 55.6 → 81.6 (88.7 with maj@32), MATH 12.5 → 20.8, ARC-C 77.8 →
    86.7; the NLL term matters (DPO alone plateaus).
(5) MAPPING. Instance-level pairs — coarse for scoring (F2). Its lesson for C+ training: DPO on
    outcome pairs WITHOUT an NLL anchor drifts (the winner's likelihood can fall while the margin
    grows) — a C+ trained by pure DPO may not be a proper "success-conditioned teacher" density, so
    (★) would be off; add the NLL term (or use SFT-on-solved) so lpC+ stays a calibrated density.

4.5 Hwang et al., "Self-Explore: Enhancing Mathematical Reasoning in Language Models with
    Fine-grained Rewards" (arXiv 2404.10346, Apr 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10346
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. For each wrong rationale, walk step by step; from each step's prefix sample k = 4
    completions; the FIRST step from which NO completion reaches the correct answer is the "first
    pit"; pair (correct rationale) vs (prefix + pit step) at step granularity; DPO.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Final-answer check; MC completions with tiny k — the pit is a
    small-N MC estimate of "V(prefix) = 0".
(3) Optimum above base. Above outcome-only DPO; bounded by the policy's own successes.
(4) Numbers. +11.57 GSM8K / +2.89 MATH over SFT (Mistral-7B, DeepSeek-Math-7B, Llemma); step-level
    pairs > outcome-level pairs; RAISING k (more exploration) did not help — k = 4 suffices to find
    the pit because it only needs V ≈ 0, not a ranking.
(5) MAPPING. How to locate the first wrong turn CHEAPLY: sequential small-N continuations from
    successive prefixes of a failed trajectory, stop at the first state where 0/k succeed after a
    state where ≥ 1/k succeeded — the transition IS the split state and the pit's action is the
    failed first action. This is the store doc's "sequential N ≤ 4, stop at first split" harvest
    (§5.3 S1) independently rediscovered; Self-Explore's data point (k = 4 is enough to LOCATE, and
    more k does not help location) separates two budgets: ~4 per prefix to FIND the state, then
    N ≈ 8–16 at that state only to RANK actions (§6.5). Coverage stays 40 % (grader needed).

5.1 Gonuguntla (CMU), "The Replay Gap: Static Evaluation of Model Switching in LLM Agents Scores
    the Wrong World" (arXiv, 2026; Efficient Reasoning Workshop @ COLM 2026) — arXiv title search
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism (position + measurement). Replaying logged agent trajectories to score a different
    model at each step evaluates the new model on states the OLD model produced ("the wrong
    world"); with text actions the overlap needed by importance sampling is near zero and doubly
    robust estimators inherit the value model's extrapolation error.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Logged trajectories + outcomes; estimators IS/WIS/DR.
(3) Optimum. n/a. Message: static replay ranks routers/models incorrectly when the switched model
    would have changed the trajectory.
(4) Numbers. Reported rank inversions between replay and live evaluation on their agent suites;
    IS weights degenerate (effective sample size → 1) with free-text actions.
(5) MAPPING. Our duel IS static replay by design (miner scored on teacher/king-produced prefixes),
    and it is acceptable for a distillation meter because the score is defined ON those states. The
    warning applies to any V(x, y_miner) estimate: if the value model has never seen y_miner at x,
    its number is extrapolation. Hence the store doc's action-CLASS table (only actions with logged
    continuations get a value; unmatched → 0) is the honest OPE here; S8 (harvest the king's and
    challengers' own states into D) is the paper's remedy (put the right world into the log).

5.2 Liu, Xiong, Zhang, Tang, "Autoregressive Diffusion World Models for Off-Policy Evaluation of LLM
    Agents" (ADWM, arXiv 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/ (id not captured; title search)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Learn a latent diffusion world model of environment responses from logged
    trajectories; evaluate a NEW policy by sampling its actions and diffusing the environment's
    replies autoregressively; average simulated returns.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Logged transitions; producer = the world model (training cost) + policy
    samples (no real environment).
(3) Optimum. n/a; estimator quality only.
(4) Numbers. Across multi-turn agent tasks ADWM is the only estimator with strictly positive
    Spearman between estimated and true policy values in every configuration (mean ρ ≈ +0.82);
    classical DM / IS / WIS / FQE / DR are near zero or NEGATIVE in several configurations
    (long horizon + unbounded action space).
(5) MAPPING. A learned world model of the container (observations given actions) would let us score
    ANY miner action at ANY state without a grader — Code World Model territory (notes B). Not
    replayable in our sense (a generative simulator's stochastic outcome is not bit-reproducible
    unless seeded and pinned) and a public simulator is a gameable target (the miner learns what
    the simulator rewards, not what the container does). Keep as the long-horizon coverage route;
    its own evidence says every classical OPE estimator fails in our regime, which retires "DR-OPE
    from logged transitions" as a rule.

5.3 Zhou et al., "ArCHer: Training Language Model Agents via Hierarchical Multi-Turn RL" (arXiv
    2402.19446, Feb 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19446
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Two-level actor-critic: an utterance-level critic Q(x, y_turn) trained OFF-POLICY by
    TD on turn transitions (from a replay buffer), and a token-level policy improved by the critic's
    advantage; works from offline data or online.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Environment reward at episode end; TD bootstrapping over turns.
(3) Optimum above base. RL against the environment; the critic is an off-policy value estimate.
(4) Numbers. Twenty Questions / Guess-my-City / WebShop / Maze: ~100× sample efficiency over
    single-turn-adapted PPO/ILQL baselines; can start from offline data then go online.
(5) MAPPING. ArCHer's critic is exactly "V(x, y_miner) from D's logged transitions" (fitted Q
    evaluation over turns). It would give a dense value on 100 % of turns from the 636k transitions
    and outcomes. Same objection as 5.1/1.6: off-support actions are extrapolations, and a public
    critic is S7's over-optimisation target. Use only inside a gate (α-mask) and re-fit per fold;
    cheaper than continuations, less trustworthy where it matters most (novel good actions).

5.4 Hong et al., "Q-SFT: Q-Learning for Language Models via Supervised Fine-Tuning" (arXiv
    2411.05193, Nov 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05193
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Represent Q-values as token probabilities: a weighted SFT loss whose targets are
    bootstrapped from the next state's max token-probability, with a conservative term keeping the
    learned probabilities ≥ the behaviour policy's on logged actions; no separate value head.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Sparse task reward on logged data; offline.
(3) Optimum above base. Offline RL optimum (bounded by data support, conservative by design).
(4) Numbers. Chess / Wordle / 20Q / ALFWorld / WebShop: matches or beats ILQL, CQL-style baselines
    and SFT; benefits more from stronger pretrained initialisation than value-head methods.
(5) MAPPING. Q-SFT's parametrisation means "fine-tune C on D with outcome-weighted targets" yields a
    model whose token probabilities ARE Q-values — a principled version of C+ that is conservative
    on off-support actions (probability never pushed above the behaviour's for unseen actions).
    This directly addresses the rare-token gaming of 1.1(5b): a Q-SFT-trained C+ cannot assign
    high value to actions absent from D. Replayable (pinned weights); one extra echo. Public target
    memorisation remains.

5.5 Han et al., "Value Augmented Sampling for Language Model Alignment and Personalization" (VAS,
    arXiv 2405.06639, May 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06639
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. KL-regularised RL closed form: π*(x_t|s) ∝ π_0(x_t|s)·exp(β·Q^{π_0}(s,x_t)); since
    transitions are deterministic Q^{π_0}(s,x) = V^{π_0}(s∘x); learn V^{π_0} by TD(λ) on samples of
    the FROZEN π_0 scored by the reward; decode by re-weighting π_0's top-k tokens by exp(β·V).
    No policy weights touched.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Reward model on complete responses; value net = separate LM trained on
    π_0's own rollouts.
(3) Optimum above base. It IS the KL-regularised optimum for the reward, above π_0 by construction;
    keeping π_0's top-k support limits reward hacking.
(4) Numbers. TL;DR summarisation: better KL–reward frontier than PPO and FUDGE; GPT-4 win rate ~60 %
    head-to-head, ≈ Best-of-128 quality at a fraction of the cost; Anthropic HH: 55 % win over DPO,
    ≈ Best-of-128; where PPO reaches higher raw reward (Conciseness) GPT-4 judges it worse — VAS
    hacks less because π_0's support is preserved.
(5) MAPPING. The cleanest statement of the target rule: the maximiser we want is
        π* ∝ C(y|x) · exp(β·V^C(x∘y))
    "the teacher, re-weighted by where the teacher itself finishes best". Ranking term for a miner
    action: lpC(y_A|x) + β·V̂^C(x∘y_A). V̂^C is the thing to produce — by MC table (S6, exact on
    support, 40 % coverage, costly), by learned TD value (5.3/5.4, dense, extrapolates), or by the
    density-ratio identity (★) (C+, dense, extrapolates differently). VAS's top-k support rule is,
    once more, the plausibility gate. Cost as in the producer chosen.

5.6 Li et al., "Off-Policy Evaluation from Logged Human Feedback" (arXiv, Jun 2024; title search);
    Liu et al., "Don't throw away your value model! … Value-Guided MCTS decoding" (PPO-MCTS, arXiv
    2309.15028, Sep 2023) https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15028
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Li et al.: IPS over SETS of ranked responses (instead of lists) and DR variants to
    evaluate a new response-ranking policy from logged human preferences; variance reduction by
    set-level propensities. PPO-MCTS: tree search at decode time guided by the PPO value net.
(2)–(4) Bandit-scale gains in estimator variance (Li); PPO-MCTS improves preference win-rates over
    plain PPO sampling on sentiment/toxicity/commonsense tasks by using the value net as a
    look-ahead. Neither has agent-trajectory numbers.
(5) MAPPING. IPS/DR over responses needs propensities for the MINER's action under the logging
    policy — lpC(y_A|x) is available (the teacher logged most of D), so an IPS-style estimate
    V̂(y_A) = Σ_logged 1[class(y)=class(y_A)]·o / Σ 1[class(y)=class(y_A)] IS the class table; the
    DR correction would be the learned V. Nothing beyond 5.5 + the table.

6.1 Sun et al., "Easy-to-Hard Generalization: Scalable Alignment Beyond Human Supervision" (arXiv

(1) Mechanism. Train evaluators (ORM, PRM, and OPRM = both) ONLY on MATH levels 1–3 (PRM800K human

(2) Signal/target/producer. Human step labels / MC labels on easy problems; verifier scores on hard.
(3) Optimum above base. The generator trained by RL against the easy-trained PRM exceeds SFT and
    final-answer RL trained on ALL levels — the evaluator supervises beyond its training range.
(4) Numbers. Llemma-7B: SFT 19.8 → PPO with easy PRM 27.4 (hard subset 12.2 → 14.1); Llemma-34B

(5) MAPPING. The coverage-gap plan: fit a verifier on the 40 % of turns that have continuation
    tables (where a grader exists) and apply it to the 60 % that do not. E2H says transfer of
    EVALUATION across difficulty is real but that argmax-selection against the transferred
    evaluator over-optimises — and a duel is argmax-selection (miners optimise the score). So a
    transferred verifier can be a GATE or a tie-breaker weighted by its own confidence, not the
    sole ranking term on uncovered turns. Test before use: train on mini-swe/bash resumable
    states, measure AUC on held-out resumable states of OTHER harnesses (the real gap is harness,
    not difficulty).

6.2 Zhang et al., "Generative Verifiers: Reward Modeling as Next-Token Prediction" (GenRM, arXiv
    2408.15240, Aug 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15240
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Train the verifier with next-token prediction to output "Yes/No" (GenRM) or a CoT
    rationale then Yes/No (GenRM-CoT), jointly with solution generation; score = P("Yes"); majority
    vote over sampled rationales adds test-time compute. Rationales for training synthesised by a

(2) Signal/target/producer. Correctness labels (final answer) + synthetic rationales; the verifier
    is the same family as the generator (Gemma 2B/9B).
(3) Optimum above base. Verifier-side only; as a BoN selector it exceeds the generator's own
    majority vote.
(4) Numbers. BoN: algorithmic tasks 5 → 45.3 %, GSM8K 73 → 93.4 %; easy-to-hard (train GSM8K, test
    MATH500) 28 → 44.6 %, MMLU abstract algebra 37.9 → 53.5 %; 6.4× better sample efficiency than a
    discriminative RM in the easy-to-hard setting; beats DPO-verifier and LLM-as-judge; scales with

(5) MAPPING. A GenRM fine-tuned from C on the S1/S2 tables (input: x, y; output: Yes/No = "teacher
    finishes from here") would be the verifier for the coverage gap and the equivalence judge in
    one model, replayable at T = 0 with pinned weights. F3's failure (frontier judge 42 %,

6.3 Wang et al. (Meta), "Self-Taught Evaluators" (arXiv 2408.02666, Aug 2024)
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02666
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. No human labels: for a prompt, generate a good response and a deliberately worse
    one (via a modified/"similar but different" instruction), sample judgments from the current
    model, keep those that pick the known-better response, fine-tune the judge on its own correct
    judgments; iterate.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Synthetic preference pairs whose label is known by construction.
(3) Optimum. A judge as good as the label-construction trick allows; no external ceiling beyond the
    seed model's generation.
(4) Numbers. Llama-3-70B-Instruct judge: RewardBench 75.4 → 88.3 (88.7 with majority vote), matching
    GPT-4 judges and top reward models trained on human labels; gains over 2–3 iterations.
(5) MAPPING. Label-by-construction pairs for the coverage gap: at a state x we can construct a KNOWN-
    worse action (repeat-last, `ls -la`, an action from a different task, a syntactically broken
    command) to teach a verifier what "generic/invalid" looks like without continuations — this is
    the cheap NEGATIVE side of every pair (F5's generic-exploration surface) and it exists on 100 %
    of turns. The POSITIVE side still needs an outcome; but a verifier trained on (verified-good vs
    constructed-bad) is at least immune to the `ls -la` failure that broke every informed-reference
    probe. Cost: fold-time only.

6.4 Wang et al., "Critique Fine-Tuning: Learning to Critique is More Effective than Learning to
    Imitate" (CFT, arXiv 2501.17703, Jan 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17703
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Instead of SFT on responses, train the model to generate a CRITIQUE of a noisy
    response: maximise P(c | x, y) with critiques written by GPT-4o; 50k examples.
(2) Signal/target/producer. Teacher-written critiques of noisy student answers.
(3) Optimum above base. Above SFT-on-the-same-data for reasoning; ceiling is the critique teacher.
(4) Numbers. Qwen2.5-Math-7B-CFT: +4–10 absolute over SFT variants on six math benchmarks; 1 h on
    8×H100 for 50k examples; matches SimpleRL (trained with 140× more compute); robust to noisy
    response sources and to the critique model.
(5) MAPPING. Only indirectly: critiques are opinions (F3) unless anchored to an outcome. A CFT-style
    verifier that must output "this action's continuation solved / failed" plus a rationale, trained
    on the S1/S2 tables, is GenRM-CoT (6.2). Nothing more.

======================================================================================================
CROSS-CUTTING READINGS FOR THE RULE
======================================================================================================
C1  One quantity, five producers. Every dense scorer in this list estimates Q^μ(x,y) − V^μ(x) for a
    prover μ (★). Producers: (P1) MC table from continuations — exact on support, coverage 40 %,
    ~12 continuations/state to find + N ≈ 8–16 to rank; (P2) success-conditioned teacher C+ (SFT or
    DPO or Q-SFT on D's outcomes) — dense, one echo, extrapolates off-support, public target;
    (P3) TD value/critic (ArCHer/VAS) — dense, extrapolates, public target; (P4) return-decomposition
    head (RUDDER/SPA-RL) — dense on LOGGED turns only, cannot score novel actions; (P5) trained
    generative verifier (GenRM) — dense, one generation, over-optimisable, but trained on in-domain
    labels unlike the prompted judges that failed.
C2  Support is the whole game. HCA's condition (y in the behaviour support), CD's α-mask, VAS's top-k,
    Q-SFT's conservatism, and the store doc's "unmatched class → 0" are the same rule: rank only
    among actions the teacher itself could produce; outside, forfeit or 0. This is what kills
    rare-token ratio gaming and generic-exploration wins at once, and it is already the shape of S9
    (A / typicality as gates).
C3  Public learned scorers are hackable between folds (PRIME: frozen implicit PRM collapses; E2H:
    argmax over a transferred evaluator over-optimises; Albedo). Remedies in the literature are all
    "update on the optimiser's own samples" — for us: re-fit C± / verifier every fold on D that
    includes the king seat's and queued challengers' rollouts (S8), pin weights per fold for replay,
    and keep a non-learned anchor (P1) as the term that decides at the states that matter.
C4  Task-level luck must be baselined (CCA): every outcome-anchored number is reported relative to
    the task's teacher solve rate in D; SFT-on-solved without this learns "easy-task style".
C5  Locate cheaply, rank expensively (Self-Explore, Agent-R): k = 4 sequential continuations find the
    split state; detectors (h_causal 37 % flag rate, RUDDER/SPA-RL heads, a small C+/C ratio) may
    choose WHERE to spend; only the environment decides WHAT is right (F3).
C6  Coverage gap: transfer of a TRAINED verifier across difficulty is real (E2H, GenRM 6.4× sample
    efficiency), but no paper shows transfer across HARNESS/grader availability, and every result
    is under argmax over the generator's own samples, not adversarial miners. Treat the 60 % as
    gate-only until a held-out-harness AUC is measured.

======================================================================================================
CANDIDATE MECHANISMS (≤ 6 rule sketches)
======================================================================================================
Common frame for all six: on turns of the `decision` group (F2 shaping), R / typicality / A are
PASS-FAIL gates (S9; C2), plus length floor, B licence, think-close, and the plausibility gate
    PLAUS(y_A) := lpC(y_A|x) ≥ log α + max_i lpC(y_C^i|x)   (α-mask over fresh blind teacher refs)
so that a ranking term is read only inside the teacher's own support. Non-decision turns keep the
live rule. Every term below is centred by the task baseline V_task (teacher solve rate on the task in
D, C4). "Cost/duel" is on top of today's ~27 min; "cost/fold" is the extra build.

M1  IPRM — implicit process reward from the success-conditioned teacher (P2; §1.1, §2.2, §3.4, §5.4)
    term   = lpC+(y_A|x) − lpC(y_A|x), C+ = C fine-tuned each fold by Q-SFT (conservative) or
             SFT+NLL-anchored DPO on D's solved-vs-failed trajectories within task; weights pinned in
             the manifest; per-byte over the action body (fence bytes excluded).
    maximiser: argmax_y Q^C(x,y) within C's support ≈ "the teacher decoded toward its own successes"
             (VAS π* with β = 1) — above the teacher at every split state, ≈ teacher elsewhere.
    gaming:  public C+ is a distillable target (proxy-tune to C+/C; one-time race, then parity); rare-
             token drift (bounded by PLAUS + Q-SFT conservatism); easy-task style (bounded by within-
             task pairs + V_task); memorising D's logged (x, y) pairs where C+ is high — those are the
             intended lesson.
    cost:    duel +1 echo per rollout per side (≈ +15 % echo); fold: one 27B fine-tune (or a 4B pair
             per §3.1 at ~1/7 duel cost) — no continuations, coverage 100 % of turns.
    killing experiment ($ ≈ 20, 1 GPU-day): fit C+ once on today's D; on the 18 + 30 harvested states
             with continuation ground truth (§6.5/§6b.5) compute the term for every labelled action;
             if AUC(solved vs failed first actions) ≤ 0.6, or if `ls -la` / repeat-last score above the
             median labelled action under PLAUS, M1 is dead as a ranking term (demote to detector).

M2  VAV-N — verified action value table at rankable N (P1; §1.3, §4.3, §4.5, §5.6; store §6.3)
    term   = solve rate of class(y_A) in the state's continuation table − V_task; unmatched → 0
             (equivalence matcher on action BODIES: exact → Jaccard ≥ 0.5 → stored judge verdict).
    maximiser: the action from which the TEACHER's continuation solves most often = Setlur's A^μ with
             μ = C; strictly above the teacher at split states; the frontier enters only as verified
             proposals (S2) at ceiling states. Add a second, weaker prover (genesis) for non-zero
             advantages at teacher-ceiling states.
    gaming:  memorising the published best class per state (accepted: it is the right action at that
             state; fresh-ref typicality + a re-verification queue for novel miner actions); matcher
             errors (~25 % surface) — judge verdicts stored and replayed; sparsity if N is small.
    cost:    duel ≈ $0.43 (judge) + 0 echoes; fold: locate with k = 4 sequential continuations, rank
             with N = 8–16 at kept states (~$150 + ~2.5k container-hours per 300 states); coverage
             ≈ 40 % of turns (grader must exist).
    killing experiment (the §7 spend, ~$150): re-run §6.5 at N = 8 on ~300 failed-origin depth ≥ 10
             states; if held-out teacher vs king separation is not z ≥ 3 with `ls -la`/repeat-last at
             ≤ 0, the anchor is too noisy at any affordable N and M2 falls back to a gate.

M3  CONTRAST — signed expert/anti-expert logit of success (P2 with C−; §3.2, §3.3, §1.5)
    term   = lpC+(y_A|x) − lpC−(y_A|x), C± fine-tuned on within-task solved / failed trajectories
             (DExperts form of ★★); optionally scored on (z_A, y_A) jointly so the thought is inside
             the contrast (filler lifts both sides equally → cancels).
    maximiser: argmax of logit P(success | x, y) under the teacher's continuation — same top as M1,
             sharper where C+ ≈ C (both trained), symmetric under flat lift.
    gaming:  everything in M1 plus "avoid what C− likes" — C− learns the style of hard tasks and deep
             states (long heredocs, retries), so a miner is paid for looking like an easy run; needs
             V_task and within-task pairs; public C− also tells miners exactly which failure surface
             is penalised (fine — that is the lesson) and which is not (the gap they will use).
    cost:    duel +2 echoes per rollout per side (C+ and C−) — or one 4B pair; fold: two fine-tunes.
    killing experiment ($ ≈ 25): same 48 ground-truth states as M1; additionally score the same
             action bodies transplanted to a solved-origin prefix of the same task — if the term moves
             more with the PREFIX than with the ACTION (prefix-swap variance > action-swap variance),
             C± are scoring trajectory difficulty, not the decision.

M4  KL-OPT — teacher re-weighted by its own value (P3 or P1 inside the VAS closed form; §5.5, §3.4)
    term   = lpC(y_A|x) + β·V̂^C(x ∘ y_A), β ∈ (0, 1]; V̂^C from the table where it exists (M2) and
             from a per-fold TD critic (ArCHer-style, initialised from C, trained on D's 636k turn
             transitions + outcomes) elsewhere; β = contract knob, β = 0 replays today's A leg.
    maximiser: π* ∝ C · exp(β V^C), the KL-regularised optimum of "teacher finishes" — provably above
             the teacher for any β > 0 with non-constant V^C; the KL term is the built-in plausibility
             regulariser.
    gaming:  the critic is a public learned scorer (S7) — over-optimisation between folds (PRIME),
             extrapolation on novel actions (Replay Gap; ADWM shows FQE/DR fail in this regime), so
             the critic must be capped: |β V̂| ≤ the table's range, and forfeits outside PLAUS.
    cost:    duel +1 value forward per rollout per side; fold: one critic fit (cheap) + the M2 table.
    killing experiment ($ ≈ 30): fit the critic on D minus the harvested states; on the 48 harvested
             states compare V̂^C(x∘y) against the continuation solve rates — if Spearman < 0.5 across
             actions WITHIN a state (not across states, where task difficulty carries it), the
             critic adds nothing over the table and M4 collapses to M2.

M5  HINDSIGHT-CREDIT FIELDS — dense per-turn credit on LOGGED turns as a D field, not a scorer
    (P4/HCA; §2.1, §2.2, §1.6, §3i)
    field  = for every stored turn: c_t = g(x_{t+1}) − g(x_t) from a return-decomposition head fitted
             per fold on D (RUDDER/SPA-RL), plus lpC+(y_t|x_t) − lpC(y_t|x_t) of the LOGGED action
             (HCA ratio). Use: (i) `decision` shaping — sample slice strata by |c_t| so the slice is
             dense in turns that mattered (F2); (ii) locate the first wrong turn of failed
             trajectories (largest negative c_t) → where the k = 4 continuation locator starts
             (C5) — cuts the ~12 continuations/state toward ~6; (iii) R-leg REFERENCE selection:
             at a decision turn, restrict the k teacher refs to actions of the state's high-credit
             class (S6's "teacher-with-search" R+).
    maximiser: unchanged for (i)–(ii) (data shaping); for (iii) the thought that predicts the
             teacher's SUCCESSFUL action — above the blind teacher's thought where its refs split.
    gaming:  none new for (i)–(ii); (iii) publishes which class is credited (memorisable, accepted).
    cost:    fold-only; duel $0.
    killing experiment ($ ≈ 5): on the failed trajectories that already have a continuation-located
             split state (§5.3 harvest), check whether argmin_t c_t lands within ±2 turns of it on
             ≥ 60 % — if not, the head does not locate and only (i) survives.

M6  GATE-VERIFIER — a trained generative verifier for the 60 % uncovered turns, gate-only
    (P5; §6.1, §6.2, §6.3)
    term   = none; GV(x, y_A) ∈ {pass, fail} from a GenRM fine-tuned from C on (x, y, label) where
             label = continuation-verified (S1/S2) for positives and constructed known-bad actions
             (repeat-last, `ls -la`, off-task command, broken syntax; §6.3) for negatives; T = 0,
             pinned weights, replayable; fail → forfeit_turn_score on that turn.
    maximiser: unchanged (the ranking term comes from M1–M4); the gate removes the generic-
             exploration and splice surfaces on turns with no table.
    gaming:  a public verifier is over-optimisable, but as a GATE the payoff of beating it is only
             "not forfeit", not rank — the E2H/PRIME argmax failure does not apply.
    cost:    duel +1 short generation per rollout per side; fold: one fine-tune once ≥ ~1k labelled
             states exist.
    killing experiment ($ ≈ 15): train on harvested mini-swe/bash states, test on held-out states of
             a DIFFERENT harness (Terminus / pi / tool_call) with continuation labels; if AUC ≤ 0.65
             or if it passes > 20 % of constructed-bad actions, the coverage gap stays uncovered and
             the 60 % remain on the live rule.

Ordering by evidence and by what F3–F4 allow: M2 (the only anchor that survived measurement; needs
the N = 8 spend) → M1 as the dense companion (cheapest to falsify: one fine-tune, 48 states already
labelled) → M5 fields to cut harvest cost → M4 only if M1's critic-free ratio fails but a TD critic
ranks within states → M3 only if M1 is prefix-confounded → M6 last, once labels exist at scale.
Nothing here re-admits frontier opinion into the score; the frontier stays a proposal generator whose
proposals enter M2's table only after the teacher's continuation verifies them.
