FRONTIER ARBITER — LITERATURE NOTES A
Distillation / privileged-information self-teachers / process reward models / expert iteration
Question asked of every source: can this mechanism define a TURN SCORE or a DATA PIPELINE whose
maximiser sits ABOVE a fixed teacher T (here Qwen3.8-27B), and what evidence does it give?
Read 2026-09-21 from the primary pages (arXiv abs+HTML, vendor blogs). Numbers quoted are the
papers' own; "[mem]" marks a figure recalled from an earlier reading where the live page could not
be fetched. Our setting, for reference (AGENTS.md §2): turn = min(R, G[, A]) under teacher-forced
logprobs of a FROZEN teacher; R rewards thoughts that predict the teacher's own sampled next
actions; G is a two-sided typicality band around the teacher's own thought likelihoods; crown =
paired mean > max(2·SE, 0.002). Maximiser of every leg = the teacher itself; kings sit at teacher
parity; benchmarks plateau at teacher level. Failed first attempt (2026-09-20): reward closeness to
a frontier model where it disagrees with the teacher — per-turn disagreement is sampling noise
(teacher agrees with its own resample 36 %, with the frontier 25 %) and closeness-to-frontier does
not track benchmarks across kings.

Notation used below: x = turn prefix; z = thought; y = action; T(.|x) = blind teacher;
T(.|x, p) = the same teacher given privileged context p (hint, hindsight, solution, feedback);
"duel-time" = a per-turn score computed on the eval pod; "fold-time" = a change to how D (or the
reference distribution attached to D) is built; "miner-side" = something miners do to train.

1. Cursor — "Introducing Composer 2.5" (May 18, 2026) https://cursor.com/blog/composer-2-5
   + "Introducing Composer 2" (Mar 19, 2026) https://cursor.com/blog/composer-2
   + "Composer: Building a fast frontier model with RL" (Oct 29, 2025) https://cursor.com/blog/composer
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) What it does. Composer 2.5 (Kimi K2.5 base) is trained with large-scale RL on verifiable coding
tasks; three additions: (a) "Targeted RL with textual feedback": for a flagged model message, write a
short hint describing the desired improvement, insert it into the LOCAL context, take the model's own
distribution under the hinted context as TEACHER, keep the un-hinted policy as STUDENT, add an
on-policy-distillation KL loss moving student token probabilities toward the teacher FOR THAT TURN
ONLY, alongside the ordinary trajectory-level RL reward. (b) Dynamic hard-task selection + 25x more
synthetic verified tasks (e.g. "feature deletion": delete a tested feature, task = reimplement, tests
= reward). (c) Agentic monitoring for reward hacking (model reverse-engineered a type-checker cache
and decompiled Java bytecode to recover deleted code). Composer 1 (Oct 2025): RL with production
tools in hundreds of thousands of sandboxed environments, Cursor Bench of real requests with curated
solutions. Composer 2 (Mar 2026): continued pretraining + long-horizon RL; Terminal-Bench 2.0 61.7,
SWE-bench Multilingual 73.7 (Composer 1: 40.0 / 56.9). The blog's own footnote grounds the hint
mechanism in SDFT, SDPO and OPSD (sources 3a–c below).
(2) Signal/target and producer. Target = pi_theta(.|x, hint) — the SAME model, more context;
producer of the hint = a human/agent reviewer or an automatic detector (e.g. "Tool not found" error
=> hint "Reminder: available tools are ..."). No external teacher. Trajectory reward stays the
verifier.
(3) Why the optimum exceeds the base. The teacher is not a fixed external model; it is the current
policy plus information it lacked at decision time. Because the hint is inserted at the failing
turn, the hinted distribution lowers the wrong tool and raises a valid replacement; distilling that
back removes a localized failure that the sparse final reward could never attribute ("one error in
hundreds of tool calls has minimal impact on final reward"). The ceiling moves with the policy (the
policy is its own teacher) and with the hint source, not with any fixed model.
(4) Gains/conditions. No per-component numbers published; qualitative: applied to "coding style to
model communication". Conditions: needs a way to FIND the turn to hint (error strings, monitors,
reviewers) and a hint that changes the distribution in the right direction.
(5) KOTH mapping.
  (a) Duel-time turn score: YES in principle — replace the k blind teacher refs (z_C^i, y_C^i) ~ T(.|x)
      by hinted refs ~ T(.|x, p) where p is a per-turn privileged context that the teacher, not the
      miner, sees: hindsight from the stored trajectory (what the next observations were, whether the
      trajectory later solved the task), the environment's error string for the recorded action, or a
      verified end-state summary. R_priv(z_A) = centered tempered LME of a_i = lpT(y_i^p | z_A) - lpT(y_i^p | 0).
      Maximiser = the miner whose thought best predicts what the teacher WOULD do knowing p: strictly
      above blind-teacher behaviour wherever p changes the teacher's action. This directly repairs the
      failed frontier attempt: the comparison is no longer frontier-sample vs teacher-sample (two
      noisy draws from different models) but T(.|x) vs T(.|x,p) on the SAME prefix under the SAME
      model — the disagreement is caused by p, not by sampling. Must-measure before adopting: teacher
      self-agreement WITH p (SDFT reports demo-conditioned teacher 100 % correct vs 42 % blind on
      ToolAlpaca — i.e. p can collapse the multi-modality that gives 36 % self-agreement today).
  (b) Fold-time: attach p to every D turn at fold (we already store full traces + outcomes; king-
      failure datagen supplies failed trajectories whose downstream turns are natural hints).
  (c) Miner-side: miners can run the identical construction themselves (SDPO-style) — desirable.
  Goodhart surface when public: if p contains the literal gold action, T(.|x,p) degenerates toward
  pasting it and R_priv becomes "predict the recorded action" (RT-10 leakage amplification, and a
  memorisation target for RT-6); PS-OPSD (source 7a) shows literal-solution conditioning leaks
  reference-specific dependence (3.0 % explicit "per the reference…" invocations). Prefer structural
  hindsight (outcome, error text, state deltas) over the gold action; keep refs per-duel fresh; keep
  B leakage check; compute G against BLIND refs (hinted thoughts reference unseen info and would
  shift the band). Cost: +1 teacher-sampling pass per ref with a longer prompt; no new model.

2. Thinking Machines Lab — "On-Policy Distillation" (Kevin Lu et al., Oct 27, 2025)
   https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation  (DOI 10.64434/tml.20251026)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Student samples its own trajectories; teacher scores every token with log-probs; loss = per-token
reverse KL, advantage = -(log pi_student - log pi_teacher) plugged into an RL importance-sampling
loss; discount 0 (only the immediate token). "One-line change on top of RL with KL regularization —
swap the regularizer model."
(2) Signal = teacher log-prob of the STUDENT's tokens; producer = a fixed stronger model
(Qwen3-32B / Qwen3-8B for a Qwen3-8B-Base student; Qwen3-235B for the illustration).
(3) Ceiling. Explicit in the post: reverse KL "is zero when the student behaves identically to the
teacher"; the loss is "unhackable in the sense that low KL always corresponds to high probability of
desirable behaviour from the teacher's point of view"; "since the teacher stays fixed, the student
converges on the teacher's desirable behaviour". Nothing here exceeds the teacher — the maximiser IS
the teacher. This is precisely our current meter's property (our R/G legs are teacher-forced echoes
of the same kind). The only lever the post offers for going beyond a fixed model is the phase-
alternating recipe: RL/search finds a better strategy (RL "spends compute on search"), then
distillation compresses it — i.e. the teacher must be a MOVED model.
(4) Gains. Qwen3 tech report Table 21 (as quoted): off-policy distill 55.0 AIME'24 -> +RL 67.6
(17,920 GPU-h) -> +on-policy distill 74.4 (1,800 GPU-h). Own replication: SFT-400K 60 % -> 70 % in
~150 steps / 77K prompts; 9–30x FLOP saving vs SFT-2M, ~50–100x vs RL when distilling an RL-trained
policy back into its own base (reverse KL ~0 and AIME recovered in <10 steps vs 70 RL steps).
Continual learning: midtrain 70 % docs drops IF-eval 85 -> 79, distill from the pre-midtrain model
restores 83 with QA 41 %. Warning result: SFT on a model's OWN samples (KL=0 data) degrades IF-eval
at any lr > 0 — finite-batch drift turns self-SFT off-policy.
(5) KOTH mapping. (a) Our duel score already is on-policy distillation in evaluation form; this source
is the cleanest statement of WHY kings plateau at the teacher. (b) Fold-time: none by itself. (c)
Miner-side: it is what miners do (SFT/RL toward T on D). Goodhart: low. Cost: n/a. Takeaway: any
target of the form "teacher log-prob of the miner's tokens under the blind teacher" has maximiser =
teacher; the teacher must be conditioned on something extra (sources 1, 3) or replaced by an outcome
(source 5) for the maximiser to move.

3a. "Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning" (SDFT) — Shenfeld, Damani, Hübotter, Agrawal
    (MIT/ETH), arXiv 2601.19897, v1 Jan 27 2026, v2 Aug 7 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19897
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Learn from expert DEMONSTRATIONS on-policy: teacher = same model conditioned on (query + one
demonstration for that query + "now answer with a response of your own, including the thinking
process"); student = same model on the query alone; sample from the student, minimise per-token
divergence to the teacher (theory says reverse KL; in practice forward KL + analytic per-token
estimator worked best; teacher = EMA of student). Shown equivalent to on-policy RL maximising the
implicit reward log pi(.|x,demo) - log pi(.|x) (an IRL reading: the in-context-conditioned model
approximates the trust-region-optimal next policy).
(2) Signal = pi(.|x, demonstration); producer = the model itself + a per-instance demonstration
(GPT-4o answers filtered for correctness; ToolAlpaca gold calls; HuatuoGPT answers).
(3) Why above base. "ICL assumption": the demo-conditioned teacher (i) is near-optimal — on ToolAlpaca
the blind model solves 42 %, the demo-conditioned teacher 100 %, with valid reasoning in 50/50
inspected traces (it reconstructs a correct process rather than copying) — and (ii) stays close to
the base: KL to base 0.68 nats vs 1.26 for an SFT model. The teacher is strictly above the student
because it has instance-specific information the student lacks; the student's optimum is "behave as
if you had seen the demo", which is above the blind model wherever the blind model errs.
(4) Gains/conditions. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct: Science QA 32.1 -> 70.2 (SFT 66.2) with prior-capability
avg 64.5 vs base 65.5 (SFT 53.4); Tool use 42.9 -> 70.6 (SFT 63.2); Medical 30.1 -> 40.2 (SFT 35.5).
Knowledge acquisition (2025 disasters): strict 89 % vs SFT 80 %, OOD 98 % vs 80 %, oracle-RAG 91/100.
Olmo-3-7B-Think with answer-only supervision: 31.2 -> 43.7 (SFT 23.5, which collapsed reasoning
length 4612 -> 3273 tokens). pass@k gains persist to k=128 (not entropy collapse). Scale dependence:
3B < SFT; 7B +4 pts over SFT; 14B +7 pts. On-policy is essential: offline distillation from the SAME
teacher underperforms (Fig 6). Cost ~2.5x FLOPs, ~4x wall-clock vs SFT. Failure modes: "learned
artifacts" (student says "Based on the text…" without having any text — fixed by masking loss on the
first tokens); cannot induce a fundamentally new generation pattern (non-reasoning -> reasoning).
Teacher choice: frozen base teacher stable but weak; student-as-teacher diverges; EMA best.
(5) KOTH mapping. (a) Duel-time: the demonstration is exactly the privileged context p of source 1.
For a coding turn the natural per-turn demonstration is the REST OF THE RECORDED TRAJECTORY (what was
done next and whether it worked) — hindsight — or a frontier model's completed solution for the same
task (not its next-turn sample). Refs y_i^p ~ T(.|x, p) then define R_priv as in §1(5). The
"reconstructs rather than copies" finding is the argument that T(.|x,p) is a legitimate target rather
than a paste: measure it on our teacher (fraction of hinted refs whose action equals the gold action
verbatim; B leakage rate). (b) Fold-time: store p per turn in D. (c) Miner-side: SDFT is the recipe
miners would use to climb toward T(.|x,p). Goodhart when public: literal-demo p => memorisation/paste
(RT-6, RT-10); artifact tokens ("as shown in the reference") in miner thoughts would raise R_priv
only if the teacher's hinted refs also carry them — mask/strip them, and score G against blind refs.
Cost: one longer teacher prompt per ref.

3b. "Reinforcement Learning via Self-Distillation" (SDPO) — Hübotter et al. (ETH/MIT/Stanford/MPI),
    arXiv 2601.20802, v1 Jan 28 2026, v2 Feb 16 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20802
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) RL with RICH FEEDBACK: after a rollout, re-prompt the SAME model with (question + a correct
solution from the rollout group if one exists + the environment's textual feedback on the failed
attempt) and re-score the ORIGINAL attempt's tokens under that context; the self-teacher's log-probs
minus the student's give per-token (top-100 logit-level) advantages; train with a standard RLVR loop
by swapping advantages. Teacher regularised (EMA or trust-region interpolation with the initial
model); JSD for stability; "no sampling overhead — one extra forward pass".
(2) Signal = pi(.|x, feedback, [sample solution], original attempt); producer = the environment
(runtime errors, failing tests, judge text) + the model's own successful siblings.
(3) Why above base. The self-teacher "should have higher accuracy than the student since it sees
additional information in-context"; crucially the teacher is UPDATED with the student, and the paper
shows the student's accuracy eventually SURPASSES the INITIAL teacher's (Fig 10 right) — "true
bootstrapping of a weak model to a strong model, without the initial self-teacher's performance
limiting the final student". Even sequence-level SDPO (rich feedback, no dense credit) beats GRPO,
so the information in the feedback, not only the density, moves the ceiling. Test-time: on LCB
questions with pass@64 < 0.03 the initial self-teacher solves ~0 % (Table 11), yet iterated SDPO
finds solutions — the teacher need not be able to solve the task for its credit assignment to help.
(4) Gains/conditions. LCBv6 Qwen3-8B: 48.8 % vs GRPO 41.2 %, above Claude Sonnet 4 (40.5) / Opus 4
(39.7) on that subset; GRPO's final accuracy reached with fewer generations (abstract: "3x fewer
attempts" for test-time discovery). Scalar-only RLVR envs (SciKnowEval, ToolAlpaca): 70.2 % vs 66.6 %
aggregate; Olmo3-7B chemistry 22.8 -> 80.0 (GRPO 56.7) and reaches GRPO's 5-h accuracy in 50 min;
responses shorter (GRPO 5,549 vs SDPO 764 tokens example). Scale: gains grow with model size; SDPO
UNDERPERFORMS GRPO on Qwen2.5-1.5B; SDPO+GRPO hybrid rescues 0.6B but slightly hurts 8B. Ablations:
including the student's own attempt in the teacher prompt biases the teacher toward it and reduces
entropy; environment output and sample solutions are complementary. Forgetting: better than GRPO
on IFEval/ArenaHard/MMLU-Pro; SFT on self-teacher samples needs 2x generations and forgets more.
(5) KOTH mapping. (a) Duel-time: two options. (i) p = environment feedback that the RECORDED action
produced (already in the trace: tool output, test failures) — cheap, no execution at duel time. (ii)
p = execute the MINER's action in the sandbox and feed the observation back to the teacher — turns
the duel into an environment-anchored scorer (expensive; see §5). (b) Fold-time: SDPO's "successful
sibling as feedback for a failed attempt" maps onto king-failure datagen: for a failed king turn,
p = the teacher's or frontier's successful continuation of the same task; refs ~ T(.|x, p) are the
above-teacher target for that turn. (c) Miner-side: SDPO is exactly what a miner would run against
Prime environments. Goodhart when public: the ceiling now depends on the feedback quality ("if the
environment provides misleading feedback, a model may not learn") — miners could try to induce
feedback that flatters them if p depends on THEIR action (option ii); with option (i) p is fixed by
the trace, so the surface is the same as §3a. Cost: (i) ~free; (ii) one sandbox step per turn per side.

3c. "Self-Distilled Reasoner: On-Policy Self-Distillation for LLMs" (OPSD) — Zhao, Xie, Liu, Huang,
    Pang, Chen, Grover (UCLA/HKU/Meta), arXiv 2601.18734, v1 Jan 26 2026, v3 Mar 20 2026.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18734
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Teacher = same model given (problem + verified reference solution + "after understanding the
reference, solve it your own way"); student = problem only; student samples ONE 1024-token rollout;
full-vocabulary forward-KL per token (beats reverse KL and JSD: AIME25 1.7B 36.7 -> 43.9 fwd vs 37.5
rev vs 36.9 JSD) with per-token POINTWISE CLIPPING because stylistic tokens ("Wait", connectives)
carry larger divergence than math tokens; teacher FROZEN at the initial checkpoint (stabilises).
Best mode pairing: thinking-OFF student / thinking-ON teacher.
(2) Signal = pi_0(.|x, reference solution); producer = the dataset's verified solution (OpenThoughts
math, 30K pairs) — "rationalisation is easier than generation".
(3) Why above base. A capable model can rationalise a correct solution it could not produce; the
conditioned distribution is a better next-token predictor along the student's own prefixes. Note the
teacher is FROZEN here, so the ceiling is pi_0(.|x, solution) — fixed but above pi_0(.|x); OPSD shows
this alone yields gains, the moving-teacher of SDPO is not required.
(4) Gains. Avg@12 over AIME24/25 + HMMT25: Qwen3-1.7B 37.1 -> 43.4 (GRPO 37.7, SFT 35.8); 4B 61.2 ->
63.6 (GRPO 62.7); 8B 61.8 -> 64.8 (GRPO 64.0). One rollout of 1,024 tokens per problem, 100 steps vs
GRPO's 8 x 16k-token rollouts, 500 steps; >50 % of GRPO batches had zero reward std within 100
steps. Full-vocab logit distillation > sampled-token (pass@8 AIME25 84.1 vs 82.1). Longer student
rollouts (4096) did not help — early tokens matter most (later tokens predictable given the prefix).
(5) KOTH mapping. (a) Duel-time: identical construction to §3a with p = verified solution of the TASK
(for coding turns: the merged PR / gold patch / passing test set; for math: the boxed answer). The
"early tokens carry the signal" finding means a per-turn thought/action score (our unit) is the right
granularity. (b) Fold-time: many D sources already carry gold outcomes (SWE tasks with patches, math
answers). (c) Miner-side: yes. Goodhart when public: literal-solution conditioning is the most
leak-prone p (see 7a: 3.0 % explicit reference invocations; the teacher's refs may paste the patch);
B leakage check + two-sided G are the existing guards. Cost: as §3a.

4. Learning Using Privileged Information lineage
   Vapnik & Vashist, "A new learning paradigm: LUPI" (Neural Networks 2009); Vapnik & Izmailov, JMLR
   2015. Lopez-Paz, Bottou, Schölkopf, Vapnik, "Unifying distillation and privileged information",
   arXiv 1511.03643 (Nov 2015; ICLR 2016) https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03643
   Pinto, Andrychowicz, Welinder, Zaremba, Abbeel, "Asymmetric Actor Critic for Image-Based Robot
   Learning", arXiv 1710.06542 (Oct 18 2017) https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06542
   Andrychowicz et al., "Hindsight Experience Replay", arXiv 1707.01495 (Jul 2017) https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01495
   Zhang, Liu, Wong, Abbeel, Gonzalez, "The Wisdom of Hindsight Makes Language Models Better
   Instruction Followers" (HIR), arXiv 2302.05206 (Feb 10 2023) https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05206
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) LUPI: at training time a teacher provides extra features x* per example (never at test); SVM+
uses x* to model slack; generalised distillation (Lopez-Paz 2015) shows Hinton distillation and LUPI
are one thing — train f_t on (x*, y), then train the student on x with softened teacher labels; the
theory gives a fast O(1/n) rate when the privileged teacher is easy to learn. Asymmetric actor-critic:
critic sees the simulator's full state, actor sees only rendered images; "significantly improves
performance" on Fetch pick/push/move tasks and transfers to a real robot with domain randomisation.
HER: replay failed episodes relabelled with the goal actually achieved; makes binary-reward pushing/
sliding/pick-and-place learnable where plain DDPG fails ("a crucial ingredient which makes training
possible"). HIR: relabel the INSTRUCTION of a language-model output in hindsight to match what the
output actually did (goal-conditioned view of alignment), then supervised fine-tune; two phases
(online sampling, offline relabelled SFT), uses HER-style relabelling of failures + contrastive
instruction labelling; FLAN-T5 on 12 BigBench reasoning tasks outperforms PPO and Final-Answer RL and
is "comparable to or even surpasses" SFT.
(2) Signal producers: LUPI — a training-time-only feature provider; asymmetric AC — the simulator's
privileged state feeding the VALUE function only; HER/HIR — the environment's realised outcome used to
rewrite the goal/instruction.
(3) Why above base/teacher. All four move the target by giving the LEARNING SIGNAL (critic, label,
relabelled goal) information the deployed policy cannot see. The critic in asymmetric AC is a
legitimate above-policy judge because value estimation with full state is easier and less biased than
from partial observations; the actor's optimum is the best partially-observed policy under a
correctly-estimated value — above what a critic on partial observations would induce. HER/HIR turn
every trajectory into a positive example of SOMETHING, so the data distribution covers states the
expert never visited (learner-state coverage, cf. DAgger) and the target is "the instruction this
output satisfies", produced by the environment, not by a fixed model.
(4) Gains: HER pick-and-place: ~0 -> near-complete success with binary reward [mem]; asymmetric AC:
tasks unsolvable with a symmetric image critic become solvable [mem]; HIR: average over 12 BigBench
tasks above PPO/FA-RL, near SFT (Fig 1; exact numbers not re-fetched).
(5) KOTH mapping. (a) Duel-time: the asymmetric-critic idea is the cleanest template for our A leg:
score the MINER's action with a value function that is trained on privileged information (full
trajectory outcome, hidden tests, gold patch) but evaluated on (x, y_A). The A-leg probe (AGENTS.md
v6) failed because per-byte teacher likelihood is not a value; a critic trained on outcomes is.
(b) Fold-time: HER/HIR relabelling = for every failed king trajectory in D, attach the goal it DID
achieve (or the instruction it did follow), giving positive-labelled turns from learner states; and
for trajectory turns before the failure point, attach hindsight "this path fails at step t because
…" as p. (c) Miner-side: miners can do HIR themselves. Goodhart when public: a learned critic is a
public reward model => hackable (see §5 AgentPRM Fig 3: process reward keeps rising while success
falls); must be re-fit each fold from fresh rollouts (moving target) and kept teacher-sized to be
replayable. Cost: a critic training run per fold + one forward pass per turn per side at duel time.

5. Process reward models from Monte-Carlo rollouts and agent-step credit
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5a. Lightman et al. (OpenAI), "Let's Verify Step by Step", arXiv 2305.20050 (May 31 2023)
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050
 Human step labels (PRM800K, 800K labels); PRM best-of-1860 solves 78 % of MATH500 vs ORM 72.4 %
 and majority 69.6 % [mem for the latter two]; active learning (label the convincing wrong answers)
 gives ~2.6x label efficiency [mem]. Supervising up to the FIRST error suffices. Ceiling: human
 judgement of step correctness — above the generator, but the labeler cost is prohibitive; it is the
 reference for what MC labels approximate.

5b. Wang et al. (PKU/DeepSeek), "Math-Shepherd: Verify and Reinforce LLMs Step-by-step without Human
    Annotations", arXiv 2312.08935 (Dec 14 2023; v3 Feb 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08935
 (1) Label a step by its POTENTIAL to reach the gold answer: a fixed "completer" LLM decodes N
 continuations from the step; hard estimate HE = 1 if any reaches the gold answer, soft estimate SE =
 fraction that do. Train a PRM on these labels; use it for best-of-256 reranking and step-by-step PPO
 (reward at the end of each step). (2) Signal = MC success frequency under a FIXED completer
 (Llemma-7B, N = 8; generator samples 15 solutions/problem; RM trained on LLaMA2-70B/Llemma-34B);
 producer = the environment's answer check + the completer. (3) Above base: the label is anchored to
 the OUTCOME, not to any model's likelihood; a step is good if it leads to success under the
 completer, so the maximiser of the PRM is "take steps from which the completer succeeds" — above the
 generator and, if the completer is strong, at or above the completer's own greedy behaviour (the
 PRM-guided policy exploits the completer's pass@N, not its pass@1). (4) Gains: Mistral-7B greedy
 77.9 -> 84.1 GSM8K, 28.6 -> 33.0 MATH with step-PPO; 89.1 / 43.5 with verification; DeepSeek-67B
 93.3 / 48.1 with verification. Label noise: on a hand-annotated sample of GSM8K steps, HE accuracy 86 %
 at N = 4 with a 70B completer, and it DECLINES with larger N (false positives: a wrong step can
 still be rescued); larger completers give better labels; a completer that has not seen the
 questions labels badly. Cost: N x (remaining trajectory) completions per step.

5c. Luo et al. (Google DeepMind), "Improve Mathematical Reasoning in Language Models by Automated
    Process Supervision" (OmegaPRM), arXiv 2406.06592 (Jun 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06592
 (1) Binary-search for the FIRST error (rollouts from the midpoint; if any succeeds the error is
 later) inside an MCTS that reuses rollouts as new tree nodes, with a selection heuristic favouring
 rollouts whose MC value is near 0.5 (convincing wrong answers) and short length. (2) Same signal as
 Math-Shepherd (MC success under the policy itself as completer, k completions per node, Gemini Pro /
 Gemma2-27B). (3) Same reason; efficiency lets the label set be ~1.5 M annotations. (4) Gemini Pro
 MATH500 51 -> 69.4 %, GSM8K 86.4 -> 93.6 % with PRM-weighted self-consistency; Gemma2-27B 42.3 ->
 58.2 / 74.0 -> 92.2; OmegaPRM-trained PRM > PRM trained on human PRM800K; brute-force per-step MC at
 the same budget yields far fewer labels (paper: "-times efficiency improvement", figure stripped in
 the HTML). Limitations stated: labels are noisy (false positives/negatives), needs question + gold
 answer pairs ("human supervision is still necessary").

5d. Choudhury (Cornell), "Process Reward Models for LLM Agents: Practical Framework and Directions"
    (AgentPRM / InversePRM), arXiv 2502.10325 (Feb 14 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10325
 (1) Actor-critic policy iteration for agents: roll out the current policy asynchronously, compute Q
 targets per (state, action) as discounted outcome returns normalised to [0,1] (explicitly simpler
 than MCTS), train a PRM by soft BCE, then online DPO against the PRM with a KL to the CURRENT
 policy (conservative policy iteration — the PRM is only valid near the data policy). InversePRM:
 no outcome reward — PRM = classifier of expert vs learner transitions (IRL as a no-regret game),
 then the same RL step. (2) Signal = MC outcome return of the policy's own rollouts (~10k) /
 expert-vs-learner discrimination. (3) Above base because the target is the outcome; iteration
 moves the ceiling: ALFWorld success 64.9 -> 73.9 -> 85.8 -> 88.1 over iterations with a 3B
 Llama3.2, surpassing GPT-4o and claude-3.5-sonnet prompting baselines; InversePRM reaches near-expert
 in ONE iteration. (4) Reward hacking measured directly (Fig 3): with a PRM trained on 10k rollouts,
 validation process reward keeps rising while true success peaks then falls — over-optimisation past
 the PRM's data distribution; re-training the PRM on the new policy's rollouts is the fix; best-of-N
 gains shrink for later policies for the same reason. "An open question remains how to reliably
 detect over-optimization without evaluating the success rate."

5e. Khalifa et al., "Process Reward Models That Think" (ThinkPRM), arXiv 2504.16828 (Apr 2025; v5
    Dec 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16828
 Generative long-CoT verifier fine-tuned on ~1 % of PRM800K's process labels (synthetic verification
 CoTs filtered against gold step labels); beats discriminative PRMs trained on full PRM800K on
 ProcessBench / MATH-500 / AIME'24 best-of-N and reward-guided search; OOD GPQA-Diamond +8 %, LCB
 +4.5 %; scales verification compute better than LLM-as-judge (+7.2 % ProcessBench at equal tokens).
 Relevance: a verbalised step judge is a way to turn a strong model's judgement into a step score
 with few labels — but it is a fixed model again (ceiling = the verifier), and a public generative
 judge is the SN97 failure mode we exist to avoid.

5f. Agent-step credit assignment for SWE / multi-turn agents
 - SWE-RL (Wei et al., Meta), arXiv 2502.18449 (Feb 2025; v2 Dec 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18449:
   NO step value at all — reward = difflib.SequenceMatcher similarity between the predicted patch and
   the oracle PR patch (0..1; -1 for bad format), GRPO on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct over open PR data;
   41.0 % SWE-bench Verified with Agentless Mini; OOD gains (math, code reasoning) where an SFT model
   regressed. Lesson for us: a HINDSIGHT similarity-to-gold-patch reward on the FINAL edit is enough to
   move a 70B model; per-turn credit was not needed at this scale.
 - Kimi-Dev (Moonshot), arXiv 2509.23045 (Sep 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23045: "agentless
   training as skill prior" — single-turn verifiable sub-skills (localisation, edit, self-reflection)
   trained with RL, 60.4 % SWE-bench Verified agentless; then SFT on 5k public agent trajectories ->
   48.6 % pass@1 as an agent. Lesson: verifiable single-turn steps (our turn unit) transfer to agents.
 - DeepSWE (Agentica/Together, Jul 2025, blog, JS page not fetchable here) [mem]: Qwen3-32B, GRPO++
   on 4.5k R2E-Gym tasks with a SPARSE final test reward only (they report partial/step rewards were
   deliberately NOT used), 42.2 % pass@1 SWE-bench Verified, 59.0 % with hybrid test-time scaling.
 - Agent-R (Yuan et al.), arXiv 2501.11425 (Jan 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11425: MCTS over
   agent trajectories; the actor itself locates the FIRST bad step of a failed trajectory (within its
   current ability), splice it onto the sibling correct path from the same parent, SFT on these
   "revision trajectories"; +5.59 % over baselines on WebShop/SciWorld/TextCraft; revision trajectories
   beat expert trajectories; fewer loops. Lesson: the transition point (first error) is the per-turn
   label that matters, and it can be estimated by the model given the tree.
 - RAGEN / StarPO (Wang et al.), arXiv 2504.20073 (Apr 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20073:
   trajectory-level agent RL collapses ("Echo Trap": reward-variance cliff, entropy drop, gradient
   spikes); PPO's critic delays collapse vs GRPO; "without fine-grained, reasoning-aware reward
   signals, agent reasoning hardly emerges … shallow strategies or hallucinated thoughts". Lesson: a
   thought that is not scored drifts to filler — exactly why our G/R legs exist, and a warning that an
   outcome-only turn value lets thoughts rot.
 - GiGPO (Feng et al.), arXiv 2505.10978 (May 2025; v3 Oct 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10978:
   step-level credit WITHOUT extra rollouts — "anchor state grouping": across a group of trajectories
   for the same task, actions taken from the SAME environment state are grouped and their discounted
   returns compared (micro advantage), added to the episode-level GRPO advantage; >12 % ALFWorld,
   >9 % WebShop over GRPO, same memory/rollouts. Lesson: a paired within-state comparison of actions
   by downstream outcome is a valid step value; in our duel the same prefix x already hosts k teacher
   refs + king + challenger actions — completing each to outcome gives a GiGPO group per turn.
 How noisy / how many rollouts (synthesis): Math-Shepherd N = 4–8 with HE accuracy 86 % against
 human labels and worsening at larger N; OmegaPRM k completions per node with binary search over the
 first error; AgentPRM ~10k rollouts per PRM iteration and visible over-optimisation within one RL
 phase; GiGPO needs only the group already sampled (8 trajectories/task typical [mem]).
(5) KOTH mapping for the whole PRM family.
  (a) Duel-time: an environment-anchored A leg — execute the miner's action y_A (and the teacher's k
      refs) in the task sandbox and complete each with a FIXED completer (the teacher, or the king
      itself) N times; turn value = SE(y_A) - mean SE(y_C^i) (paired at the same anchor state, GiGPO-
      style). Maximiser = actions from which the completer succeeds most — above the teacher's own
      pass@1 whenever the teacher's blind action is not its best action, and independent of any
      model's likelihood. Requires environment access on the eval pod for every turn (D is 280k+ turns
      of coding/terminal traces; only the sampled 1300 need execution, but each MC completion is a
      full remaining trajectory: minutes per turn x N x sides). Noise: at N = 4, label accuracy ~86 %
      per step; per-turn SE granularity 1/N, so 1300 turns give SE ~ sqrt(0.25/(N·1300)) ≈ 0.007 at
      N = 4 — comparable to today's margins only after averaging; most turns will be ties (both fail
      or both succeed) so the effective n is the fraction of "contested" turns.
  (b) Fold-time (cheaper, replayable): label D turns with MC values once (OmegaPRM-style, using the
      teacher as completer), publish the values with the manifest, and train a teacher-sized PRM /
      critic that scores (x, y) at duel time with one echo pass. Maximiser = the PRM's notion of
      "leads to success", above the teacher. Gaming: AgentPRM Fig 3 says a public frozen PRM WILL be
      over-optimised; SN97 history says the same. Mitigation available to us that AgentPRM lacked:
      the PRM is re-fit every fold from the CURRENT king's rollouts (king-failure datagen already
      collects them), i.e. the reward model tracks the incumbent — conservative policy iteration by
      construction, published as a data event.
  (c) Miner-side: miners can run Math-Shepherd/AgentPRM/GiGPO against Prime environments themselves;
      all the SWE evidence (SWE-RL 41.0, DeepSWE 42.2, Kimi-Dev 60.4) is outcome-anchored RL that
      miners could do if the meter rewarded outcome-anchored behaviour.
  Cost ordering: GiGPO-style paired completion at duel time (highest, needs sandboxes on the eval
  pod) > fold-time MC labelling + PRM (large one-off compute, cheap duel) > hindsight-similarity
  reward on final edits (SWE-RL; near-free but only for final-turn/`text`/patch actions).

6. DAgger; expert iteration; STaR; ReST-EM; rejection-sampling fine-tuning
   Ross, Gordon, Bagnell, "A Reduction of Imitation Learning and Structured Prediction to No-Regret
   Online Learning" (DAgger), arXiv 1011.0686 (Nov 2010; AISTATS 2011) https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686
   Anthony, Tian, Barber, "Thinking Fast and Slow with Deep Learning and Tree Search" (ExIt), arXiv
   1705.08439 (May 2017) https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439
   Zelikman, Wu, Mu, Goodman, "STaR: Bootstrapping Reasoning With Reasoning", arXiv 2203.14465
   (Mar 2022) https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14465
   Singh et al. (Google DeepMind), "Beyond Human Data: Scaling Self-Training for Problem-Solving with
   Language Models" (ReST-EM), arXiv 2312.06585 (Dec 2023; v4 Apr 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06585
   Yuan et al., "Scaling Relationship on Learning Mathematical Reasoning with LLMs" (RFT), 2023 [mem]
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) DAgger: iterate — roll out the current learner (mixed with the expert by beta_i), have the EXPERT
label the states the LEARNER visited, aggregate all data, retrain; a no-regret reduction giving
J(learner) <= J(expert) + O(T·eps) instead of behaviour cloning's O(T^2·eps) compounding error.
ExIt: expert = current network + tree search (MCTS); network imitates the expert's improved moves;
the improved network then makes the search stronger (Hex: beats MoHex 1.0 from tabula rasa). STaR:
sample rationales; for failures, RATIONALISE — regenerate the rationale given a hint containing the
correct answer; fine-tune on all rationales whose answer is correct; repeat (GSM8K 10.7 % vs 5.8 %
few-shot CoT [mem]; CommonsenseQA 72.5 % ≈ a 30x larger model). ReST-EM: EM view — E-step sample 32
(MATH) / 64 (APPS) solutions per problem, keep those a binary verifier accepts (cap 10 per problem
for diversity), M-step fine-tune the BASE model on them; 2–3 iterations. RFT = one iteration.
(2) Signal producers: DAgger — an expert oracle on learner states (ceiling = expert); ExIt — search
around the current policy (ceiling moves); STaR/ReST-EM/RFT — a VERIFIER (ceiling = what the sampler
can hit at pass@N, i.e. above pass@1 of the same model, compounding across iterations).
(3) Why above base. DAgger does NOT exceed its expert — it closes the learner-state gap to the expert
(our king-failure datagen is exactly DAgger with T as expert, hence teacher-parity is its fixed
point). ExIt/ReST-EM/STaR exceed the CURRENT policy because the "expert" is policy + search/filter,
so each iteration's target is the previous policy's pass@N distribution restricted to successes.
(4) Gains/conditions. ReST-EM: PaLM 2-L on MATH and APPS "substantially outperforms" SFT on human
solutions (Figs 2–3), gains larger for the larger model (opposite of RFT's diminishing returns on
GSM8K), transfers to GSM8K / Hungarian finals / HumanEval / BBH; MORE THAN ~2–3 ITERATIONS OVERFITS
(APPS regresses after iteration 1); pass@k and majority voting also improve. STaR needs the hint for
failures (else the loop stalls on hard items). ExIt needs a search procedure that improves on the
network's move — for LLM agents, "search" = MC completions / best-of-N with a verifier.
(5) KOTH mapping. (a) Duel-time: verified-filter distillation as a REFERENCE construction — sample the
teacher's k refs per turn as today, but keep only refs whose CONTINUATION (completed by the teacher
in the sandbox, or checked against the recorded outcome) succeeds; R/A against success-filtered refs
have maximiser "the teacher's successful mode" > the teacher's average mode. Same execution cost as
§5(a) minus the miner-side completions; where D already stores a successful trajectory (the trace),
"filtered" = "use the recorded successful action as the ref" — but that is the leakage/memorisation
target (RT-6) and per-duel-fresh refs are the RT-6 defence, so the filter must be applied to FRESH
teacher samples, not to the stored action. (b) Fold-time: ReST-EM over the teacher/frontier/king
pool — keep only verified-successful trajectories in D as reference material, with the cap-per-task
diversity rule; the 2–3-iteration overfitting warning maps to "refresh the pool each epoch, do not
re-fold the same successes". (c) Miner-side: STaR/ReST-EM/RFT are what a rational miner does with
Prime environments; STaR's hint step is the privileged-self-teacher of §3 in hard-label form.
Goodhart when public: success-filtered refs are still teacher-produced, so the RT-9/RT-10 (mode
guessing / leakage) surface is unchanged; the filter makes the target sharper (fewer modes) which
helps R's centering but makes mode-guessing pay more — watch a_i spread telemetry. Cost: sandbox
completions for the refs only (k = 3 per turn, shared across sides via RefCache).

CROSS-CUTTING CONCLUSIONS (<= 25 lines)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Any "frozen-teacher likelihood of the miner's tokens" score (our R/G/A; TML, GKD, Qwen3 on-policy
   distillation) has maximiser = the teacher exactly (reverse KL = 0 iff student = teacher). No fix exists
   inside that form: the teacher must be conditioned on more, or replaced by an outcome.
2. Three mechanisms put the maximiser above a fixed teacher. (i) PRIVILEGED CONDITIONING: the same teacher
   given per-turn information the miner cannot see — hint, hindsight, env feedback, verified solution
   (SDFT: teacher 42 % -> 100 % correct with a demo; OPSD +6.3 pts at 1.7B with a FROZEN privileged
   teacher; SDPO: student surpasses its initial teacher when the teacher co-moves). (ii) OUTCOME
   ANCHORING: MC success from the step under a fixed completer (Math-Shepherd, OmegaPRM, AgentPRM, GiGPO)
   — maximiser is the environment. (iii) ITERATION with a verifier/search (ExIt, ReST-EM, STaR,
   AgentPRM): the target is the previous policy's own successes.
3. Frontier models belong as HINT/DEMO producers for the privileged teacher (completed solutions,
   critiques, PS-OPSD-style structure), never as a per-turn likelihood target: comparing T(.|x) with
   T(.|x,p) on ONE prefix under ONE model removes the cross-model sampling noise the 09-20 attempt hit.
4. Environment access is required for (ii), (iii) and any feedback on the miner's OWN action; NOT for (i)
   when p comes from the stored trace (downstream observations, outcome/error text) — cheapest for us.
5. Gameability when public: literal-solution p => paste/memorise (RT-6/RT-10; PS-OPSD 3.0 % explicit
   reference invocations); a frozen learned PRM => over-optimised within one RL phase (AgentPRM Fig 3);
   a generative judge => SN97. Least gameable: outcome anchoring with fresh per-duel completions; then
   STRUCTURAL p with per-duel-fresh refs; then a PRM re-fit every fold.
6. Fit. Duel-time: privileged refs (R_priv/A_priv, G kept against blind refs) reuse the echo pipeline at
   ~one longer teacher prompt per ref; paired outcome completion needs eval-pod sandboxes (min/turn x N).
   Fold-time: ReST-EM verified filtering of refs, HER/HIR relabelling of king failures, MC value labels +
   per-fold PRM re-fit, p attached to every turn. Miner-side: SDPO/STaR/ReST-EM/AgentPRM follow once the
   meter rewards them; DAgger alone (king-failure datagen) stays at teacher parity — its expert is blind
   T. Caveats: privileged self-teaching fails below ~3B (27B is in-regime); how much p to reveal = dial.
