Written 2026-09-20 (UTC) for Affine / SN120 (AGENTS.md §1–2). Sources were fetched as arXiv HTML/abs pages (or the vendor page) on
2026-09-20; numbers marked (mem) are recalled from prior reading, not re-read from the fetched text — treat as approximate.

Question every section answers: how could this mechanism anchor a TURN SCORE (duel-time), a DATA PIPELINE for D (fold-time), or
miner-side training to something ABOVE a fixed teacher (Qwen3.8-27B), given: score must replay from published artifacts; public
targets get memorised (RT-6 lesson: a published per-turn target IS the target); a static judge gets Goodharted (Albedo SN97 GLM
checklist: every judge-crowned king below genesis on swe-rebench, MOTIVATION.md); budget ~$10s and ~1 h per duel; live duel today =
~1000 turns, 2 sides, teacher-forced logprob legs (R/typicality/A), crown at paired margin > max(2·SE, δ).

Notation for the mappings: (a) duel-time turn score, (b) fold-time data mechanism for D, (c) miner-side training; "gaming" = what
a miner who sees the published rule + artifacts can do; "cost" = per duel unless stated.

PART 1 — VERIFIABLE-REWARD AGENTIC RL FOR CODING AGENTS (how verifiable task supply is made, what reward shape was used)
==========================================================================================================================

1.1 DeepSWE-Preview (Agentica × Together AI, 2025-07-01) — https://www.together.ai/blog/deepswe
(1) Qwen3-32B trained with pure RL (no SFT/distillation) as a multi-turn SWE agent on 4.5k R2E-Gym tasks; 200 RL steps, 6 days on
    64 H100; 42.2% pass@1 SWE-bench Verified (from 23%), 71.0% pass@16, 59% with hybrid best@16 test-time scaling.
(2) Reward: sparse OUTCOME only — 1 if the submitted patch passes the sampled F2P+P2P tests inside 5 min, else 0. Produced by the
    repository test suite (ground truth), not by any model. No per-step/turn reward. "Compact filtering": trajectories that hit
    max context / max steps / timeout are masked from the loss, so a lucky mid-trajectory pass without a deliberate submit earns
    nothing — this was needed to stop reward collapse ("patch random files after having fixed it").
(3) Optimum is the environment's pass rate, not any model's behaviour: the target is executable truth; more RL compute kept raising
    it; RL beat SFT/distillation from stronger proprietary teachers on comparable data.
(4) 4.5k tasks; 512 containers per RL step (BS 64 × 8 rollouts); Kubernetes because Docker daemon crashed; reward sparsity was OK
    at 32B because the base could already solve ~23%. Emergent behaviours: edge-case tests, regression-test search, adaptive
    per-step thinking (~2k tokens on localisation steps vs ~100–200 on navigation).
(5)(a) Duel-time: the only outcome-anchored turn score is "continue the trajectory from this turn with a FIXED continuation
    policy and grade the env" (a counterfactual value V(x, a_miner)). Cost is the blocker: 1000 turns × 2 sides × k continuations ×
    ~20 steps × ~2k tokens ≈ 10^8–10^9 generated tokens + 10^3–10^4 container-minutes; fits ~1 h only on a SUBSET (e.g. 100–200
    late-depth turns where the remaining horizon is short) or with a cheap continuation policy. Replay: store env image digest,
    seed, continuation transcript, grader log → replayable bit-for-bit only if the env is deterministic (mini-swe/verifiers
    envs mostly are; flaky tests must be filtered as SWE-rebench does).
    (b) Fold-time: outcome labels per ROLLOUT (already in D as `outcome`); DeepSWE's compact-filtering lesson says: exclude
    trajectories that hit caps from the "solved" bucket (already: `stop_condition` filter).
    (c) Miner-side: exactly this recipe is what a miner would run; the KOTH's job is to make the ranked quantity agree with it.
    Gaming: none on the outcome itself; the surface is the CONTINUATION policy (if it is the teacher, its blind spots cap the score;
    if it is the miner itself, the miner controls both action and continuation → must be the same fixed public policy for both
    sides, or paired-with-king continuation). Cost: $10–30 CPU + GPU generation dominated; see 1.6/1.7 for task supply.

1.2 SWE-RL (Meta FAIR, 2025-02-25) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18449
(1) RL on 11M GitHub PR seeds (from 24M aggregated PRs): given issue + code context, produce search/replace edits; Llama-3.3-70B →
    Llama3-SWE-RL-70B, 41.0% SWE-bench Verified with Agentless Mini (500 repair samples, 30 tests); OOD gains on math/code
    reasoning while the matched SFT baseline degraded.
(2) Reward: difflib.SequenceMatcher similarity ∈ [0,1] between predicted and ORACLE patch; −1 for malformed output. Target = the
    human developer's merged patch (ground truth by proxy, per-PR). GRPO. Ablation: a discrete exact-match reward stayed ≈ 0 all
    training (real patches are too diverse to match exactly) — the continuous partial-credit reward is what trained.
(3) Not capped by a model: the target is the human PR corpus (11M distinct targets). But it IS capped by the corpus: the optimum is
    "reproduce human patches", not "better than humans", and similarity ≠ correctness.
(4) Scale mattered: 24M → 11M PR instances, 70B policy; repair pass rate rose 33.6 → 40.0 → 41.0 with 20 → 160 → 500 samples
    (plateau past 160). No execution during RL (cheap), execution only at eval.
(5)(a) Duel-time analogue: score a miner's action by similarity to a hidden gold action — this is exactly the "published target"
    hole (RT-6) unless the gold is fresh per duel. Our teacher-anchored R is the logprob version of this idea with the TEACHER as
    the gold; SWE-RL shows the gold can be a HUMAN artifact instead (PR patch for coding turns). Similarity rewards are gameable by
    surface mimicry (whitespace/ordering hacks) — the paper accepted that risk because the seeds were private during training.
    (b) Fold-time: D turns whose task has a merged-PR oracle (swerebench/multiswe/scaleswe sources) could carry a `gold_patch`
    field; a turn's action can be scored on "does this edit move toward the gold diff" (execution-free, cheap, replayable).
    (c) Miner: the cheapest of all recipes (no containers). Gaming when public: memorise gold patches for public repos (contamination
    = the SWE-bench Verified problem, §1.8). Cost: ~0 compute beyond the echo; needs the oracle patch stored per task.

1.3 Kimi K2 technical report (Moonshot, 2025-07-28) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534
(1) 1T-param MoE; post-training built on (i) a large-scale agentic tool-use data synthesis pipeline and (ii) joint RL mixing
    verifiable rewards (RLVR gym) with a SELF-CRITIQUE RUBRIC reward for non-verifiable tasks. 65.8 SWE-bench Verified (71.6 with
    internal-verifier best-of-N), 66.1 τ²-bench, 76.5 ACEBench.
(2) Targets: (a) tool-use SFT data — 3,000+ real MCP tools + 20,000+ evolved synthetic tools, thousands of synthetic agents, tasks
    with explicit RUBRICS, a stateful LLM tool SIMULATOR ("world model") with controlled stochasticity, LLM user simulators, an
    LLM JUDGE filtering trajectories against the rubric (= large-scale rejection sampling); real sandboxes for coding where
    authenticity matters (test pass rates). (b) RL: deterministic verifiers where possible + LLM-judge with an anti-"claims
    compliance" hack-check layer; a sentence-level faithfulness judge; (c) self-critic pairwise comparisons against core +
    "prescriptive" (anti-reward-hacking) + human rubrics. The critic is CONTINUOUSLY REFINED on RLVR on-policy rollouts
    ("closed-loop critic refinement": objective signals from verifiable tasks are distilled into the judge that scores the
    subjective ones).
(3) Why not capped: the verifiable part is env truth; the subjective part is capped by the critic, but the critic co-evolves with
    the policy and is re-grounded on verifiable data — the fix Kimi used for the static-judge Goodhart problem is "judge moves
    with the policy and is re-anchored on ground truth every step" (cf. Intuitor §4.6 online vs offline reward).
(4) Tens of thousands of synthetic tool-use examples; only rubric-passing trajectories kept. No public ablation of critic drift.
(5)(a) Duel-time: a rubric judge as the turn score is the Albedo failure mode unless it is (i) re-anchored on verifiable outcomes
    each epoch and (ii) accompanied by a hack-check leg. Kimi's design says: keep the judge OFF the ranked quantity, or make it a
    gate re-trained per fold on env outcomes. (b) Fold-time: rubric-based task generation + simulator is how to get τ²/tool-use
    supply without real APIs (our tool_use/τ² axis moved with the wvk-22 shape change; simulator-generated tasks can renew D
    cheaply). (c) Miner: same recipe. Gaming: LLM-judge legs (verbosity, claim-of-compliance — Kimi needed an explicit hack-check).
    Cost: simulator tasks are API-token-only (~$ per 1k trajectories); rubric judging per turn ~1 API call/turn ($1–3/duel at
    flash-class prices per feasibility/recommendation.txt).

1.4 Kimi-Dev (Moonshot, 2025-09-27) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23045
(1) 72B "Agentless" SWE model: mid-training 150B tokens of GitHub issue/PR data, R1 cold start, RL on two roles — BugFixer and
    TestWriter — then test-time SELF-PLAY between them (40 patches × 40 tests, execution cross-matrix); 60.4% SWE-bench Verified
    (workflow SOTA); +5k agent trajectories SFT → 48.6% as an agent.
(2) Reward: OUTCOME ONLY, 0/1 from execution. BugFixer: passes ground-truth tests. TestWriter: test FAILS on buggy repo AND PASSES
    with the gold fix applied (both conditions). At test time the two roles verify each other: score(patch) = reproduction-test
    pass count + regression (P2P) count, no gold needed.
(3) Why not capped: the anchor is execution; the self-play at inference uses the model's own tests as a verifier, which improves
    with the model — but training still needed gold patches/tests (the TestWriter reward references the gold fix).
(4) Mid-training tokens scale monotonically (50B → 100B → 150B improves BugFixer pass@1); RL only on the edit stage; 40×40 execution
    matrix per instance at inference (expensive: 1,600 test runs/instance).
(5)(a) Duel-time: the TestWriter idea gives a GOLD-FREE turn verifier for coding turns: a miner's patch-action can be scored by
    tests written by a fixed public test-writer (fail-before/pass-after on the miner's edit) — verifiable, replayable, no
    published target text. Gaming: miner edits that pass generated tests while wrong (tests are weak; R2E-Gym §1.7 measured low
    test distinguishability). (b) Fold-time: TestWriter-style F2P tests for tasks that lack them (issue-only PR mirrors) is how to
    make more of D verifiable. (c) Miner: BugFixer/TestWriter RL is a strong recipe; expect miners to adopt it once actions are
    graded by execution. Cost: 2 test runs per scored action (~1–3 CPU-min) → 1000 turns × 2 sides ≈ 70–100 CPU-hours/duel
    (~$5–10) if containers are pre-built; only coding/terminal turns with an executable env qualify.

1.5 SWE-Gym (Pan et al., 2024-12-30) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139
(1) First real-repo SWE training env: 2,438 Python tasks from 11 repos with executable envs + unit tests (~200 human hours to build);
    rejection-sampling FT of Qwen2.5-Coder-32B on 491 successful OpenHands trajectories → +12.3 Lite / +13.6 Verified; trained a
    trajectory VERIFIER (outcome RM) → best@N 32.0% Verified (log-linear in N).
(2) Reward: env unit tests (ground truth) label trajectories; the learned verifier is a proxy trained on those labels.
(3) Not capped: the env test signal is truth; gains were compute-limited (no saturation at 491 trajectories), not task-limited.
(4) 2,438 tasks were enough for a 32B; 491 trajectories moved 13 points; verifier best@8–16 adds ~+11.
(5)(a) Duel-time: a trajectory/turn verifier trained on env outcomes is a cheap replayable turn score (one forward pass) BUT it is a
    learned proxy → Goodhart curve (Gao §3.5) once miners optimise against it; R2E-Gym found such verifiers "rely on stylistic
    features" and agent thoughts. Use only as a gate, re-trained per fold. (b) Fold-time: 200 human hours for 11 repos is the
    cost SWE-smith/SWE-rebench removed (§1.6, §1.9). (c) Miner: RFT from env success is the baseline recipe. Cost: verifier
    scoring ≈ 1 echo/turn, negligible.

1.6 SWE-smith (Yang et al., 2025-04-30) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21798
(1) Task synthesis at scale: build the env FIRST (SWE-agent installs the repo, human checks install/test parsing ~min per repo),
    then break it — LM Modify / LM Rewrite of functions, procedural AST mutation, combining bugs, PR Mirror (undo PRs) — keep only
    candidates that break ≥1 passing test (F2P) within 2 min; LM writes issue text from the F2P test + failure output. 50k
    instances / 128 repos for $1,360 total ($1,000 bugs, $160 installs, $200 issue text for 10k). 5,016 Claude-3.7 trajectories →
    SWE-agent-LM-32B 40.2% Verified.
(2) Reward/target: hidden test suite of the real repo (ground truth); tasks are synthetic but verification is real execution.
(3) Not capped: unlimited fresh tasks per repo; difficulty rating comparable to SWE-bench (5.27–5.72 vs 5.01, SWE-Gym 5.62); LM
    Rewrite and PR Mirror gave the most useful expert trajectories; more instances/bug types/repos all helped.
(4) Storage/labour collapse vs SWE-bench-style curation; difficulty classifier 75.3% test acc; LM issue text ≈ real issue text.
(5)(a) Duel-time: not a turn score, but the SUPPLY of fresh verifiable tasks the outcome score needs; fresh-per-duel tasks are the
    structural RT-6 defence (a task minted after the reveal cannot be memorised). (b) Fold-time: SWE-smith on any Python repo ⇒
    hundreds–thousands of graded tasks per repo at ~$0.03/task; D's coding share could be renewed each epoch and the KING-FAILURE
    seat can be run on synthetic tasks disjoint from public benchmarks. (c) Miner: same pipeline at home — the point is the
    verifier is not the target. Gaming: the four bug generators leave fingerprints (single-function edits, PR mirror = a known
    historical diff) → a miner can learn "revert the last function edit" priors; mix generators and use PR Mirror sparingly.
    Cost: ~$1.4k per 50k tasks (2025 prices); env images dominate storage.

1.7 R2E-Gym / AgentGym (Jain et al., 2025-04-09) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164
(1) 8.7k procedurally curated executable tasks via SWEGEN (commit back-translation → synthetic issue + test generation, no human
    issues/tests); 32B agent 34.4% pass@1 Verified; HYBRID test-time scaling: execution-based verifier (agent-written tests) and
    execution-free verifier (Qwen2.5-Coder-14B YES/NO on trajectory + patch) each saturate at 42–43% best@K; combined 51% best@26.
(2) Targets: env tests for training; at inference (a) generated tests (execution-based) and (b) a learned discriminator
    (execution-free). Finding: test-based verifiers have LOW DISTINGUISHABILITY (for most problems <~20% (mem) of generated tests
    separate the top correct from top incorrect patch); execution-free verifiers DISCRIMINATE better but are BIASED toward
    stylistic features and agent thoughts rather than the patch.
(3) Not capped for training (env truth). For selection, each verifier alone plateaus; hybrid raises the ceiling — evidence that
    two DIFFERENT error models are worth more than one better one.
(4) 26 trajectories/problem at inference; pass@26 ≫ best@26 (selection, not coverage, is the bottleneck — same as Monkeys §4.2).
(5)(a) Duel-time: an execution-free turn verifier alone will be gamed on style (our G/typicality leg already shows style dominance
    on the live board: reigns 16–19 crowned on typicality only, notes.md). Pair it with an executable leg (tests/continuation) and
    take min or require both — the R2E "hybrid" result is the empirical case for min(R, G, A)-style conjunction over legs with
    orthogonal failure modes. (b) Fold-time: SWEGEN-style commit back-translation = tasks from ANY commit, not only issue-linked
    PRs (SWE-rebench needs issue links) → more supply per repo. (c) Miner: hybrid best-of-N is how miners will pick submissions.
    Gaming: execution-free verifier → thought-styling; execution-based → overfit to the generated tests. Cost: EF verifier ≈ one
    echo/turn; EB ≈ test generation + 2 runs/candidate.

1.8 SWE-Bench Pro (Scale AI, 2025-09-21) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16941
(1) 1,865 long-horizon tasks from 41 repos: public set (11 GPL/copyleft repos), held-out set (12 repos, private, for overfitting
    checks against the public set), commercial set (18 purchased startup codebases; results released, code not). Human
    augmentation: requirements + interface spec grounded in tests; tests human-reviewed; flaky-test runs dropped. Frontier
    pass@1: public Claude Sonnet 4 42.7 / GPT-5-high 41.8; commercial Opus 4.1 17.8 / GPT-5-high 15.7 (a 2–2.5× gap = contamination
    + realism).
(2) Target = human-reviewed F2P/P2P tests; contamination resistance from LICENCE (copyleft excluded from commercial pretraining
    corpora) + ACCESS (private repos).
(3) Not model-capped; the point is a REFERENCE that stays honest because it is held out, not because it is hard.
(4) 161/500 SWE-bench Verified tasks are 1–2 line edits; Pro requires multi-file edits; public-vs-commercial gap is the cleanest
    published contamination estimate for agentic SWE.
(5)(a) Duel-time: not usable directly (fixed benchmark = Goodhart target), but the held-out-set design is the template for an
    OPERATOR-PRIVATE audit pool run on each new king (like our bench role today) — advisory, never in the ranked score.
    (b) Fold-time: prefer copyleft/less-crawled repos for D's renewal to reduce miner pretraining leakage; keep a rotating held-out
    repo set to measure "public-set overfit" of kings (public − heldout gap as a Goodhart telemetry).
    (c) Miner: nothing. Gaming: publishing the held-out results but not the tasks leaks ~nothing per round. Cost: human-verified
    tasks are expensive (Pro is a purchased dataset); use only for the audit pool.

1.9 SWE-rebench (Nebius, 2025-05-26) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20411  [discovered; renewing task supply + contamination]
(1) Fully automated pipeline: ~450k issue-linked PRs from 30k permissive-licence Python repos → LLM (Qwen2.5-72B) writes install
    recipes (≤3 candidates, error-log-driven repair) → execution verification (≥1 F2P fails before / all F2P pass after / P2P
    stable) → LLM quality labels (clarity, complexity, test correctness; 81% acc on complexity). Result: 21,336 tasks / 3,468
    repos; install works for ≥1 task in 31% of repos. A continuously refreshed 294-task/169-repo benchmark with issue-date vs
    model-release-date contamination flags.
(2) Target = repo tests (ground truth); freshness = tasks created after a model's release date.
(3) Not capped by any model; the supply RENEWS monthly, which is what makes it un-memorisable at the time of scoring.
(4) 21k tasks from 450k PRs (~5% yield); some models' SWE-bench Verified scores "might be inflated" vs fresh tasks.
(5)(a) Duel-time: fresh tasks minted between reveal and duel (or a private buffer released only at duel time) are the outcome-
    score's supply; their outcome is replayable because the env image + tests are published WITH the verdict (after the duel).
    (b) Fold-time: this IS the D renewal engine for coding (D already draws from `swerebench_v2`); the date-stamp lets the fold
    prefer tasks newer than the king's submission block for the audit/outcome slice. (c) Miner: can run the same pipeline; that is
    fine — the tasks in the duel are ones they have not seen. Gaming: repo-level priors (repo-specific memorisation) → cap tasks
    per repo (Pro caps 50–100/repo). Cost: pipeline is CPU + one 72B LLM pass per repo; per-task marginal cost ≈ cents.

PART 2 — SELF-PLAY / PROPOSER–SOLVER TASK GENERATION (why self-play can exceed a fixed teacher; what stops it in language)

2.1 Absolute Zero Reasoner (Zhao et al., 2025-05-06) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03335
(1) One model plays PROPOSER (writes code-reasoning tasks: deduction/abduction/induction over (program, input, output) triplets) and
    SOLVER; a Python EXECUTOR validates tasks and grades answers; zero external data. Qwen2.5-Coder-7B: coding SOTA among zero
    models, math +15.2 (coder) / +10.9 (base); gains scale 3B +5.7 → 7B +10.2 → 14B +13.2.
(2) Solver reward: executor correctness (ground truth). Proposer reward: LEARNABILITY = 1 − mean solver success over n Monte-Carlo
    rollouts, zero if all-solve or none-solve — i.e., tasks at the solver's frontier. Both produced by the model itself + executor.
(3) Not capped: the environment (executor) is the arbiter, and the task distribution moves with the solver (autocurriculum), so
    the optimum is bounded only by what the executor can verify. Still bounded to executor-checkable domains.
(4) TRR++ advantage estimator; buffer of past triplets seeds proposals; "uh-oh moment" (unsafe CoT in Llama-3.1-8B) — no filter on
    proposed content; token counts grow (trial-and-error in abduction).
(5)(a) Duel-time: not directly (the loop is training). (b) Fold-time: a PROPOSER seat — the teacher/king/frontier writes tasks whose
    learnability for the current king is ~0.5 (measured by k rollouts), executor-validated → D's math/code-reasoning share
    renews at the king's frontier automatically; this is a principled version of the king-failure seat (which uses a noisy 0/1
    outcome filter). (c) Miner: AZR-style self-play on executor-checkable tasks. Gaming: a miner who knows proposals come from a
    public proposer prompt can pre-train on its distribution — mitigated because tasks are fresh per fold and the executor, not the
    proposer, grades. Cost: n=8 solver rollouts per proposal to score learnability (~8× generation), cheap on small tasks.

2.2 R-Zero (Huang et al., 2025-08-07) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05004
(1) Two copies of one base: CHALLENGER (GRPO; reward = solver uncertainty 1 − 2|p̂ − 0.5| with p̂ = majority-agreement of 10 solver
    samples, + BLEU repetition penalty) and SOLVER (GRPO on challenger questions kept in the informative band 3 ≤ #majority ≤ 7 of
    10, pseudo-label = MAJORITY VOTE). Qwen3-4B-Base +6.49 math / +7.54 general (MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA); 8B 49.18 → 54.69 over 3
    iterations.
(2) Target: the solver's own majority vote (no executor). Producer: the solver.
(3) Claimed uncapped by external data — but the label is self-consensus, so the ceiling is "what the majority already gets right".
    Measured: pseudo-label accuracy vs GPT-4o oracle on challenger questions FALLS 79.0% → 69.0% → 63.0% over iterations 1–3
    (Table 5) — the curriculum gets harder faster than the labeler gets better; gains shrink per iteration. Appendix E: instability
    roots beyond label noise.
(4) 3 iterations; 10 samples/question; math domain chosen because answers are checkable by string match.
(5)(a) Duel-time: uncertainty-of-the-king as a TURN SELECTOR (not a score): sample the turn slice where king rollouts disagree
    (contested turns) — feasibility/outcome/report.txt already measures ~47% contested turns; scoring only contested turns
    concentrates SE budget where the crown can be decided. (b) Fold-time: R-Zero's band filter (30–70% success) is the cheap
    difficulty gate for king-failure data (replace "failed" with "solved 3–7 of 10"). (c) Miner: majority-vote self-training.
    Gaming when public: majority targets are collusion/mode-hugging targets (see §4.3–4.4); label accuracy decays → do NOT use
    majority as the ranked quantity. Cost: 10 rollouts/turn for the band.

2.3 SPIRAL (Liu et al., 2025-06-30) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.24119
(1) Self-play on two-player ZERO-SUM language games (TicTacToe, Kuhn Poker, Simple Negotiation) with a fully online multi-agent RL
    system + Role-conditioned Advantage Estimation (RAE). Transfers to reasoning: Qwen3-4B-Base 34.0 → 44.5 avg over 8 benchmarks
    (+10.5), 8B 39.5 → 49.6; beats SFT on 25k expert game trajectories; fixed-opponent RL (Mistral, Gemini) FAILS where self-play
    keeps improving.
(2) Reward: terminal game OUTCOME (rules), zero-sum; opponent = current self. No labels.
(3) Not capped: the opponent improves with the policy (autocurriculum), the rule-based outcome is exact; fixed opponents are
    exploited (static strategy overfit) — the same mechanism that makes a static judge/teacher a ceiling.
(4) Without RAE "thinking collapse" after ~200 steps (empty CoT); multi-game > single-game; instruction-tuned models gain less
    (+2.0 Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct).
(5)(a) Duel-time: the KOTH itself is a zero-sum game between king and challenger, but today the PAYOFF is teacher-measured, so
    the "opponent" never moves the target. A self-play-anchored payoff = the two miners' actions are compared by an OUTCOME the
    other cannot influence (env continuation, tests) → the target moves with the field, not with the teacher. (b) Fold-time:
    game-like envs (negotiation, hidden-info) are cheap renewable verifiable sources with exact rules. (c) Miner: self-play RL.
    Gaming: zero-sum with a rule outcome has no judge to fool; collusion (two miners sharing a coldkey throwing games) is the
    residual — pay by coldkey (notes.md already proposes). Cost: game rollouts are short (~10 turns).

2.4 SPIN — Self-Play Fine-Tuning (Chen et al., 2024-01-02) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01335
(1) Iterative DPO-like self-play: the new model learns to distinguish HUMAN SFT responses from its previous iteration's responses;
    the previous iteration is the opponent; no extra labels beyond the SFT set. zephyr-7b-sft-full 58.14 → ~63.2 (mem) Open LLM
    Leaderboard over 3 iterations on a 50k Ultrachat subset; MT-Bench 5.94 → ~6.78 (mem); beats DPO with extra GPT-4 preferences.
(2) Target: the human SFT data distribution. Theorem: global optimum ⇔ policy = target distribution — so the optimum is CAPPED at
    the demonstrator (converges when the discriminator cannot tell model from human).
(3) Not above a fixed target; it squeezes more out of a fixed target than SFT does (continued SFT plateaued/declined).
(4) 3 iterations; gains shrink each iteration.
(5) Mapping: SPIN is the formal statement of our situation — a distribution-matching objective's optimum is the reference
    distribution. Our R/G legs are teacher-distribution-matching legs ⇒ optimum = teacher, observed as the parity plateau
    (teacher-vs-king control z 12.9 → 1.3, notes.md). Replace the SFT set with FRONTIER samples and SPIN gives "match the
    frontier" — a higher ceiling but still a ceiling, and the frontier samples become RT-6 targets if published at fold time
    (feasibility rec: fresh per duel, $1.8–27/duel). Gaming: same as today (mode-hugging the reference).

2.5 Language Self-Play (Kuba et al., Meta, 2025-09-09) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07414
(1) One model as CHALLENGER (prompted to write hard instructions, minimises solver reward) and SOLVER (maximises); minimax with
    GRPO group trick; KL to reference stops adversarial nonsense; a QUALITY SELF-REWARD (reference model judging) is added to both
    players (game becomes non-zero-sum) because pure zero-sum degenerated. Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct on AlpacaEval vs base: Base
    26.5%, RL with data 38.8%, LSP-Zero 32.0%, LSP 36.4% — data-free ≈ data-backed RL.
(2) Reward: an external RM (OpenAssistant deberta RM in early runs) or verifier + self-reward. The RM was hacked ("answer everything
    in Python"). Producer: fixed RM + reference-model judge.
(3) Capped by the RM/judge — LSP demonstrates the ceiling and the hacking, not an escape from it. Its contribution is that a
    challenger can generate the PROMPT distribution for free.
(4) 3B model; instruction-following; no verifiable domain result reported beyond math/coding benchmarks in the abstract.
(5)(a) Duel-time: challenger-generated prompts are a renewable D source for chat/IF turns (our `text` dialect), but the score
    must not be the self-reward (hackable). (b) Fold-time: use LSP-style challengers to write instruction prefixes; grade with
    verifiable IF checks (IFEval-style constraints) not a judge. (c) Miner: same. Gaming: RM hacking demonstrated in-paper.

2.6 AlphaZero (Silver et al., 2017-12-05) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01815
(1) Tabula-rasa self-play + MCTS; single network updated continuously (no best-player gating, unlike AlphaGo Zero); chess surpassed
    Stockfish after 4 h (300k steps), shogi > Elmo <2 h, Go > AlphaGo Lee 8 h; 700k steps, 5,000 TPUv1 for self-play, 64 TPUv2
    for training; 100-game matches: 0 losses to Stockfish.
(2) Reward: game RESULT under the RULES — exact, un-gameable, unbounded above by any fixed player; opponent = self.
(3) THE canonical proof that self-play exceeds any fixed teacher: the target (winning) is defined by rules, not by a model; the
    curriculum is the improving self; search (MCTS) turns the current policy into a stronger "expert" whose moves are imitated
    (this amplification step is what a pure policy-gradient loop lacks).
(4) Massive compute; deterministic rules; perfect information; short horizons per decision; cheap simulators — none of which
    language agents have except partially (executors/tests).
(5) KOTH analogue: king vs challenger IS AlphaZero's evaluator match; what is missing is a rules-defined WIN. Language-domain
    blockers: (i) no exact rules for most turns → need executors/tests/consensus as rule surrogates; (ii) no cheap simulator →
    continuation rollouts are expensive; (iii) evaluation by a fixed model = fixed opponent = exploitable (SPIRAL). Debate (§3.2)
    is the attempt to turn a judged task into a rules-like game. Gaming: none where a rule grader exists. Cost: prohibitive at
    AlphaZero scale; the KOTH version only needs the WIN to be rules-defined per turn on a sample.

PART 3 — VERIFIER / JUDGE ROBUSTNESS, GOODHART, WEAK-TO-STRONG, SCALABLE OVERSIGHT

3.1 Prover-Verifier Games improve legibility (Kirchner et al., OpenAI, 2024-07-18) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13692
(1) Checkability training on GSM8K: iterate (verifier SL on solutions from all previous provers) → (prover RL, role-sampled
    HELPFUL = correct & convincing, SNEAKY = incorrect & convincing). Verifier robustness rises each round (sneaky needs more RL
    steps to find an exploit); helpful-prover accuracy rises; human time-constrained accuracy tracks the verifier's.
(2) Reward: ground-truth correctness (labels needed) + verifier acceptance; the verifier is SMALL (~10^-3–10^-2 of prover compute
    (mem)); verifier-leading Stackelberg equilibrium ⇔ perfectly legible protocol (Thm 1).
(3) Not above ground truth (needs labels) — its contribution is a VERIFIER that stays hard to fool under optimisation pressure
    because it is trained against the current best attacker every round. Too-small verifier (~10^-4×) is unstable; SAME-SIZE
    verifier fails to become robust (the sneaky prover "simulates the verifier's mistakes", citing Burns et al.). Legibility TAX:
    checkable provers are less accurate than correctness-only provers.
(4) Adversaries: sneaky-only, Goodharting (optimise prover on verifier score), CGC; robustness measured as steps-to-exploit and
    balanced best-of-n accuracy.
(5)(a) Duel-time: a judge leg is defensible ONLY if it is re-trained each epoch against the best current attacker (the king's own
    rollouts and adversarial "sneaky" variants) and is much SMALLER than the miners (so miners cannot simulate its errors) — the
    opposite of Albedo's frozen frontier judge. (b) Fold-time: the "sneaky prover" = deliberately wrong-but-plausible actions
    (from king failures or perturbed gold) as negatives for verifier training per fold. (c) Miner: miners become the sneaky
    prover by construction; the loop is adversarial by design. Gaming: Goodharting the verifier; measured cost to attacker rises
    with rounds. Cost: verifier training per fold on published rollouts (GPU-hours), scoring 1 echo/turn.

3.2 AI safety via debate (Irving, Christiano, Amodei, 2018-05-02) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00899
(1) Two agents argue for answers, a weaker judge decides; complexity analogy: debate with optimal play decides PSPACE with a
    polynomial judge (direct judging = NP). MNIST toy: sparse 6-pixel classifier 59.4% → 88.9% with debate (4 pixels 48.2% → 85.2%).
(2) Reward: judge's verdict, zero-sum between debaters; self-play finds approximate Nash. Producer: the (weak) judge.
(3) Above the judge's unaided ability, in theory unboundedly (the judge only checks the last step of a recursive argument); the
    AlphaZero analogy is explicit: a novice judges a Go debate by playing it out. Fails if lying is easier than refuting (§5.3 of
    the paper), if the judge is biased/persuadable, or if the equilibrium is unstable.
(4) Toy scale only in 2018; see Khan et al. for LLM evidence.
(5)(a) Duel-time: a DEBATE turn — king and challenger each propose an action; each gets one round to critique the other's action
    (with executable evidence: run a command, show a test); a small fixed judge (or the teacher) picks. Turns the ranked quantity
    into a zero-sum game the field plays against itself rather than against a fixed reference; replayable (store both transcripts).
    (b) Fold-time: critique transcripts are training data for the verifier. (c) Miner: train to argue/critique. Gaming: judge
    biases (§3.4/3.5) — persuasion ≠ truth; collusion between sides of one coldkey (throw the debate). Cost: 2 extra model calls
    + judge call per turn; on a 200-turn subset ≈ $2–5 with a flash-class judge.

3.3 Debating with More Persuasive LLMs Leads to More Truthful Answers (Khan et al., 2024-02-09) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06782
(1) Information-asymmetric QuALITY: expert debaters (GPT-4-Turbo, read the story, best-of-16, verified quotes) vs non-expert judges
    (no story). Debate: LLM judges 76%, humans 88% (naive 48% / 60%; consultancy 54% / 78%). Optimising debaters for PERSUASIVENESS
    (best-of-N, critique-and-refine, no labels) RAISES judge accuracy in debate and LOWERS it in consultancy; generalises to
    unseen judges; humans well calibrated.
(2) Target: judge approval (unsupervised); truth enters only through the adversary's ability to expose the weaker side with
    verified quotes.
(3) Weak judge exceeds its own knowledge (48 → 76) — evidence for scalable oversight ABOVE the judge; but the setting has a
    verifiable-quote tool (ground-truth evidence exists), and "human judges outperform LLM judges" (LLM judges are the weak link).
(4) >1,000 annotator hours; best-of-16 experts; no RL training of debaters.
(5) Mapping: strongest empirical case that a ZERO-SUM protocol with VERIFIABLE EVIDENCE lifts a weak judge above a fixed level —
    our agent turns have exactly such evidence (command outputs, tests). Gaming: consultancy (one-sided argument) gets WORSE with
    persuasiveness → never let one miner argue alone to a judge; always paired. Cost as §3.2.

3.4 Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena (Zheng et al., 2023-06-09) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685
(1) GPT-4 judge agrees with humans >80% (human-human level) but: POSITION bias (only GPT-4 consistent in >60% of swapped pairs;
    Claude-v1 name bias), VERBOSITY bias ("repetitive list" attack fools GPT-3.5/Claude-v1 in 91.3% of 23 answers; GPT-4 much less),
    SELF-ENHANCEMENT bias, weak math/reasoning grading (judge fails to see errors when it cannot solve).
(2) Target = judge preference; producer = a frozen frontier model.
(3) Capped at the judge and gameable along measurable axes.
(4) 3k expert + 3k crowd votes.
(5) The Albedo failure in general form: a frozen judge + a public rule = a fixed exploitable target. Mitigations (position swap,
    reference-guided grading, CoT judging) reduce but do not remove bias. For KOTH: judge legs only as re-trained gates; log the
    length/position telemetry; never as the ranked quantity.

3.5 Justice or Prejudice? CALM — 12 judge biases (Ye et al., 2024-10-03) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02736  [discovered]
(1) Automated attack-and-detect framework over 12 biases (position, verbosity, compassion-fade, bandwagon, distraction, fallacy
    oversight, authority, sentiment, diversity, chain-of-thought, self-enhancement, refinement-aware); robustness/consistency rates
    across 6 judges; advanced judges still fail on specific biases (e.g., fallacy oversight in math grading).
(5) Use as the checklist for any judge/verifier leg: run CALM-style perturbations on the leg before it goes live; publish the
    robustness rate with each fold. Directly relevant to the "in-band mimicry" (RT-13) watch item.

3.6 Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization (Gao, Schulman, Hilton, 2022-10-19) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760
(1) Synthetic gold RM (6B) labels data for proxy RMs (3M–3B); optimise policy against the proxy by BoN or RL; gold score rises then
    FALLS with KL distance d = sqrt(KL): BoN gold = d(α − βd), RL gold = d(α − β log d); coefficients scale smoothly (log) with
    proxy-RM size and data; larger policies gain less but overoptimise about the same; KL penalty did not improve the gold–KL
    frontier.
(2) Target = proxy RM (a fixed learned model of a fixed gold). Goodhart in closed form.
(3) Capped and then negative: optimising a fixed proxy past its trust region REDUCES true quality. This is the law behind Albedo's
    kings falling BELOW genesis on swe-rebench.
(4) BoN reaches the peak at small KL; RL is far less KL-efficient; ensembles help modestly (later work).
(5) Mapping: any FIXED learned leg (judge, EF verifier, even the teacher-echo legs once miners train on them) sits on this curve;
    the KOTH ratchet pushes each reign further out in KL from the reference → predicted gold decline after the peak (observed:
    kings at teacher parity on the meter while benchmarks plateau). Defences the law implies: re-fit the proxy each epoch on fresh
    gold (moving target), bound KL from a moving reference (δ does this weakly), or anchor on gold itself where gold is executable.

3.7 Weak-to-Strong Generalization (Burns et al., OpenAI, 2023-12-14) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09390
(1) Finetune a STRONG pretrained model on labels from a WEAK supervisor (GPT-2-level → GPT-4-level; 7 OOMs of compute) on NLP
    tasks, chess puzzles, and ChatGPT reward modeling. Naive: the student consistently BEATS the supervisor — NLP recovers ~50% of
    the weak→strong-ceiling gap (PGR ≈ 0.5); with an auxiliary CONFIDENCE loss (trust own predictions where confident) PGR ≈ 0.8
    (GPT-2-level supervisor → near GPT-3.5-level student); chess: bootstrapping through intermediate sizes helps; REWARD MODELING:
    PGR only ~10%, almost never >20%, even for small gaps.
(2) Target: weak labels (an imperfect fixed teacher). Producer: the weak model.
(3) Why the student exceeds the teacher: the strong model's pretrained representations make the TRUE concept more salient than
    the teacher's error pattern, so naive imitation generalises to the truth instead of the mistakes — as long as the errors are
    not easy to imitate. Students OVERFIT to weak labels with longer training (Fig. 7: imitation of supervisor mistakes); the
    confidence loss is a regulariser against imitating the teacher where the student is sure the teacher is wrong. Caveat: future
    superhuman students may find imitating the supervisor MORE salient ("imitation saliency" disanalogy).
(4) Gains largest where the task is salient in pretraining (NLP, chess), smallest where the target is a preference judgment (RM).
(5) IMPLICATIONS FOR A TEACHER-ANCHORED METER: (i) a student trained toward the teacher's labels can end above the teacher on the
    TRUE task — but our meter would score that student BELOW a perfect imitator, because the meter measures agreement with the
    teacher, not truth; the meter caps what the ratchet rewards, not what training can achieve. (ii) The regime where exceeding
    the teacher happens is "student disagrees confidently where the teacher is wrong" — exactly the turns our R/typicality legs
    penalise (out-of-band thought, low-likelihood action). (iii) Reward-modeling PGR ≈ 10% says a judge/preference leg is the
    worst place to hope for weak-to-strong lift. (iv) Practical: score DISAGREEMENT turns by an outcome/frontier anchor, and only
    AGREEMENT turns by the teacher — the AGENTS.md "contested/agreed" split (~47% contested) is where the lift would show.
    (b) Fold-time: weak labels (teacher outcome guesses) + confidence-weighted acceptance of king disagreements = the W2S
    "bootstrap" recipe for D. Gaming: none intrinsic; the risk is the opposite (a meter that punishes the W2S winner).

3.8 Measuring Progress on Scalable Oversight (Bowman et al., Anthropic, 2022-11-04) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03540
(1) SANDWICHING: pick tasks where the model beats non-experts but not experts; non-experts must align the model without expert help;
    experts only grade at the end. Baseline: humans chatting with an unreliable assistant beat the model alone by ~10 points on
    MMLU/timed QuALITY and their own unaided score by up to 36 points.
(2) Target: expert ground truth (held out for evaluation only).
(3) The paradigm for TESTING whether a weak-supervision protocol exceeds a fixed level: sandwich the meter between a weak signal
    (teacher) and an expert (frontier/outcome on held-out tasks).
(5) Mapping: the operator's frontier panel + env outcomes are the "experts"; the teacher-anchored duel is the non-expert protocol;
    the sandwiching test = does crowning by protocol X raise the expert-graded score of the king? That is the exact experiment the
    coordinator's outcome/ and panel/ artifacts run. Keep experts OUT of the ranked score to preserve the test.

3.9 LLM Critics Help Catch LLM Bugs — CriticGPT (McAleese et al., OpenAI, 2024-06-28) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00215
(1) RLHF-trained critic writes natural-language bug reports on ChatGPT code; on human-inserted bugs, critiques preferred over human
    contractor critiques in 63% of cases (>80% (mem) on inserted bugs), catches more bugs than paid reviewers; finds hundreds of
    errors in "flawless"-rated training data; hallucinated bugs are the failure mode; human+critic teams hallucinate less; FSBS
    (Force Sampling Beam Search) trades comprehensiveness vs nitpicks.
(2) Target: human preference over critiques + "tampering" labels (contractors insert bugs and record them = ground truth for
    recall). Producer: humans + tampering.
(3) Above the human reviewer on recall; capped by the critic's own hallucinations and by human preference for the critique.
(5)(a) Duel-time: a critic that must point to a CONCRETE, CHECKABLE defect in the opponent's action (a failing command, a
    reproduction) is the debate protocol's evidence layer; critiques without checkable evidence get zero. (b) Fold-time:
    tampering — insert known bugs into gold actions to create labelled negatives for verifier training (also §3.1's sneaky data).
    Gaming: nitpick spam / hallucinated bugs → require execution evidence. Cost: 1 critic call per contested turn.

PART 4 — CONSENSUS / MAJORITY AS A TARGET (does the model's own majority exceed the base? what breaks when public?)

4.1 Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2022-03-21) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171
(1) Sample many CoTs, majority-vote the final answer: PaLM-540B/GPT-3 GSM8K +17.9, SVAMP +11.0, AQuA +12.2, StrategyQA +6.4,
    ARC-c +3.9 absolute.
(2) Target: the model's own modal answer; producer: the model. (3) Exceeds greedy decoding of the SAME model (coverage of the
    correct mode), never exceeds the model's own pass@k; saturates with ~40 samples. (5) Mapping: majority-of-teacher is a
    higher-quality teacher label than one teacher sample (our k=3 refs already approximate it); a majority-of-FRONTIER-panel label
    is higher still — the panel's modal action is a cheaper "expert" than an outcome rollout. Gaming: known modal answer → hug the
    mode (RT-9 mode-guessing); safe only if the panel is fresh per duel.

4.2 Large Language Monkeys (Brown et al., 2024-07-31) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787
(1) Coverage (any-sample-correct) grows ~log-linearly over 4 orders of magnitude of samples (exponentiated power law); SWE-bench
    Lite DeepSeek-Coder-V2 15.9% @1 → 56% @250 (SOTA single-sample was 43%); CodeContests Gemma-2B 0.02% → 7.1% @10k. Without a
    verifier, MAJORITY VOTE and REWARD MODELS PLATEAU beyond a few hundred samples and stop tracking coverage.
(2) Target for the headline: unit tests / proof checkers (truth). For the negative result: majority/RM (proxies).
(3) A weak model + verifier exceeds a strong model's single sample; a weak model + majority does not scale past its own mode.
(5) Mapping: PRECISION is the binding constraint — an outcome/test verifier converts sampling into capability, a consensus target
    does not. For the KOTH: a king with a verifier-in-the-loop harness beats a smarter king without one on outcome; this argues
    for scoring OUTCOMES of whole (short) continuations rather than single-step likelihoods. Cost: verifier runs dominate.

4.3 TTRL — Test-Time RL (Zuo et al., 2025-04-22) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16084
(1) RL on UNLABELED test prompts with reward = agreement with the MAJORITY answer of the model's own samples. Qwen2.5-Math-7B AIME
    2024 16.7 → 43.3 (+211% rel.), avg +~50% rel. over AIME/AMC/MATH-500/GPQA; final pass@1 SURPASSES the initial model's maj@n
    "upper bound" and approaches RL with ground-truth labels.
(2) Target: self-majority (pseudo-label). Why it works despite wrong labels: "LUCKY HIT" — when the majority is wrong, most rollouts
    that disagree with it are ALSO wrong, so the 0-reward they receive is still correct; reward accuracy stays high even when label
    accuracy is low (Fig. 9). RL tolerates reward noise more than SFT.
(3) It exceeds the base's maj@n because training sharpens the distribution AND improves it (the majority moves); but it is bounded
    by the prior — §4.3 failures: LLaMA-3.1-8B on AIME ≈ no gain ("lack of prior knowledge on target task"), sensitive to
    temperature/batch, collapse risk.
(4) Small unlabeled sets (30–500 problems); many episodes; math only.
(5)(a) Duel-time: a "panel-majority" turn score (frontier panel or king+challenger+teacher pooled) is TTRL's reward — cheap and
    replayable if the samples are stored — but when PUBLIC the majority is a collusion target (a coldkey running king AND
    challenger shapes the majority) and a mode-hugging target; TTRL's own failure mode (no prior → no signal) is the "kings at
    0/25" regime. Use majority only over FRESH frontier samples, never over the contestants. (b) Fold-time: pseudo-labelling
    unlabeled turns by frontier majority gives D outcome-like labels at ~$0.5–2 per 1000 turns (deepseek/flash prices,
    feasibility). (c) Miner: TTRL on D is a free capability lift (and is what miners will do). Cost: n samples per turn.

4.4 Self-Rewarding Language Models (Yuan et al., Meta, 2024-01-18) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10020
(1) Llama-2-70B judges its own responses (LLM-as-a-Judge prompt), builds preference pairs, iterative DPO; AlpacaEval 2.0 LC win rate
    9.94 → 15.38 → 20.44 over M1→M3 (beats Claude 2, Gemini Pro, GPT-4-0613 at the time); reward-modeling ability also improves
    with iterations.
(2) Target: self-judgment. (3) Rises for 3 iterations because both generator and judge improve; authors expect saturation; no
    ground-truth domain — the gains are on a judge-graded benchmark (partly self-referential). Length grows (mem).
(5) Mapping: co-evolving judge > frozen judge (same lesson as Kimi K2 closed loop, Intuitor online), but a self-judge is the
    ultimate self-preference target — publicly, a miner that is its own judge maximises its own bias. Use only as a data
    filter with an external gate, never as a ranked quantity.

4.5 Intuitor — RL from Internal Feedback (Zhao et al., 2025-05-26) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19590
(1) GRPO with reward = SELF-CERTAINTY (mean KL of the token distribution from uniform); Qwen2.5-3B on MATH matches GRPO with gold
    answers in-domain and generalises better OOD (LiveCodeBench, CRUXEval-O). KEY ABLATION §5.4: OFFLINE self-certainty (from a
    FIXED base model) is hacked at ~step 100 (the policy appends an already-solved problem to inflate confidence; length spikes,
    accuracy collapses); ONLINE self-certainty (from the evolving policy) is stable.
(2) Target: own confidence. (3) Not above the prior; sharpening only.
(5) Mapping: the offline/online ablation is the cleanest micro-demonstration that ANY fixed scorer (a frozen teacher's likelihood
    included) gets exploited by appended content, while a scorer that moves with the policy resists — our G/typicality band is a
    frozen-scorer leg; centering + band close the known flat-lift attack, but the law says the next appended-content exploit is a
    matter of time. Confidence-style legs must be OFF the ranked quantity.

4.6 RENT — RL via Entropy Minimization (Prabhudesai et al., 2025-05-28) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22660  and
    The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Entropy Minimization (Agarwal et al., 2025-05-21) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15134
(1) Reward = negative token entropy (RENT: last-token / answer-span entropy most predictive); EM-RL on Qwen-7B matches GRPO/RLOO
    trained on 60k labeled examples; EM-INF (inference-time logit sharpening) lets Qwen-32B match/exceed GPT-4o/Claude-3-Opus on
    SciCode (mem, from abstract). Limitations sections: gains depend on the base being "confidently correct"; Llama-3.1-8B gains
    much smaller.
(2) Target: own entropy. (3) Sharpening the prior — cannot exceed what the prior already ranks first.
(5) Mapping: entropy legs are trivially gameable when public (emit deterministic filler) and add nothing above the prior.

4.7 Spurious Rewards: Rethinking Training Signals in RLVR (Shao et al., 2025-06-12) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10947  [discovered]
(1) Qwen2.5-Math-7B MATH-500 improves +21.4 with RANDOM rewards vs +29.1 with ground truth (also with incorrect labels, format-only
    rewards); mechanism = GRPO clipping bias amplifying high-prior behaviours (code reasoning); Llama3/OLMo2 get ~nothing or
    degrade.
(3) Devastating for "consensus/self" targets as evidence of capability: large gains from contentless rewards are model-specific
    prior elicitation, not learning.
(5) Mapping: any turn score whose optimiser could be satisfied by "elicit the base model's priors harder" (entropy, confidence,
    self-majority, typicality-to-a-fixed-teacher) will show benchmark gains at first and then plateau at the prior — which is the
    observed live board. Require the anchor to contain information the prior does not (env outcome, frontier action, fresh task).

PART 5 — DATA-CENTRIC RATCHETS (amplification, expert iteration, STaR/ReST, rejection sampling at scale, difficulty curricula)
==========================================================================================================================

5.1 Iterated Distillation and Amplification (Christiano, Shlegeris, Amodei, 2018-10-19) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08575
(1) Train a model to imitate AMPLIFY(H, model) — a human/algorithm decomposing a task into subtasks answered by the current model;
    then distil; iterate. No external reward function ("closely related to Expert Iteration except it uses no external reward").
    Results on algorithmic tasks (permutation powering, shortest path, wildcard search, …) match supervised baselines with only
    decomposition supervision.
(3) Exceeds the fixed overseer because the overseer + the current model, given more compute/decomposition, is a stronger target
    than the overseer alone; the target moves each iteration (an "amplified" teacher).
(5) Mapping: an AMPLIFIED TEACHER as the anchor — teacher with tools/search/multiple samples/critique (best-of-k with a test
    runner, or teacher continuing the trajectory and grading) is a higher-ceiling reference than one greedy teacher sample, and
    can be re-amplified each epoch. Cost: k× teacher compute per turn; replayable if samples stored. Gaming: still a reference-
    matching target (mode-hugging), but the mode moves.

5.2 Expert Iteration (Anthony, Tian, Barber, 2017-05-23) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439
(1) Apprentice (NN) imitates an EXPERT (MCTS guided by the apprentice); expert improves as apprentice improves; Hex 9×9: beats
    MoHex (Computer Olympiad champion since 2009) tabula rasa; imitation-learning framing where "the best known experts are unable
    to achieve satisfactory performance".
(3) Not capped by the initial expert because the expert = search over the apprentice; ceiling is the game's rule outcome.
(5) Mapping: same as 5.1 with search = sampling + verifier. For coding turns "expert" = teacher/king × k samples × test runner;
    the imitation target is the VERIFIED best sample. Fold-time this is the "hybrid verifier best@k" label per state. Cost k×.

5.3 STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022-03-28) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14465
(1) Generate rationales; keep those that reach the correct answer; for failures, RATIONALIZE given the answer (hint) and keep;
    fine-tune from the base each iteration; GPT-J matches a 30× larger model on CommonsenseQA; GSM8K gains.
(2) Target: gold answers (labels required); rationales self-generated. (3) Bounded by label availability and by the base's
    pass@k (rationalization extends coverage); plateaus when no new correct rationales appear.
(5) Mapping: rationalization = "given the frontier/outcome-verified action, write the thought" — a fold-time way to generate
    THOUGHTS for D's frontier-anchored turns without a frontier thought (frontier APIs hide CoT). Gaming: n/a (data). Cost: 1 call
    per turn.

5.4 ReST (Gulcehre et al., 2023-08-17) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08998  and  ReST-EM (Singh et al., 2023-12-11) —
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06585
(1) Grow (sample) / Improve (filter by reward, fine-tune) loops. ReST: MT with a learned reward model; multiple Improve steps
    raise RM score but the RM is a proxy (human eval gains smaller). ReST-EM: binary VERIFIABLE feedback (MATH, APPS) on PaLM-2;
    self-generated data beats fine-tuning on human data, scales with model size, improves pass@k and majority vote, transfers to
    held-out tasks — but "exceeding a couple of iterations leads to diminishing improvement, indicating potential overfitting on
    small amount of training problems".
(3) Uncapped only with verifiable rewards AND a large fresh problem supply; with a learned RM it is the Gao curve; with a small
    problem set it overfits after ~2–3 iterations.
(5) Mapping: the iteration cap is a TASK-SUPPLY cap → every ratchet needs renewing tasks (Part 1). Fold-time: ReST-EM with env
    grading on fresh SWE-smith/rebench tasks is the D ratchet; keep the set growing, re-fine-tune from base.

5.5 Llama 3 post-training (Meta, 2024-07-31) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783
(1) Six rounds of rejection sampling (10–30 samples/prompt, RM picks best) + SFT + DPO; code: a 1T-token code expert used to
    annotate; ~1M synthetic coding dialogues with EXECUTION FEEDBACK because "training Llama 3 405B on its own generated data is
    not helpful (and can even degrade performance)" — static analysis + unit tests filter, error-correction rounds; "learning from
    feedback and mistakes" for reasoning.
(2) Targets: RM (preference) for chat; execution for code; the model's own samples otherwise.
(3) The 405B self-distillation failure is the industry-scale statement that a model cannot lift itself on its own samples without
    an external truth source; execution feedback restored the gain.
(5) Mapping: rejection sampling under an RM = Gao curve; under execution = ratchet. For KOTH: the king's own rollouts are useless
    as D unless graded by something the king does not control (env, frontier). Cost: 10–30 samples/prompt.

5.6 DART-Math — Difficulty-Aware Rejection Tuning (Tong et al., 2024-06-18) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13690
(1) Vanilla rejection tuning is biased to EASY queries (hard ones yield zero correct samples); DART allocates more trials to hard
    queries (Uniform: same #correct per query; Prop2Diff: proportional to difficulty); 590k examples synthesised with a 7B
    open model (DeepSeekMath-7B-RL), no GPT-4; DART-Math-Llama3-70B ~53–55% MATH (mem for exact), beating VRT at equal size.
(3) Better use of a fixed verifier by spending sampling budget where the model fails — the RT-6/DAgger idea in sampling form.
(5) Mapping: fold-time SAMPLING policy for D — spend teacher/frontier/outcome budget on turns where the king fails or the panel is
    contested (the ~47% contested turns), not uniformly; duel-time: weight or restrict the slice to contested/hard strata (fixed
    n → more SE where it matters). Cost: k× more samples on hard strata.

5.7 Self-Evolving Curriculum (Chen et al., 2025-05-20) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14970
(1) Curriculum as a non-stationary multi-armed bandit over problem categories; reward = |advantage| (proxy for learning gain);
    TD(0) update; beats random curriculum on Countdown/Zebra/math; a reverse (hard→easy) curriculum badly hurts generalisation.
(5) Mapping: the fold's `[mix]` shares could be set by a bandit on king learning signal (e.g., variance of king outcomes per
    stratum) instead of fixed targets; duel-time n/a.

(2) Target: frozen frontier judge + its own reference. (3) Capped and inverted (Gao curve past the peak): kings optimised checklist

6.2 Affine SN120's own board (AGENTS.md §3b, notes.md): teacher-anchored likelihood legs → live Spearman(duel margin, swe_lite)
    −0.42 (n=29, p=0.024); three S-crowned kings 0/25; later kings converge to the teacher's shape (typicality-only crowns, teacher-

    Held-out benchmarks did move on the IF/general axis and the τ² ask axis after a rule change (wvk-22), coding/terminal did not.

6.3 Templar / Gauntlet — Incentivizing Permissionless Distributed Learning of LLMs (2025-05-27) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21684,
    docs https://docs.tplr.ai/
(1) Bittensor incentive for distributed PRE-TRAINING: peers submit DeMo pseudo-gradients; validator scores LossScore = loss before −
    loss after applying a peer's gradient (scaled step) on a random data subset; OpenSkill ratings over sampled peers per round
    (raw loss scores are noisy across iterates, RANKS are stable); Sync Score (uptime/synchronisation); UNIQUENESS: peers are
    assigned data; a compliant peer has lower loss on its assigned subset than on a random one, which detects copying ("Peer
    Copying — copy another's pseudo-gradient and post it before the window closes") and free-riding. Trained a 1.2B LLM
    permissionlessly.
(2) Target: held-out loss decrease (ground truth for the training objective), fresh random subset per evaluation.
(3) Not capped by a model; the ratchet is the global model's loss.
(5) Lessons for KOTH: (i) rank-based ratings smooth a noisy per-round score (our z-test is per duel; an OpenSkill-style rating
    across duels would dampen winner's-curse churn); (ii) copy-attack detection by ASSIGNED work — e.g., each miner must show
    lower error on a private subset assigned at reveal → a king-copy has no such asymmetry; (iii) score with a fresh random
    subset each round, never a fixed set. Cost: validator-side evaluation dominates (as ours).

6.4 Taoshi / Proprietary Trading Network (SN8) — https://github.com/taoshidev/proprietary-trading-network (README, fetched 2026-09-20)
(1) Miners submit trade signals; validators track portfolio returns; rewards by risk-adjusted return under hard drawdown limits (5%
    intraday, 8% end-of-day from high-water mark → ELIMINATION); PLAGIARISM elimination — an order-uniqueness analysis detects
    miners copying other miners' trades; probation; inactivity elimination; debt-based weekly payout.
(2) Target: realised market returns (exogenous ground truth, unforgeable, renewing every day).
(3) Not capped; the market is the arbiter.
(5) Lessons: exogenous, time-stamped outcomes + explicit COPY detection + hard risk gates. For KOTH: our analogue of "market" is
    env outcome on tasks minted after reveal; copy detection = weight/tensor fingerprint gate (notes.md "tensor-fingerprint gate
    on") plus behavioural fingerprint (action-level similarity to the king on the slice).

6.5 Numerai tournament — https://docs.numer.ai/numerai-tournament/scoring (fetched 2026-09-20)
(1) Weekly predictions on obfuscated features; payouts on CORR (correlation with a 20/60-day realised target) and MMC = covariance
    with the target AFTER neutralising the submission against the stake-weighted META MODEL (only the ORTHOGONAL component is
    paid); staking with burn on negative scores; reputation = trailing 1-year stake-weighted average; permanent round history.
(2) Target: realised returns (exogenous, resolves weeks later); MMC anchors on the field's consensus but pays only what the
    consensus does not already have.
(3) Uncapped (market truth); MMC explicitly makes "copy the meta model" worth ZERO.
(5) Lessons for KOTH: (i) an MMC-style leg — pay the challenger only for the component of its action distribution ORTHOGONAL to
    the king's/teacher's that correlates with outcome — kills king-copy and teacher-mimicry in one stroke (a perfect teacher
    imitator has zero MMC); (ii) delayed resolution + staking with burn punishes lucky/noisy wins (winner's-curse fix); (iii)
    scores are replayable from the published predictions + the realised target.

6.6 Proper scoring rules — Gneiting & Raftery 2007 (JASA 102:359–378, doi:10.1198/016214506000001437), summary fetched from
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoring_rule; peer prediction / Bayesian Truth Serum from memory (Miller, Resnick & Zeckhauser
    2005 Mgmt Sci; Prelec 2004 Science 306:462)
(1) A STRICTLY PROPER rule (log score, Brier) is maximised in expectation only by reporting one's true belief — but only when the
    outcome RESOLVES independently of the reports. Peer-prediction rules (score a report by how well it predicts OTHER reporters'
    reports) are incentive-compatible for private information WITHOUT ground truth only under common-prior assumptions and are
    known to have uninformative colluding equilibria (everyone reports the same thing).
(5) Mapping: our logprob legs are log scores of the TEACHER's belief about the miner's text — not a proper score of the MINER's
    belief about anything that resolves. A proper-scoring turn leg would ask the miner for a probabilistic claim that resolves
    (e.g., "P(this test passes after my edit)") and log-score it on execution. Consensus targets (§4.3, §4.4) are peer-prediction
    rules and inherit the colluding equilibrium — with one coldkey holding both seats (reigns 17–19 were one coldkey), the
    equilibrium is reachable today.

CROSS-CUTTING CONCLUSIONS (≤ 25 lines)
==========================================================================================================================
1. Anchors whose optimum is ABOVE a fixed teacher: (i) executable/environment outcome on renewing tasks (DeepSWE, Kimi-Dev,
   SWE-Gym/smith/rebench, ReST-EM, Llama-3 code); (ii) rules-defined zero-sum self-play (AlphaZero, SPIRAL — fixed opponents get
   exploited, self-play does not); (iii) debate/prover-verifier with verifiable evidence (Khan: weak judge 48→76%, humans 60→88%);
   (iv) amplified/moving references (IDA, ExIt, Kimi K2's re-grounded critic, Intuitor's ONLINE scorer); (v) fresh frontier
   samples (higher ceiling, still a ceiling — SPIN's theorem: a distribution-matching objective converges TO the reference).
2. Anchors whose optimum is AT OR BELOW the prior: self-majority (TTRL — bounded by the prior; R-Zero label accuracy 79→69→63%),
   self-judge (Yuan), confidence/entropy (Intuitor/RENT/EM), similarity to a fixed sample set (SWE-RL, SPIN, our R/G legs).
   Spurious-rewards shows +21.4 MATH from random rewards on Qwen — "gains" from contentless anchors are prior elicitation.
3. Public-target gaming: any FIXED learned scorer sits on Gao's overoptimisation curve (gold rises then falls with KL) — Albedo
   (judge-crowned kings 11–38 vs genesis 58) and SN120's live inversion (ρ = −0.42) are both on the falling side. Resistant
   designs share one property: the target is either exogenous and fresh (tests on post-reveal tasks; realised returns in
   Taoshi/Numerai; random held-out subsets in Templar) or moves with the field (self-play, re-trained verifiers, online critics).
   Consensus over the CONTESTANTS is a peer-prediction rule with a colluding equilibrium; one coldkey already held both seats.
4. Verifier hygiene from the literature: small re-trained verifiers resist Goodhart better than same-size ones (PVG); execution-free
   verifiers key on style/thoughts and saturate (R2E 42–43%), execution-based ones lack distinguishability — the HYBRID (51%) is the
   case for conjunctions of legs with orthogonal failure modes; run CALM-style bias attacks before any judge leg ships.
5. Verifiable-task supply is solved at ~cents/task and renews: SWE-smith 50k tasks/128 repos for $1.36k; SWE-rebench 21k tasks from
   450k PRs fully automated with date stamps; R2E-Gym from arbitrary commits; Kimi-K2 simulator+rubric for tool-use; AZR/R-Zero
   proposers for executor-checkable reasoning at the king's ~50% frontier. The binding costs are containers per scored action and
   continuation tokens, not tasks — so outcome anchoring fits ~1 h/$10s only on a SUBSET of turns (short remaining horizon or
   contested states, ~47% of states are contested) with a fixed public continuation policy shared by both sides.
6. Weak-to-strong implies: a student trained toward the teacher can end above it on truth (PGR ≈ 0.5 naive, ≈ 0.8 with a confidence
   loss) precisely by disagreeing confidently where the teacher is wrong — the turns a teacher-agreement meter penalises. A teacher-
   anchored meter therefore caps the RATCHET, not the miners; the fix is to score DISAGREEMENT turns by an outcome/frontier/debate
   anchor and reserve teacher agreement for the agreed set. PGR ≈ 10% for reward modelling says a preference/judge leg is the worst
   place to expect lift. Sandwiching (Bowman) is the test: does the protocol raise the held-out expert score of the crowned king?
7. Mechanism-design transfers: pay only the component ORTHOGONAL to the incumbent/teacher that correlates with outcome (Numerai
   MMC — makes copying worth zero); rank-based ratings across duels (Templar OpenSkill) against winner's-curse churn; delayed
   resolution with staking/burn; assigned-work asymmetry as copy detection; proper log scores only on claims that RESOLVE.
