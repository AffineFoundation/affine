per-step value estimation for LM agents / thought imitation / simulated execution as verification
Read 2026-09-21 from the primary pages (arXiv abs/HTML/PDF, OpenReview, NeurIPS proceedings).
Numbers are the papers' own; "[mem]" = recalled, page not re-fetched. Companion sweeps: A_*.txt
(distillation / PI self-teachers / PRMs / expert iteration) and B_*.txt (self-play / verifiers /

Setting (AGENTS.md §1–2; store doc dataset-rule-solutions.md §0–1, §3g, §3i, §6b.5, §7):
turn = min(R, G[, A]) under teacher-forced logprobs of a FROZEN teacher T = Qwen3.8-27B; maximiser
of every leg is T; kings sit at T parity. Design constraints: one published rule, computable from
offline data (frontier calls, T samples, stored trajectories with outcomes, containers where a
grader exists), replayable, not gameable by filler / parroting / splicing / generic exploration.
Failure modes to design around (this week's measurements):
  F1 per-turn similarity to a frontier sample — sampling noise; frontier not right where it disagrees.
  F2 hindsight-informed T refs — thought leg: informed thoughts fall outside the typicality band;
     action leg: measures "did you follow the hint's plan", generic exploration wins (§3g, §3i).
  F3 routed pool of other models' refs — splice (T thought + member action) wins.
  F4 frontier as preference judge — 42 % accuracy vs env outcome.
  F5 frontier deliberated consensus k=10 — confident and wrong; modal decision = T's failed
     decision at 9/11 states (§6b.5).
  F6 verified action tables from N=3–4 continuations — right shape, too sparse.
Survivors: frontier PROPOSALS verified by T's own continuation add correct actions above T's
ceiling at 27 % of stuck states; verified-solved T thoughts sit inside the typicality band.
Notation: x prefix; z thought; y action; T(.|x) blind teacher; T(.|x,p) teacher with privileged
context p; Q^T(x,a) = value (solve rate) of taking a at x then letting T finish; V^T(x)=E_a~T Q^T(x,a);
"duel-time" = per-turn work on the eval pod (1300 turns/duel); "fold-time" = building D / the
reference fields attached to D.

PART I — THE IMITATION GAP AND PRIVILEGED TEACHERS
======================================================================================================

1. Weihs, Jayaraman, Sun, Schwing, Kembhavi — "Bridging the Imitation Gap by Adaptive Insubordination"
   (ADVISOR), NeurIPS 2021. https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12173 (v1 Jul 2020, camera-ready 2021)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Teacher pi^teach sees state s; student sees o=f(s). Proposition 1 (policy averaging):
the BC minimiser is pi_f(o) = E[pi^teach(s) | f(s)=o] — the teacher averaged over all states that
look alike to the student — which can be arbitrarily bad (PoisonedDoors: teacher knows the safe
door, student learns "open a random door" and is poisoned). ADVISOR trains an AUXILIARY student
pi^aux_f by pure imitation, uses its per-state distance to the teacher as the gap estimator, and
weights the loss L^ADV = E[ w(s)·CE(pi^teach(s), pi_f(s)) + (1−w(s))·L_RL ], w(s)=exp(−alpha·d(pi^teach,
pi^aux_f)(s)). Imitate where the gap is small; learn from reward where it is large.
(2) Signal. Teacher action distribution (privileged); env reward; gap estimate produced by a
learner-side auxiliary net trained on the same data — no extra oracle.
(3) Above the expert? The expert IS optimal given privilege; the student's optimum is the best
f-restricted policy, which imitation cannot reach (Prop. 1) but RL can where the gap is large.
Framing for us: our T is NOT privileged relative to the miner (same x), so the classic gap is zero;
the gap re-appears exactly when we hand T hindsight (F2) — informed refs are a privileged teacher,
and scoring "match the informed teacher" is Prop. 1's failure in reverse (the miner can only learn
the average of hindsight-conditioned actions over hidden hindsight = a generic hedge).
(4) Numbers. 13 tasks (PoisonedDoors, Lighthouse, 8 MiniGrid incl. corrupted/faulty-switch experts,
PointNav, ObjectNav, CoopNav); ADVISOR-based routines "perform as well or better" than BC, DAgger,
BC→PPO, BC+PPO, PPO on every task (Tab. 1–2); central empirical claim "smaller imitation gap ⇒
better performance"; still works when the expert is corrupted near the goal (LC/WC Corrupt Expert).
(5) Mapping. ADVISOR prescribes a GATE, not a target: only imitate the (informed) reference where an
un-informed learner could have matched it; elsewhere score outcome. Dataset field g(x) = distance
between the blind teacher's action distribution T(.|x) and the informed one T(.|x,p) (both from
fold-time samples, k=3..10 each; JS over canonicalised actions, or the per-byte lp gap). Ranking
term: turn = w(x)·min(R,G) + (1−w(x))·[verified-value term, see §I.4/§III], w=exp(−alpha·g). Avoids
F2 (informed refs never become the target; they only mark where T is unreliable); does not touch F1,
F3, F5. Replayable (g is a stored number). Cost: fold +k informed samples per decision state (already
produced for §3g/§3i); duel 0 extra if the second term is a stored table. Re-opens: nothing new on
the imitation half; whatever the value half re-opens.

2a. Chen, Zhou, Koltun, Krähenbühl — "Learning by Cheating", CoRL 2019. https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12294
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Stage 1: a privileged agent (ground-truth BEV map, all traffic) is trained by BC on
human demos. Stage 2: the sensorimotor agent is trained by imitating the PRIVILEGED AGENT, which is a
white box: it can be queried for the correct action at ANY state (all navigation commands at once)
and on the student's own on-policy states (DAgger without a human).
(2) Signal. Privileged agent's waypoints/actions at student-visited states; producer = the cheating
agent (cheap, deterministic, queryable). No reward.
(3) Above the expert? No — the student is bounded by the privileged agent; the gain is over
one-stage BC (decomposition of perception from planning + dense on-policy labels).
(4) Numbers. 100 % success on all tasks of the original CARLA benchmark; new NoCrash record;
infractions down an order of magnitude vs prior SOTA. Ablation: on-policy + all-command supervision
are the two big contributors.
(5) Mapping. LBC's usable lesson is "white-box, queryable, on-policy" — a reference that can be
asked at the MINER's states, not just T's. Our D already does this for king failures (king seat).
The trap it walks into is Prop. 1 of ADVISOR the moment the privileged agent's decision depends on
information the student cannot see (they get away with it because the BEV map is inferable from
pixels). For us: a hindsight-informed reference is only admissible where the hint is inferable from x
(= ADVISOR gate). No per-turn term of its own; F2 stays open if used raw.

2b. Shenfeld, Hong, Tamar, Agrawal — "TGRL: An Algorithm for Teacher Guided Reinforcement Learning",
    ICML 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03186
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Objective r_task + lambda·(teacher-imitation reward) with lambda adapted online:
maintain a COUNTERFACTUAL policy trained on task reward only (same data), compare its performance to
the main policy; if the guided policy is not ahead, decay lambda (stop trusting the teacher). Cast as
constrained optimisation; no per-task tuning.
(2) Signal. Privileged/suboptimal teacher actions + task reward; the trust weight comes from a
performance COMPARISON, not from a state-wise distance (contrast ADVISOR).
(3) Above the expert? Yes when the teacher is privileged or suboptimal: the task-reward term takes
over once imitation stops paying; paper notes ADVISOR fails where the student must deviate early to
gather information the teacher never needed.
(4) Numbers. In-hand reorientation (privileged teacher, student without object pose): TS 54 % →
TGRL 73 % success; wins/ties on MiniGrid, Shadow-Hand, Trifinger vs ADVISOR/PBRS/COSIL without
hyperparameter search.
(5) Mapping. TGRL says the trust in a reference should be set by whether following it is
OUTCOME-competitive, measured on the same states. Dataset field: at each decision state with a
grader, solve rate of T's blind continuation (V^T(x)) vs solve rate of the informed/proposal action's
continuation (Q^T(x,a*)); the reference at x is admitted only if Q^T(x,a*) − V^T(x) > margin at N
continuations. That is the survivor ("proposals verified by T's continuation") stated as a rule:
verification, not deliberation, decides admission → avoids F4/F5 by construction; F6 (sparsity) is the
cost. Replayable (table). Cost fold: N×(candidates) continuations per decision state; duel 0.

2c. Lambert, Sicilia, ... — "Privileged Information Dropout in Reinforcement Learning", 2020.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09220
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) PI conditions multiplicative (Gaussian) dropout noise on the policy's features during training;
at test the PI is marginalised out. Compared to distillation and PI auxiliary heads.
(2) Signal: privileged observation used only as a regulariser, never as a target.
(3) Not above the expert; there is no expert. Sample-efficiency gain in a partially observed maze.
(4) Beats distillation and aux-task baselines on the partially observed task [figures, no headline %].
(5) Mapping: miner-side training trick only; nothing for a scoring rule. Confirms the general theme
of Part I: PI is safe as CONDITIONING FOR AN ESTIMATOR, unsafe as a TARGET.

2d. Walsman, Zhang, Choudhury, Fox, Farhadi — "Impossibly Good Experts and How to Follow Them",
    ICLR 2023. https://openreview.net/pdf?id=sciA_xgYofB
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) "Impossibly good" expert = one whose long-run return relies on PI no learner can reproduce.
ELF Distillation: (a) FOLLOWER = student distilled from the expert (bounded by the averaged
expert, Prop. 1 again); (b) EXPLORER = RL agent whose reward is shaped by the follower's VALUE
function V^follower — the follower's value tells the explorer how much an information-gathering
detour is worth.
(2) Signal: expert actions → follower; follower's learned V → potential-based shaping for the
explorer; env reward.
(3) Above the follower, by design; above the expert only in the observation-restricted sense (it
learns to gather information the expert never needed).
(4) Beats ADVISOR/BC/PPO on Minigrid + Vizdoom PI tasks [figures].
(5) Mapping: the reusable object is "use the imitator's VALUE, not the expert's action, as the
dense signal". For us the "follower" is the teacher itself and its value V^T(x) is measurable by
continuation. A per-turn potential term Phi = V^T(x_{t+1}) − V^T(x_t) (progress under the teacher's
own play from the state the miner's action produces) is a shaped reward that does not require the
miner to match anybody. Needs the next observation → containers at fold, or a simulator (Part IV).
Avoids F1–F5 (no reference matching at all); inherits F6.

2e. Two 2025–26 follow-ups on privileged CRITICS (discovered):
   Venkatraman, Dinot, Aitchison — "Le Critique: Privileged Value Functions for LLM Reinforcement
   Learning", 17 Aug 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16739
   (authors not recorded) — "Informed Asymmetric Actor-Critic: Leveraging
   Privileged Signals Beyond Full-State Access", Sep 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26000
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Le Critique: the critic V_phi(h_t, z_t) conditions on the policy's token history PLUS training-
only context z_t — the REFERENCE SOLUTION, the verifier rubric, or the other K−1 leave-one-out
sibling rollouts and their rewards; policy unchanged; advantage A = R − V_phi. Admissibility condition:
z ⟂ y_t | h_t (z must not encode the current token). TETHER = adaptive linear mix of the LOO group
baseline and the token-level value, rho fit by least squares per batch. Informed-AAC: any
state-dependent privileged signal (not only full state) yields an unbiased policy gradient; proposes
two informativeness tests (HSCIC dependence before training; return-prediction-error after).
(2) Signal: reference solutions / sibling outcomes / latent state → critic only.
(3) Above the expert: n/a (RL with a verifier); the point is that PI goes to the ESTIMATOR of value,
never to the policy target, and this is unbiased. Their "reference solution" case is our hindsight
hint used correctly: it lets the critic judge "is this partial trajectory heading to the known
correct end state" — a VALUE statement about the learner's own move, not "does it look like the hint".
(4) Numbers: PVF improves explained variance of the critic on all four tasks (Reasoning Gym,
CodeIO with LOO siblings, Sudoku, MiniF2F); competitive with or better than mean-baseline GRPO at
K∈{4,8}; MC advantages so the PI affects only variance. Informed-AAC: matches full-state asymmetric
baselines with strictly less information on POMDP benchmarks.
(5) Mapping. The direct transplant is a FROZEN, PUBLISHED privileged critic: at fold, fit
V_phi(x, z, y | hint/patch/sibling outcomes) on D with env outcomes; at duel score the miner's (z_A,
y_A) with the hint fed only to the critic. Cheap at duel (one forward). But a learned scorer is a
judge: its maximiser is argmax V_phi, and F4 (42 %) is exactly what a frontier "critic" did here.
Unbiasedness of a gradient is not robustness of a ranking target — treat as high-Goodhart unless
the critic is only used to PRIORITISE which states get real verification. Informed-AAC's post-hoc
test (does the PI reduce return-prediction error?) is the right acceptance test for any hint field
we add to D: keep the hint only if it demonstrably predicts outcome.

PART II — SCORING THE LEARNER'S ACTION BY THE EXPERT'S COST-TO-GO
======================================================================================================

3a. Ross, Bagnell — "Reinforcement and Imitation Learning via Interactive No-Regret Learning"
    (AggreVaTe), 2014. https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.5979
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Mechanism. Iteration n: roll in with the current learner (mixed with expert, beta_n), pick a
uniformly random time t, take an EXPLORATORY action a (uniform, or a contextual-bandit choice), then
let the EXPERT finish from t+1..T and record its cost-to-go Q̂ → cost-weighted example (s,t,a,Q̂). Train
a cost-sensitive classifier / Q-regressor, pi_{n+1} = argmin_a Q_n(s,t,a). Aggregate datasets;
no-regret online learner over iterations.
(2) Signal: the EXPERT'S cost-to-go after the LEARNER'S action — one rollout per sample, arbitrary
variance accepted; the no-regret learner averages it. Alternative explicitly allowed: a learned
regressor Q_n(s,t,a) replaces rollouts ("argmax regression"); in the partial-information setting
which action to explore is a contextual bandit.
(3) Above the expert? Theorem 2.1: J(pî) ≤ J(pi*) + T[eps_class + eps_regret] + O(Q_max T log T /
(alpha N)), with eps_class = min over the class of the expected ADVANTAGE regret Q*(s,a) −
min_a Q*(s,a) on the learner's own state distribution. eps_class can be NEGATIVE-relative: if the
expert is not locally optimal, some a has Q*(s,a) < Q*(s,pi*(s)) and the learner that picks it
beats the expert (made explicit in AggreVaTeD Eq. with eps_N ≥ 0: "pî can outperform pi* when pi*
is not locally optimal"). Also: unlike DAgger the guarantee does not need the expert's action to be
realisable — only its value from each action.
(4) Numbers: analysis paper; the bound is the number. Variance: one rollout per (s,t,a), uniform
exploration is "simple and effective but inefficient".
(5) Mapping. AggreVaTe IS "force the miner's action, let the teacher finish": term = −Q̂^T(x, y_A)
(or advantage Q̂^T(x,y_A) − V̂^T(x)). What they say about our questions: (i) rollouts — one noisy
rollout per sample is enough for LEARNING because the online learner averages over iterations; for a
RANKING of two miners on 1300 turns the noise must be averaged by us → N continuations per turn or a
regressor; (ii) variance reduction — use the expert's own value as baseline (advantage form; see
AggreVaTeD §4.1), and share the roll-in (the same x for both sides is already paired in our duel);
(iii) Q-estimate instead of rollouts — allowed by the theory, but it is a learned function =
re-opens F4-style Goodhart; the paper only trusts it as the LEARNER'S internal model, not as the
grader. Avoids F1/F3/F5 (no reference action to copy or splice; the miner's own action is executed);
F2 partly (hindsight is not in the loop; T finishes blind); F6 is the cost. Replayable if the
continuation seeds and container images are pinned and outcomes stored. Cost: duel-time = N×(one
container run + T continuation, ~10–40 T turns) per scored turn — 2–3 orders above today's echo cost
→ only for a decision-state SUBSET; fold-time = same per (x, candidate a), amortised across duels only
if the miner's action can be matched to a stored candidate (Part V, M1).

3b. Sun, Venkatraman, Gordon, Boots, Bagnell — "Deeply AggreVaTeD: Differentiable Imitation Learning
    for Sequential Prediction", ICML 2017. https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01030
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Policy-gradient form of AggreVaTe: ∇ = E_{s~d_pi_n}[ Σ_a ∇pi(a|s)·Q*(s,a) ] (discrete) or the
importance-weighted / reparameterised analogue; roll-in mixes expert and learner; Q* from expert
rollouts or from a critic. §4.1 variance reduction: replace Q* by the ADVANTAGE A*(s,a)=Q*(s,a)−V*(s)
when V*=min_a Q* is available; natural-gradient (exponentiated) update.
(2) Signal: expert's Q* at the learner's states, all actions (discrete) or sampled actions.
(3) Above the expert: yes when the expert is not locally optimal (the eps_N ≥ 0 regret term);
Theorems 5.1–5.3: a depth-K binary-tree MDP where any RL algorithm needs Ω(2^K) samples while IL with
Q* access needs poly(K) — an exponential separation. Theorem 5.4–5.5: lower bound for IL too.
(4) Numbers: Walker +5.4 % over the expert, Hopper 97 % of expert, Acrobot +5.8 %, CartPole +25 %
over the expert (natural gradient); dependency parsing on handwritten algebra: AggreVaTeD > SL, SL-RL,
DAgger (Tab. 1). Rollouts: one expert rollout per queried (s,a) in the continuous tasks; the exponential-
gap result assumes an exact/noisy Q* oracle queried per state.
(5) Mapping. Two prescriptions we can lift verbatim: (a) score the ADVANTAGE, not the raw value —
Q̂^T(x,y_A) − V̂^T(x) removes the per-state difficulty term exactly as our centering removes the flat
lift on R; V̂^T(x) is the teacher's own solve rate at x (already part of a verified table); (b) the
value oracle is worth exponentially more than reward-only search — which is the argument for spending
containers on Q̂ tables at fold rather than on more frontier deliberation (F5). Same avoidance profile
as 3a.

3c. Chang, Krishnamurthy, Agarwal, Daumé, Langford — "Learning to Search Better than Your Teacher"
    (LOLS), ICML 2015. https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02206
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Roll-IN with the learned policy; at a random step try every one-step deviation; roll-OUT with a
MIXTURE (prob. beta reference, 1−beta learned) to get each deviation's cost; train a cost-sensitive
classifier. Analyses the 3×3 grid of roll-in/roll-out ∈ {reference, learned, mixture}: reference
roll-in is inconsistent; reference roll-out is right only when the reference is optimal; learned
roll-out ("SEARN-like") is locally optimal but can lose to a good reference.
(2) Signal: cost of a one-step deviation followed by the mixture — no expert ACTION labels needed at
all, only a cost function on final outputs.
(3) Above the expert: the regret bound is against the MIN of (reference policy, all one-step
deviations of the learned policy) — so with a bad reference the learner converges to a locally
optimal policy that beats it; with a good reference it tracks it. Explicit title claim.
(4) Numbers: dependency parsing with a deliberately BAD reference: LOLS (mixture roll-out) 89.4 UAS
vs SEARN-style 63.4 [as recorded]; with a good reference all methods ~ equal; cost-sensitive
one-step deviations = |A| rollouts per sampled step.
(5) Mapping. LOLS gives the cleanest statement of what our A-leg should measure: LOCAL REGRET,
Reg(x,y_A) = Q̂(x,y_A) − max_a Q̂(x,a) over a candidate set that includes the teacher's action AND
deviations (frontier proposals, king actions). Maximiser = locally optimal policy ≥ teacher wherever
a verified deviation beats it. The mixture roll-out is their answer to F5-type "reference is wrong
here": don't trust T's continuation alone if a learned continuation exists — for us the only
alternative finisher is a frontier agent, which is expensive and weaker on our tasks than assumed;
use T roll-out but let the CANDIDATE set carry the improvement (the survivor). Re-opens: nothing
beyond F6.

3d. Daumé, Langford, Marcu — "Search-based Structured Prediction" (SEARN), MLJ 2009 [mem]
    https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-009-5106-x
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Iteratively: roll in and roll out with the CURRENT (stochastic) policy, compute each action's cost
by rollout, train a cost-sensitive classifier, interpolate into the policy. (2) Signal: rollout cost
under the learner. (3) Locally optimal ≥ a bad reference; needs the learner to roll out (impractical
in IL, as AggreVaTe notes). (4) [mem] parsing/tagging results; no expert-beating headline.
(5) Mapping: "roll out with the learner" = the miner finishes its own trajectory — that is the
bench, not a per-turn duel term; useful only as an advisory, and it is what §RT-7 already measures.

3e. He, Daumé, Eisner — "Imitation Learning by Coaching", NIPS 2012.
    NeurIPS proceedings, https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012 (entry "Imitation Learning by
    Coaching"; fetched 2026-09-21)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) When the oracle's action is unreachable for the learner's feature class, a COACH demonstrates
"hope actions" pĩ_i(s) = argmax_a [ lambda_i·score_{pi_i}(s,a) − L(s,a) ]: the action that is both
low-loss AND highly scored by the current learner; lambda_i decays so the coach approaches the oracle.
DAgger with coaching.
(2) Signal: task loss L(s,a) (needs a cost for every action = a value table) + the learner's own
scores. Producer: the training loop, given L.
(3) Above the expert: no; the claim is a lower REGRET bound than imitating the oracle directly when
the oracle is not in the learner's class (the intermediate targets are learnable).
(4) Cost-sensitive dynamic feature selection: coaching beats DAgger and the oracle-imitation baseline
at matched budgets [tables, no single headline].
(5) Mapping — the most useful idea in Part II for the REFERENCE side. Our informed refs (F2) are the
"oracle": correct but unreachable/atypical for a blind policy (outside the band; plan-following). A
COACHED reference at a decision state = argmax over VERIFIED-solving candidate actions a of
[ lambda·lp_T(a|x) − (1 − Q̂^T(x,a)) ]: the solving action the blind teacher finds most plausible.
Dataset field per decision state: coached action y_coach + its lp_T. Per-turn term: today's A/R
machinery against y_coach instead of T's own sample. Avoids F2 (by construction the ref is in the
teacher's typicality range or it is not selected), F3 (splice gains nothing when the ref action is
already the teacher-like one), F5 (verification, not consensus, selects). Re-opens F6 (candidate
sparsity) and needs the check that a solving action with lp_T inside the band EXISTS at a useful
fraction of stuck states (the 27 % survivor says yes at some).

PART III — PER-STEP VALUE ESTIMATION FOR LM AGENTS (MC vs learned vs LLM-estimated)
======================================================================================================

4a. Putta et al. (MultiOn/Stanford) — "Agent Q: Advanced Reasoning and Learning for Autonomous AI
    Agents", Aug 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07199
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) MCTS over web states; node value Q(h,a) = alpha·Q̃ (LLM self-critique ranking of sibling actions)
+ (1−alpha)·Q̂ (MC backup of the env outcome); step-level DPO on sibling pairs with the mixed Q as
preference; iterate.
(2) Signal: env success (WebShop / OpenTable via GPT-4V grading), LLM critique as a prior.
(3) Above the base policy yes (search + outcome); no external expert.
(4) Numbers: WebShop LLaMA-3-70B zero-shot 28.6 % → Agent Q + MCTS 50.5 % [mem] / MCTS alone 48.4 %
(human 50.0 %); OpenTable zero-shot 18.6 % → Agent Q 81.7 % → +MCTS 95.4 %. Ablation: outcome-only
DPO underperforms the mixed Q; the AI-critique alone is weaker than MC.
(5) Mapping: confirms MC backup is the load-bearing part and the LLM critique is a variance-reducing
PRIOR — same shape as AggreVaTeD's advantage with a critic baseline. No new term; supports "spend
containers for Q̂, use frontier only to PROPOSE / prioritise".

4b. Koh, McAleer, Fried, Salakhutdinov — "Tree Search for Language Model Agents", Jul 2024.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01476
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Best-first search in the REAL environment (browser with backtracking) with an LM VALUE FUNCTION:
GPT-4o prompted to score a state's progress toward the goal 0..1 (self-consistency over 20 samples).
(2) Signal: LM-estimated value from screenshots/DOM; no outcome during search.
(3) Above the reactive agent, not above any expert.
(4) Numbers: VisualWebArena GPT-4o 19.8 → 26.4 % (+39.7 % rel.); Llama-3-70B 7.6 → 16.7 % (+119.7 %);
WebArena 15.0 → 19.2 %. Value-function quality: with an ORACLE value function (ground truth) success
would be 43.5 % vs 37.0 % with the GPT-4o value function on their subset — the LM value estimate is
the bottleneck; errors are mostly false positives (declaring success early).
(5) Mapping: an LLM value estimate that is good enough to STEER search is not good enough to GRADE
(6.5-point gap to oracle on the same trees, false-positive bias). This is F4 seen from the other side.
Use only as a proposal-prioritiser.

4c. Zhou et al. — "Language Agent Tree Search Unifies Reasoning Acting and Planning in Language
    Models" (LATS), Oct 2023 / ICML 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04406
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) MCTS with LM value V(s) = lambda·LM_score(s) + (1−lambda)·self-consistency(s); reflections stored
and prepended after failures; env feedback (tests, page) in the loop.
(2) Signal: LM score + vote + env feedback. (3) Above ReAct/ToT; no expert.
(4) HumanEval pass@1 92.7 % (GPT-4); WebShop score 75.9 / SR 38.0 vs ReAct 53.8 / 28.0.
(5) Mapping: the self-consistency half of V is the "deliberated consensus" that failed here (F5:
confident and wrong at 9/11). LATS only survives it because env feedback overrides V; without a
grader the LM value is not a target.

4d. Zhang et al. — "ReST-MCTS*: LLM Self-Training via Process Reward Guided Tree Search", Jun 2024.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03816
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Per-step value inferred from tree rollouts without human step labels: a step's target value is
the fraction of its rollouts that reach the correct final answer, weighted by "reasoning distance";
train a PRM on these; PRM guides further search; iterate policy SFT on the best traces.
(2) Signal: final-answer correctness (verifier) propagated to steps; producer = the search itself.
(3) Above the base policy; ceiling = what search + verifier can reach.
(4) GSM8K SC + ReST-MCTS*(value) 87.5 vs Math-Shepherd 87.1; MATH gains of a few points per
iteration; PRM accuracy over MS on their held-out step-label set.
(5) Mapping: the per-step target "fraction of continuations that solve" IS Q̂ — same object as
AggreVaTe's cost-to-go with a verifier as cost. Their trick for sparsity: reuse EVERY rollout that
passes through a node as a sample for that node (tree sharing), which is how N=3–4 per action becomes
N≈20 per state at the same container budget. Directly relevant to F6.

4e. Kazemnejad et al. — "VinePPO: Unlocking RL Potential for LLM Reasoning Through Refined Credit
    Assignment", Oct 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01679
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Replace PPO's learned value network by MC estimates: at each reasoning step sample K independent
completions, V_MC(s_t) = mean correctness; advantages from V_MC.
(2) Signal: verifier outcome of K completions per step (K∈{1,3,9}; default 9 [mem]).
(3) Above PPO's critic; no expert.
(4) The learned value network "barely outperforms a random baseline" at RANKING intermediate states
(pairwise accuracy near 0.5 on MATH); V_MC ranks them well and improves monotonically in K; VinePPO
reaches PPO's final accuracy in ~1/3 of the steps [mem] on MATH/GSM8K with DeepSeekMath-7B / RhoMath-1B.
(5) Mapping: the single most relevant negative result for a LEARNED per-step scorer (F4 / §2e
warning): a value net trained on outcomes ranked partial states no better than chance, while MC with
K=9 ranked them well. Rule-level lesson: a per-turn term must be MC-verified (containers), not
regressed; K≈9 per state is the empirical sweet spot they report — our N=3–4 (F6) is below it.

4f. Yuan et al. (ByteDance) — "Agent-R: Training Language Model Agents to Reflect via Iterative
    Self-Training", Jan 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11425
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) MCTS collects good and bad trajectories sharing a prefix; the ACTOR itself identifies the FIRST
erroneous step t* in its bad trajectory; the revision trajectory = bad prefix up to t* + a revision
thought + the sibling good branch from the same parent; iterative SFT with alpha rising 0.5→0.7→1.0.
(2) Signal: env reward (good ≥ alpha, bad ≤ good−beta, beta=0.2); the splice point from the model.
(3) Above expert-trajectory training: Table 2 avg 70.71 vs ETO 65.12, direct-revision 62.36, Agent-
FLAN 28.33, GPT-4o 45.46 (WebShop 63.91 / SciWorld 70.23 / TextCraft 78.00); "training on revision
trajectories surpasses expert trajectories"; loops (repeated action sequences) drop across iterations.
(4) Rollouts: 300/200/200 MCTS simulations per env; multiple rollouts per simulation for reward
estimation; three iterations.
(5) Mapping (dataset-side): a D field marking t* (first step where a verified sibling branch solves
and the taken one does not) turns king-failure trajectories into "decision states with a verified
recovery". Their splice is exactly our survivor at fold: teacher-like prefix + verified good branch.
Their finding that early revision beats end-of-trajectory revision says weight D toward t*, not
toward the tail of failed runs. Avoids F1/F3/F5; needs F6 handled by tree-sharing (4d).

(ARMAP / AgentTrek: not fetched this pass; ARMAP [mem] learns a reward model from LLM-generated
trajectories + synthetic intents and reports gains from reward-model-guided sampling — a learned
judge, same caveat as §2e/4e.)

   NeurIPS 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00323

(1) Upper level generates a natural-language thought per step, lower level generates the action
conditioned on it; loss = CE(actions) + alpha·CE(thoughts) − beta·H(actions); trained on synthetic

(2) Signal: expert THOUGHTS + actions (thoughts are privileged text, produced by the demonstrator).

(4) BabyAI BossLevel: TC 96.2 % vs BC 91.2 % success; larger gap OOD and on harder splits; "precrime"

(5) Mapping: thought imitation helps when the thought is a CAUSAL bottleneck for the action (their

6a. Gu et al. (OSU) — "Is Your LLM Secretly a World Model of the Internet? Model-Based Planning for

======================================================================================================
PART V — CROSS-CUTTING READ
======================================================================================================
• Every method that beats a fixed expert does so by scoring the LEARNER'S action with a VALUE
  (cost-to-go under a finisher + a verifier), never by matching an action: AggreVaTe/AggreVaTeD
  (+5–25 % over experts), LOLS (bad-reference case), Agent-R (revision > expert trajectories), TGRL.
• Privileged information is safe in three places only: (a) conditioning an ESTIMATOR (Le Critique,
  asymmetric AC, PI-dropout, Scaffolder [A/B sweeps]); (b) selecting WHICH states get outcome
  scoring (ADVISOR gate; TGRL trust); (c) as a PROPOSAL that is then verified (our survivor). As a
  TARGET it fails provably (ADVISOR Prop. 1) and empirically here (F2).
• LLM-estimated value is a prior, not a grader: Koh oracle-vs-GPT-4o gap; VinePPO's critic at chance
  on ranking; WebDreamer task-level 52 %; CRUXEval 75–81 %; our F4 42 %. MC with K≈9 (VinePPO) or
  tree-shared rollouts (ReST-MCTS*) is what ranks partial states.
• Coaching is the principled fix for "the correct reference is atypical": choose the solving action
  the blind learner already finds plausible.
• Sparsity (F6) has two known remedies: advantage form (AggreVaTeD) and tree sharing (ReST-MCTS*,
  Agent-R) — one action-set per STATE reused by every candidate, N per state ≈ 20 at the budget that
  gave N=3–4 per action.

======================================================================================================
CANDIDATE MECHANISMS (≤ 6; each: rule, maximiser, gaming surface, cost, cheapest kill experiment)
======================================================================================================

M1. Verified local-regret action leg (AggreVaTe/LOLS on D, fold-time table).
  Rule: at fold, for every decision state x with a grader, build a candidate set C(x) = {T's k sampled
  actions, frontier proposals, king-failure action, Agent-R-style sibling branches}; for each a ∈ C(x)
  run N teacher continuations in containers → Q̂(x,a) = solve rate, V̂(x) = mean over T's own actions.
  Publish the table with D. Duel term A' = Q̂(x, canon(y_A)) − max_{a∈C(x)} Q̂(x,a) (local regret, ≤ 0)
  where canon() is a deterministic dialect-level canonicaliser (normalised command string / patch
  hunk set); an unmatched y_A scores V̂(x) − max Q̂ (the teacher's own regret). Turn = min(R, G, A').
  Maximiser: the policy that at every tabled state emits the best VERIFIED action — strictly above T
  wherever a proposal beat T (the 27 % of stuck states).
  Gaming surface: memorise D's tables (RT-6: fresh D per epoch, tables built after the slice seed is
  unknown); canonicaliser gaming (emit a string that canonicalises to the best entry but does
  something else — closed by requiring canon() to be a parse of the executed action, not a fuzzy
  match); unmatched actions are never penalised below T's own regret, so generic exploration earns
  exactly T's value — no lift (closes the §3i "exploration wins" hole).
  Cost: fold N·|C(x)| continuations per decision state (N=8, |C|=6 → 48 container runs ≈ 10–40 T turns
  each; ~1k decision states/epoch ≈ 50k continuations); duel 0 extra (table lookup + canon).
  Kill: on the existing 11 tabled states re-run with N=8 and check the paired sign of Q̂(best
  proposal) − Q̂(T action) against the N=3–4 tables; if the ranking of candidates flips at > 30 % of
  states between N=4 and N=8 the table is still noise at any affordable N → dead.

M2. ADVISOR gate: imitation where the teacher is reliable, verified value where it is not.
  Rule: dataset field g(x) = divergence between blind T(.|x) and hindsight-informed T(.|x,p) actions
  (fold-time samples, k=5 each, over canon()). w(x) = exp(−alpha·g(x)). Turn = w·min(R,G) + (1−w)·A'
  (M1's term). Where hindsight changes nothing, score as today; where it changes the decision, ONLY
  verified value counts and the informed thought/action is never a target.
  Maximiser: T on low-gap states, best-verified-action policy on high-gap states → above T.
  Gaming surface: none new on the imitation half (unchanged rule); inherits M1's; a miner cannot move
  w (it is a stored field).
  Cost: fold +k informed samples per decision state (already produced for §3g/§3i); duel 0 extra.
  Kill: AUC of g(x) for predicting "T's blind continuation fails at x" on the ~50 decision states with
  outcomes; if AUC < 0.65 the gate does not find the unreliable states and M2 collapses to M1 → dead.

M3. Coached references (He–Daumé–Eisner hope actions) replacing hindsight refs.
  Rule: at each decision state pick y_coach = argmax_{a ∈ C(x), Q̂(x,a) ≥ Q̂_T + margin} lp_T(a|x) — the
  verified-better action the BLIND teacher finds most plausible; if none exists keep T's own refs.
  Thought refs = verified-solved T thoughts only (survivor). Run today's R/G/A machinery unchanged
  against these refs.
  Maximiser: the most teacher-like policy that actually solves — above T at every coached state, in-
  band on G by construction.
  Gaming surface: parroting T still loses at coached states (ref ≠ T's action there); splicing T's
  thought with a member action (F3) buys nothing because the ref action is already the teacher-
  plausible one; the residual is the coached ref being predictable from x (no more than today's refs).
  Cost: fold = M1's tables + one lp_T echo per candidate; duel 0 extra (same echo count as today).
  Kill: on the 27 % of stuck states with a verified-better proposal, measure lp_T(y_coach|x) against
  the teacher's own band; if the best verified action lies below the band floor at > 70 % of them
  the coach has nothing in-band to point at and M3 degenerates to F2 → dead.

M4. Frozen privileged critic as a prioritiser, not a scorer (Le Critique / informed-AAC).
  Rule: at fold train V_phi(x, z, y | patch/hint/sibling outcomes) on D with outcomes; publish weights
  and the informed-AAC acceptance test (return-prediction-error must drop vs the blind critic). Use
  V_phi ONLY to choose which states enter M1's container budget (highest predicted advantage spread)
  and to pre-rank proposals; it never enters the turn score.
  Maximiser: unchanged (M1's); V_phi only changes which states are tabled.
  Gaming surface: a miner cannot touch V_phi; the risk is a biased state selection (critic prefers
  states where the frontier happens to look good) — mitigated by keeping a uniform random share of
  the container budget.
  Cost: fold one critic fit (hours on one GPU) + one forward per candidate state; duel 0.
  Kill: held-out pairwise accuracy of V_phi at ranking sibling continuations by real outcome on king-
  failure states; if ≤ 0.6 (VinePPO's critic-at-chance regime; F4 was 0.42) it selects no better than
  random and the budget is better spent uniformly → dead.

M5. Thought value by teacher continuation (Thought Cloning's causal test, duel-time, subset only).
  Rule: on a seeded subset of S decision states per duel (S≈60, drawn from M1's tabled states), force
  (x, z_A) into the teacher, let T choose the action and finish in a container N times → Q̂(x, z_A);
  term = Q̂(x, z_A) − V̂(x). Both sides scored on the same states (paired). Replaces G on those states.
  Maximiser: thoughts that make T act better than it does alone — above T by definition; filler and
  parroting score V̂(x) − V̂(x) = 0.
  Gaming surface: leaking the answer into z (RT-10/12) — that IS a good thought here (leaking-is-
  knowing, EQUILIBRIUM.md) and is legitimate if x does not contain it; a thought that hijacks T into a
  memorised action string is caught by the container, not by us; B leakage check still applies.
  Cost: duel S·N container runs per side (60×4 = 240 per side per duel, ≈ 2–4 h of extra pod time) —
  the expensive candidate; fold 0.
  Kill: on 20 decision states, force verified-good vs verified-bad T thoughts (already labelled) into
  T with N=6; if the paired solve-rate difference is < 0.1 (T ignores the injected thought) the term
  has no signal → dead before any miner is scored.

M6. Simulator-filtered proposals to extend M1 to states without a grader (WebDreamer / CWM).
  Rule: at fold, for decision states with no container grader, frontier proposes a ∈ C(x); a
  purpose-trained simulator (CWM-class) predicts the observation o(a); proposals whose predicted o is
  an error/no-op/identical to T's are dropped; the survivors are tabled with Q̂ = frontier-simulated
  outcome ONLY as a tie-break prior, and the state enters M2's high-gap set with w derived from
  simulator disagreement. No simulated outcome ever becomes a turn score by itself.
  Maximiser: unchanged (M1/M2); coverage of tabled states grows.
  Gaming surface: none for miners; risk is systematic simulator error correlated with action style
  (short commands predicted better) → cap the simulator-only share of tabled states at 20 %.
  Cost: fold 1 frontier proposal call + 1 simulator call per candidate (cents); duel 0.
  Kill: on 40 grader-backed states, compare simulator pass/fail of proposals to the real container
  outcome; if agreement < 0.8 (WebDreamer task-level was 0.52; CWM trace-level 0.88) the filter
  admits wrong actions into the tables → dead as a coverage extender.
